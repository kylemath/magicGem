Dear Professor Ko,

Please find enclosed my manuscript titled "Balance in Inequality: Testing the Physical Equilibrium, Precession, and Structural Stability of Magic Gems" (24 pages, 7 multi-panel figures, 5 tables), which I am submitting for consideration for publication as a Research Article in the Journal of Mathematics and the Arts.

Over five centuries ago, Albrecht Durer contributed two enduring landmarks at the intersection of mathematics and the visual arts: the order-four magic square in his engraving Melencolia I (1514) and the first printed polyhedral net unfoldings in Underweysung der Messung (1525). A magic square is a combinatorial paradigm of "balance in inequality": its n^2 entries {1, ..., n^2} are strictly distinct, yet every row, column, and main diagonal sums to the same constant. Lifting the entries into R^3 as radial vectors p_{ij}(lambda) = (x_j, y_i, lambda z_{ij}) and taking their convex hull—the "Magic Gem" G_lambda(S)—raises one central question: when does combinatorial line-sum balance suffice for physical equilibrium in three dimensions, and when a nonlinear physical realization breaks that balance, how can an opposing inequality restore it?

Motivated by fabricating physical 3D-printed models, radial balance wheels, ball-and-rod wireframes, interconnected strut sculptures, and folded cardstock cutouts across all 880 order-four magic squares (openly shared with an interactive 3D visual essay at https://kylemath.github.io/magicGem/ and https://kylemath.github.io/magicGemWeb/3dModels/index.html), this paper tests this principle through four progressive mechanical regimes. In the radial spoke regime (Section 2), natural spoke vectors balance identically at the origin O, whereas trimming the spokes to equal unit length breaks equilibrium on all 832 non-associative squares in 3D and on every square in 2D, where the irrational ring-radius ratio r_2 / r_1 = sqrt(5) bounds the resultant below by the Pell convergent 9/4 approx. sqrt(5) (0.07049 on eight Group VI squares led by #70). By length-weight duality, balance is restored either by assigning positive unequal tip weights w* > 0 on the unit sphere S^2 (for all 880 squares) or by power-law spoke scaling r^{alpha*} where aligned inner and outer ring dipoles cancel the middle ring.

In the rotational regime (Section 3), equal point masses at the unequal vertices p_{ij}(lambda) neutralize all off-diagonal inertia moments, turning every semi-magic square into a smooth axisymmetric top that passes through an exact spherical top at lambda_{iso} = (n^2 + 1)^{-1/2} (= 1/sqrt(17) at n = 4). Conversely, weighting the vertices by their unequal magic entries m_{ij} = s_{ij} exposes the quadrant quadrupole Q_{xy}, splitting the horizontal principal axes by 2|Q_{xy}| and triggering 180-degree Dzhanibekov flips on 824 of the 880 squares.

In the polyhedral hull regime (Section 4), wrapping the point cloud in its uniform solid convex hull G_0(S) shifts the center of mass off O on 829 of the 880 squares by omitting the interior points P_S^{(1)} and weighting hull facets by unequal simplex volumes. Because the outer hull vertices and inner core points pull in strictly opposite directions across O (V_0 p_{outer} + V_1 p_{inner} = 0), re-engaging the inner core G_1(S) = conv P_S^{(1)} restores balance via two-density core inclusions (33 denser cores led by #673 and 2 lighter/hollow-cavity cores #123 and #54), single-material two-gauge nested wireframes (158 Group VI gems), or the complete 120-rod strut network K_{16} (which shares the exact axisymmetric inertia ratio of the point cloud), while all 880 boundary hulls unfold into non-overlapping papercraft nets with N_{tabs} = f_0 - 1 glue tabs. Finally, in the freestanding gravity regime (Section 5), compressing the vertical lift to the cubic print scale lambda = 1/5 stabilizes 74.5% of all 14,824 gem facets on a flat table, and alternating any gem G_lambda(S) with its inverted mirror twin G_lambda(r^2 \bar{S}) = -G_lambda(S) cancels opposing facet tilts to lock chiral knight's-move blocks (#763) into a straight vertical tower. Across all four regimes, the order-four censuses are unified by a three-tier symmetry hierarchy (Section 6) linking universal line-sum cancellation across all 880 squares, one-parameter axial counterbalancing in Dudeney's Group VI (304 self-complementary C_2 squares), and pointwise antipodal cancellation in Dudeney's Group III (48 associative squares led by Durer's #175).

For complete transparency, two companion preprints by the author examine separate pure-combinatorial aspects of lifted magic squares: one (submitted to Experimental Mathematics, arXiv:2512.09170) catalogs the static polyhedral combinatorics, and a second (submitted to Discrete Applied Mathematics) studies abstract Prouhet-Tarry-Escott polynomial matrix moments. The present manuscript is self-contained and is the only paper in the series investigating vector balance laws, 3D rotational dynamics, nested multi-material and wireframe/papercraft fabrication, and mirror-twin sculptural stacking.

This manuscript is original, is my sole work, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. Thank you very much for your time and consideration.
