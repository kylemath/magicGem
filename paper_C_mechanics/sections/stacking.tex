\section{Freestanding Gravity and Mirror-Twin Towers}
\label{sec:stacking}

Our fourth and final test removes the central pivot and places Magic Gems directly onto a flat horizontal table under gravity—first as individual resting blocks, and then stacked face-to-face into multi-block vertical columns. Because the hull vertices sit at unequal heights, the boundary facets of $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ are slanted polygons whose outward normals rarely oppose each other across the gem. We therefore test when a slanted facet $F\subset\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ supports the gem's centre of mass without tipping, and how pairing a gem with an inverted partner restores a straight vertical column when identical copies curve off-axis.

\subsection{Single-Block Table Equilibrium: Cubic Aspect Scaling Stabilizes \texorpdfstring{$74.5\%$}{74.5\%} of Facets}

Let $F\subset\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ be a facet with outward unit normal $\hat{\mathbf n}_F(\lambda)$. When $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ rests with $F$ on a horizontal table (so $\hat{\mathbf n}_F(\lambda)$ points straight down), gravity acts vertically through the centre of mass $\mathbf c(\lambda)$. Let $\pi_F(\mathbf c(\lambda))$ denote the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf c(\lambda)$ onto the affine plane of $F$, and let $h_F(\lambda)=(\mathbf p_F-\mathbf c(\lambda))\cdot(-\hat{\mathbf n}_F(\lambda))>0$ be the height of the centre of mass above the table.

\begin{definition}[Static stability margin and critical tipping angle]
\label{def:stability}
For a facet $F\subset\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$, the \emph{stability margin} $d_{\mathrm{stab}}(S,F;\lambda)$ is the signed Euclidean distance in the plane of $F$ from $\pi_F(\mathbf c(\lambda))$ to the boundary $\partial F$ of the facet polygon (positive when $\pi_F(\mathbf c(\lambda))$ lies in the strict relative interior of $F$, and negative outside). Facet $F$ is \emph{statically stable} if and only if $d_{\mathrm{stab}}(S,F;\lambda)>0$, with \emph{critical tipping angle}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tip-angle}
\theta_{\mathrm{tip}}(S,F;\lambda) = \arctan\!\left(\frac{d_{\mathrm{stab}}(S,F;\lambda)}{h_F(\lambda)}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Order-four resting stability census]
\label{prop:stability-census}
Across all $880$ magic squares of order~$4$, the gems $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ possess a total of $14{,}824$ facets (mean $16.85$ facets per gem). At the tall canonical lift $\lambda=1$ (where the vertical height span $15$ is five times the $3\times3$ floor span), the high centre of mass $\mathbf c_0(S)$ projects outside $57.0\%$ of all facet polygons: $6{,}378$ of the $14{,}824$ facets ($43.0\%$) are statically stable, with every gem possessing between $4$ and $12$ stable resting facets (mean $7.25$; using the balanced point centroid $O$ gives $6{,}340$ stable facets, $42.8\%$). Compressing the vertical lift to the cubic 3D-print scale $\lambda_{\mathrm{cube}}=1/5=0.20$ brings the centre of mass closer to the slanted upper and lower facets so that $11{,}044$ of the $14{,}824$ facets ($74.5\%$) become statically stable, with a mean critical tipping angle of $17.8^\circ$ and a maximum of $47.3^\circ$ (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(d)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each facet $F\subset\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ with exact integer plane equation $aX+bY+cZ+d=0$ in doubled coordinates, rescaling $z\mapsto\lambda z$ transforms the outward normal to $\mathbf n_F(\lambda)\propto(a\lambda,b\lambda,c)$ and the solid centroid to $\mathbf c_0(\lambda)=(c_{0,x},c_{0,y},\lambda c_{0,z})$. Projecting $\mathbf c_0(\lambda)$ onto the plane of $F$ and evaluating $d_{\mathrm{stab}}(S,F;\lambda)$ and \eqref{eq:tip-angle} across all $14{,}824$ facets yields the stated counts and angles.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why Same-Gem Stacks Wedge Apart, and How Mirror-Twin Inversion Restores Verticality}

Suppose we now stack multiple blocks vertically along an upper facet $F_+\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (outward normal $+\mathbf n_+$, $n_{+,z}>0$) and a lower facet $F_-\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (outward normal $+\mathbf n_-$, $n_{-,z}<0$). Stacking translated copies of the \emph{same} gem $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ without rotation requires parallel opposite normals $\mathbf n_-=-\mathbf n_+$, which fails on $756$ of the $880$ gems ($85.9\%$); when $\mathbf n_-\ne-\mathbf n_+$, identical copies meet at an open wedge angle that bends the column away from the vertical (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(c), left).

Equilibrium is restored by pairing a gem with its \emph{rotational complement (mirror twin)}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mirror-twin}
S^* := r^2\overline S = (N+1)\mathbf 1 - r^2 S,
\end{equation}
which inverts the polyhedron through the origin in $\R^3$ and pairs every slanted upper facet with an equal and opposite lower facet (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(a,c)).

\begin{theorem}[Mirror-Twin Inversion and Zero-Rotation Alternating Towers]
\label{thm:mirror-tower}
Let $S$ be any arrangement of order $n$, let $S^*=r^2\overline S$ be its half-turned complement, and let $\lambda>0$. In $\R^3$, the lifted point clouds and convex hulls satisfy the central inversion identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:central-inv}
P_{S^*}(\lambda) = -P_S(\lambda),\qquad
\mathcal G_\lambda(S^*) = -\mathcal G_\lambda(S),
\end{equation}
so the map $F\mapsto F^*:=-F$ is an area-preserving, normal-reversing bijection between the upper facets $F_+\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (outward normal $+\mathbf n_+$) and the lower facets $F_+^*\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S^*)$ (outward normal $-\mathbf n_+$), and between the lower facets $F_-\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (outward normal $+\mathbf n_-$) and the upper facets $F_-^*=-F_-\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S^*)$ (outward normal $-\mathbf n_-$).

Consequently, given any upper facet $F_+\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ and any lower facet $F_-\subset\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (not necessarily parallel) that are centrally symmetric polygons in $\R^3$ with centroids $\mathbf c(F_+)$ and $\mathbf c(F_-)$, the alternating sequence of unrotated blocks $B_0,B_1,B_2,\dots$ defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tower-blocks}
B_{2m} = \mathcal G_\lambda(S) + m\mathbf T,\qquad
B_{2m+1} = -\mathcal G_\lambda(S) + 2\mathbf c(F_+) + m\mathbf T,\qquad
\mathbf T = 2\bigl(\mathbf c(F_+) - \mathbf c(F_-)\bigr),
\end{equation}
meets with $100\%$ polygon and vertex overlap at every odd joint $B_{2m}\cap B_{2m+1}$ (where the upper facet $F_+ + m\mathbf T$ of $B_{2m}$ coincides identically with the lower facet $-F_+ + 2\mathbf c(F_+) + m\mathbf T$ of $B_{2m+1}$ with opposite outward normals $\pm\mathbf n_+$) and at every even joint $B_{2m-1}\cap B_{2m}$ (where the upper facet $-F_- + 2\mathbf c(F_+) + (m-1)\mathbf T$ of $B_{2m-1}$ coincides identically with the lower facet $F_- + m\mathbf T$ of $B_{2m}$ with opposite outward normals $\mp\mathbf n_-$). Every two-block repeating unit $B_{2m}\cup B_{2m+1}$ is centrally symmetric about $\mathbf c(F_+)+m\mathbf T$, and whenever $\mathbf c(F_+)=(0,0,z_+)$ and $\mathbf c(F_-)=(0,0,z_-)$ both lie on the vertical $z$-axis ($z_+>0>z_-$), the period vector $\mathbf T=(0,0,2(z_+-z_-))$ is purely vertical and places the centre of mass of any $2M$-block tower on the $z$-axis regardless of whether $\mathbf c_0(S)=\mathbf 0$ (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(a,c)).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In centred coordinates \eqref{eq:coords}, $r^2$ sends $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$ and complementation sends $z\mapsto-z$, so $r^2\overline S$ lifts to $-\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda)$, proving \eqref{eq:central-inv}. Central symmetry of $F_\pm$ about $\mathbf c(F_\pm)$ gives $-F_\pm + 2\mathbf c(F_\pm)=F_\pm$; translating by $m\mathbf T$ establishes identical facet coincidence at all odd and even joints, while central inversion about $\mathbf c(F_+)+m\mathbf T$ exchanges $B_{2m}$ and $B_{2m+1}$ and locks the two-block centroid onto the $z$-axis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Chiral Knight's-Move Towers: Exemplar Square \#763}

\begin{proposition}[Vertically centered tower census and the knight's-move square \#763]
\label{prop:knights-move}
Across the $880$ magic squares of order~$4$, exactly $48$ squares possess both an upper facet $F_+$ and a lower facet $F_-$ that are centrally symmetric polygons whose centroids lie on the vertical axis $(0,0,z_\pm)$. The maximum projected horizontal contact area among these $48$ squares is $A_{xy}=5$ (spanning $5/9\approx55.6\%$ of the $3\times3$ grid footprint), achieved by $17$ squares (\Cref{tab:knights-move}; e.g.\ squares \#603, \#763, and \#774) in which $\pi_{xy}(F_+)$ is the centred $2\times1$ knight's-move square with vertices $(\pm\frac32,\mp\frac12)$ and $(\pm\frac12,\pm\tfrac32)$ (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(b)). Only two of the $17$ (\#774 and \#792) have parallel top and bottom facets ($\mathbf n_-=-\mathbf n_+$); the remaining $15$ require mirror-twin alternation.

In particular, square \#763 (Dudeney Group~VI, $\overline S=hS$, so $S^*=r^2\overline S=vS$ is its vertical reflection) is obtained from naturally balanced solid \#756 ($\mathbf c_0=\mathbf 0$, \Cref{tab:three-exceptions}) by swapping the single complementary pair $(4,13)\leftrightarrow(16,1)$ in rows~$2$--$3$ (\Cref{fig:hero-squares}(f)):
\[
S_{\#763} = \begin{pmatrix}
5 & 2 & \mathbf{15} & 12\\
\mathbf{16} & 11 & 6 & 1\\
4 & 7 & 10 & \mathbf{13}\\
9 & \mathbf{14} & 3 & 8
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S^*_{\#763} = vS_{\#763} = \begin{pmatrix}
9 & 14 & 3 & 8\\
4 & 7 & 10 & 13\\
16 & 11 & 6 & 1\\
5 & 2 & 15 & 12
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This swap lifts $\{13,14,15,16\}$ onto the Pythagorean upper knight's-move plane $4x-3y+5z=30$ (centroid $(0,0,6\lambda)$) and $\{1,2,3,4\}$ onto the lower plane $-4x-3y-5z=30$ (centroid $(0,0,-6\lambda)$), while shifting the solid centroid slightly to $\mathbf c_0=(0,-5/126,0)$ (which cancels pairwise in $B_{2m}\cup B_{2m+1}$). Both facets have 3D area $5\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}$, stability margin $d_{\mathrm{stab}}=0.969$, and single-block tipping angle $\theta_{\mathrm{tip}}^{(1)}=39.35^\circ$ at $\lambda=0.20$. Because $\mathbf n_\pm(\lambda)\propto(\pm4\lambda,-3\lambda,\pm5)$ makes angle $\theta_{\mathrm{joint}}(\lambda)=\arctan(\lambda)$ with $\hat{\mathbf e}_z$ ($\approx11.31^\circ$ at $\lambda=0.20$), any material with static friction $\mu_s>\lambda=0.20$ holds the unbonded column at level ($\theta_{\mathrm{table}}=0$), while tilting an $M$-block column of centre-of-mass height $H_M$ is governed by $\min\bigl(\arctan(\mu_s)-\theta_{\mathrm{joint}}(\lambda),\,\arctan(d_{\mathrm{stab}}/H_M)\bigr)$ (\Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(c)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Testing each facet $F\subset\partial\mathcal G_1(S)$ across the $880$ gems for central symmetry ($F=2\mathbf c(F)-F$ with $c_x(F)=c_y(F)=0$) identifies the $48$ gems and the $17$ gems of projected shoelace area $A_{xy}=5$. Substituting the lifted coordinates of $\{13,14,15,16\}$ and $\{1,2,3,4\}$ on \#763 into $4x-3y+5(z/\lambda)=30$ and $-4x-3y-5(z/\lambda)=30$ verifies the facet planes, normals $\mathbf n_\pm(\lambda)\propto(\pm4\lambda,-3\lambda,\pm5)$, surface area $5\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}$, and joint tilt $\tan\theta_{\mathrm{joint}}(\lambda)=\lambda$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three-dimensional chirality and why a single mold cannot build the \#763 tower]
\label{rem:chirality}
Because square \#763 satisfies $\overline S=hS$ ($z(-x,y)=-z(x,y)$), its 3D gem $\mathcal G_\lambda(S_{\#763})$ is invariant under the $180^\circ$ proper rotation around the $y$-axis, $R_{y,\pi}(x,y,z)=(-x,y,-z)\in SO(3)$, which exchanges $F_+\colon 4x-3y+5z=30$ and $F_-\colon -4x-3y-5z=30$ and returns the exact same block in its original orientation. Flipping a second copy of $\mathcal G_\lambda(S_{\#763})$ upside-down around the $x$-axis via $R_{x,\pi}(x,y,z)=(x,-y,-z)$ maps $F_+$ to a lower facet with outward normal $(+4\lambda,+3\lambda,-5)\ne-\mathbf n_+=(-4\lambda,+3\lambda,-5)$, failing to mate flush with $F_+$. More fundamentally, $\operatorname{Aut}_{O(3)}\bigl(\mathcal G_\lambda(S_{\#763})\bigr)=\{I_3,R_{y,\pi}\}\cong C_2\subset SO(3)$ contains no improper reflections: $\mathcal G_\lambda(S_{\#763})$ and $\mathcal G_\lambda(S^*_{\#763})=-\mathcal G_\lambda(S_{\#763})$ are \emph{chiral 3D enantiomorphs} in disjoint proper rigid-motion orbits, so building the tower of \Cref{fig:mirror-stacking}(a) requires two opposite-handed molds.
\end{remark}
