\section{Synthesis and Open Questions}
\label{sec:discussion}

Stepping back from the individual theorems, the mechanical tests of Sections~\ref{sec:vectors}--\ref{sec:stacking} tell a unified story about how combinatorial \emph{balance in inequality} behaves when lifted from a flat grid of numbers into three-dimensional physical space (\Cref{tab:synthesis}). Across all four physical regimes—radial spokes, spinning rotors, convex polyhedra, and stacked columns—the $880$ order-four magic squares stratify into three distinct mechanical tiers determined by how their symmetries interact with nonlinearity.

At the broadest level, whenever a physical observable is linear or symmetric-quadratic in the coordinates $(x_j,y_i,z_{ij})$, the row and column sum constraints neutralize entry inequality universally across all $880$ squares ($100\%$). This universal tier encompasses natural 3D and 2D spoke balance ($\beta=1,\alpha=1$), cubic vertical spoke vanishing ($(\mathbf V_3)_z=0$), existence of positive unit-sphere counterweights ($\mathbf w^*>\mathbf 0$), axisymmetric precession and the $\lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}=1/\sqrt{17}$ spherical-top transition for equal point masses and equal-mass $K_{16}$ strut networks, outer--inner vertex opposition ($V_0\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}}=-V_1\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{inner}}$), non-overlapping papercraft nets with $N_{\mathrm{tabs}}=f_0-1$ glue tabs, and mirror-twin facet inversion ($\mathcal G_\lambda(r^2\overline S)=-\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$).

When a nonlinear geometric operation breaks this universal balance, it typically creates a three-component residual imbalance vector $\mathbf c=(c_x,c_y,c_z)\in\R^3$ that a single scalar parameter cannot zero out. In Dudeney's Group~VI ($304$ squares satisfying $\overline S=hS$ or $vS$), however, the $180^\circ$ proper half-turn symmetry ($C_2\subset SO(3)$) forces two coordinates of every residual vector to vanish identically, locking opposing components onto a single coordinate axis. This axial alignment explains why one-parameter restoration occurs throughout Group~VI: the eight planar Pell-minimum squares (\#70, $v/u=9/4\approx\sqrt{5}$), the integer power-law spoke roots ($\alpha^*=-1$ on \#45 and $\alpha^*=+3$ on \#286), all $107$ 3D power-law spoke roots $\beta^*\ne1$, the three non-associative balanced solids (\#86, \#739, \#756), all $35$ two-density ``diamond-in-a-diamond'' solids ($33$ denser cores led by \#673 plus the two lighter/hollow-cavity cores \#123 and \#54), all $158$ balanced nested wireframes ($d_1/d_0$), all $80$ balanced nested shells ($\sigma_1/\sigma_0$), and the chiral knight's-move mirror-twin towers (\#763).

Finally, when a nonlinear observable requires unweighted equilibrium without an opposing parameter—such as equal-weight unit-sphere spokes ($\mathbf V_0=\mathbf 0$), full 3D cubic spoke vanishing ($\mathbf V_3=\mathbf 0$), 3D entry-weighted rotational axisymmetry ($Q_{xy}=0$ and $\mathbf V_3=\mathbf 0$), or uniform-diameter wireframes and hollow shells—balance holds if and only if central inversion $\mathbf p_{-c}(\lambda)=-\mathbf p_c(\lambda)$ cancels opposite cells pairwise, singling out the $48$ associative squares of Dudeney's Group~III ($r^2S=\overline S$) led by D\"urer's \#175.

These findings suggest several natural directions for further investigation. In the planar spoke regime, the twenty-four non-central cells of $A_5$ at order $n=5$ lie on five concentric rings of radii $r\in\{1,\sqrt{2},2,\sqrt{5},2\sqrt{2}\}$, coupling the independent square-free radicals $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{5}$; it remains open whether the unit-spoke resultant $\mathbf B_{\mathrm{unit}}(S)=\sum z_{ij}\mathbf u_{ij}$ can ever vanish at order $n\ge5$, or how rapidly $\min_{S\in\mathcal M_n}\|\mathbf B_{\mathrm{unit}}(S)\|_2$ decays as $n\to\infty$ via simultaneous Diophantine approximation. In the rotational and solid regimes, while point-weighted Magic Gems become exact spherical tops ($I_{xx}=I_{yy}=I_{zz}$) at $\lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}=(n^2+1)^{-1/2}$, the uniform polyhedral solid $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ retains a small transverse splitting $(I_3-I_2)/I_2\in[0.00004,0.0243]$ at $n=4$, raising the question of whether a magic square of some order $n\ge5$ (or a two-density composite $(\mathcal G_0,\mathcal G_1)$ at $n=4$) has a continuous solid inertia tensor that is an exact scalar multiple of the identity $I_3$. In the convex-peeling regime, the outer hull $\mathcal G_0(S_n)$ of the Siamese magic square of odd order $n\ge5$ strips off only twelve vertices \cite[Thm.~6.4]{mathewson2026gems}, leaving $n^2-12$ points on five sheared planar polygons whose asymptotic onion-peeling depth $d_{\mathrm{onion}}(S_n)$ and inner layer $f$-vectors remain to be determined. Finally, beyond vertical mirror-twin columns (\Cref{thm:mirror-tower}), it is natural to ask whether a Magic Gem $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ and its $D_4\times\langle\tau\rangle$ images can tile a horizontal slab or interlock in three-dimensional space with full facet-to-facet contact.
