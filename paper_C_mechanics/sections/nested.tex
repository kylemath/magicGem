\section{Convex Hulls and ``Diamond-in-a-Diamond'' Counterweighting}
\label{sec:nested}

Why does passing from the discrete point cloud $P_S$ ($\sum_{i,j}\mathbf p_{ij}=\mathbf 0$) to its uniform-density solid convex hull $\mathcal G_0(S):=\mathcal G(S)=\conv P_S$ shift the solid centre of mass
\[
\mathbf c_0(S) := \mathbf c_{\mathrm{solid}}(\mathcal G_0(S)) = \frac{1}{\vol\mathcal G_0(S)}\iiint_{\mathcal G_0(S)}\mathbf x\,dV
\]
away from the origin $O$ on $829$ of the $880$ order-four magic squares? Taking the three-dimensional convex hull introduces two geometric inequalities: first, the boundary $\partial\mathcal G_0(S)$ depends solely on the $V_0=|\vertset\mathcal G_0(S)|$ extreme vertices and omits the $V_1=N-V_0$ interior and non-vertex points $P_S^{(1)}=P_S\setminus\vertset\mathcal G_0(S)$; second, even when $V_0=N=16$ ($18$ of the $880$ squares), decomposing $\mathcal G_0(S)$ into tetrahedra from $O$ weights each simplex $\Delta_k$ by its volume $\vol(\Delta_k)$ rather than weighting the sixteen vertices equally.

Since the outer hull vertices and the discarded inner points together sum to zero, their centroids must pull in strictly opposite directions across $O$. We now test whether physically re-engaging the inner core $\mathcal G_1(S)=\conv P_S^{(1)}$ restores exact balance at $O$ across cast solids, 3D-printed wireframes, complete strut networks, and folded papercraft shells.

\subsection{The 51 Naturally Balanced Solids and Outer--Inner Vertex Opposition}

We first classify the $51$ order-four magic squares whose uniform solid hull $\mathcal G_0(S)$ balances at $O$ without any counterweight, and then prove the outer--inner opposition law for the remaining $829$ squares.

\begin{proposition}[Classification of the 51 naturally balanced order-four gems]
\label{prop:51-balanced}
Across the $880$ magic squares of order~$4$, the uniform solid $\mathcal G_0(S)$ has centre of mass $\mathbf c_0(S)=\mathbf 0$ for exactly $51$ squares, consisting of the $48$ associative squares (Dudeney's Group~III, where $z(-x,-y)=-z(x,y)$ implies $\mathcal G_0(S)=-\mathcal G_0(S)$ and forces $\mathbf c_0(S)=-\mathbf c_0(S)=\mathbf 0$ by central inversion) together with three non-associative squares in Dudeney's Group~VI: squares \#86, \#739, and \#756 (\Cref{tab:three-exceptions}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every associative square $S$, $P_S=-P_S$ implies $\mathcal G_0(S)=-\mathcal G_0(S)$ and $\mathbf c_0(S)=\mathbf 0$. Each of the three Group~VI squares \#86, \#739, and \#756 satisfies $\overline S = hS$ or $\overline S = vS$, so $\mathcal G_0(S)$ is invariant under a $180^\circ$ half-turn $\iota_h\colon(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-y,-z)$ or $\iota_v\colon(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,y,-z)$ in $\R^3$ \cite[Prop.~4.3]{mathewson2026gems}. The half-turn forces two coordinates of $\mathbf c_0(S)$ to vanish identically, and exact simplicial integration (\Cref{sec:appendix}) certifies that the single remaining rational axial moment vanishes exclusively on \#86, \#739, and \#756 among all $832$ non-associative squares.
\end{proof}

To counter the centroid drift on the remaining $829$ squares, we peel $P_S$ into nested convex layers \cite{chazelle1985convex}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:peeling}
P_S^{(0)} = P_S,\qquad
\mathcal G_k(S) = \conv P_S^{(k)},\qquad
P_S^{(k+1)} = P_S^{(k)} \setminus \vertset\mathcal G_k(S)\quad (k\ge0),
\end{equation}
calling $\mathcal G_0(S)$ the \emph{outer gem} and $\mathcal G_1(S)=\conv P_S^{(1)}\subset\mathcal G_0(S)$ the \emph{inner core gem}—``a diamond inside a diamond'' (\Cref{fig:nested-diamonds}(a,b)).

\begin{proposition}[Vertex-centroid opposition and order-four onion hierarchy]
\label{prop:opposition}
Let $S$ be any arrangement of order $n$ with $V_0=|\vertset\mathcal G_0(S)|<N$ and $V_1=N-V_0$. The centroid $\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}}=\frac{1}{V_0}\sum_{\mathbf p\in\vertset\mathcal G_0}\mathbf p$ of the outer hull vertices and the centroid $\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{inner}}=\frac{1}{V_1}\sum_{\mathbf p\in P_S^{(1)}}\mathbf p$ of the inner core points satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:opposition}
V_0\,\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}} + V_1\,\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{inner}} = \mathbf 0,
\end{equation}
pointing in strictly opposite directions across $O$ whenever $\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}}\ne\mathbf 0$. Across the $880$ magic squares of order~$4$ (\Cref{fig:nested-diamonds}(c)), $18$ squares have $V_0=16$ ($P_S^{(1)}=\emptyset$), $842$ squares have onion depth~$2$ ($14$ singletons, $162$ segments, $175$ planar polygons, and $491$ 3D polyhedra $\mathcal G_1(S)$), and $20$ squares have onion depth~$3$ ($\mathcal G_0\supset\mathcal G_1\supset\mathcal G_2$, comprising $16$ with dimension sequence $(3,3,0)$ and $4$ with $(3,3,1)$), so in total $511$ order-four magic squares possess a three-dimensional inner core gem $\mathcal G_1(S)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:opposition} follows immediately from $\sum_{\mathbf p\in P_S}\mathbf p=\mathbf 0$ and $P_S=\vertset\mathcal G_0(S)\sqcup P_S^{(1)}$, and exact convex-hull peeling across all $880$ squares yields the stated layer census.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Restoring Solid Balance via ``Diamond-in-a-Diamond'' Core Inclusions}

Guided by the opposition identity \eqref{eq:opposition}, our first restoration method plays the inner core $\mathcal G_1(S)$ directly against the outer solid hull $\mathcal G_0(S)$. For a two-density composite solid to balance via a single scalar density increment $\Delta\rho_1$, the 3D vectors $\mathbf c_0(S)$ and $\mathbf c_1(S)$ must be collinear—which occurs in Dudeney's Group~VI, where $C_2$ half-turn symmetry locks both centroids onto the same coordinate axis.

\begin{theorem}[Composite ``diamond-in-a-diamond'' solid balancing]
\label{thm:diamond-balance}
Let $S$ be an order-four magic square with $\dim\mathcal G_1(S)=3$ ($511$ squares), and fabricate $\mathcal G_0(S)$ with baseline density $\rho_0>0$ and inner core inclusion $\mathcal G_1(S)\subset\mathcal G_0(S)$ of excess density $\Delta\rho_1=\rho_1-\rho_0$. The composite centre of mass
\[
\mathbf c_{\mathrm{comp}} = \frac{\rho_0\vol(\mathcal G_0)\,\mathbf c_0 + \Delta\rho_1\vol(\mathcal G_1)\,\mathbf c_1}{\rho_0\vol(\mathcal G_0) + \Delta\rho_1\vol(\mathcal G_1)}
\]
vanishes for some $\Delta\rho_1\ne0$ if and only if $\mathbf c_0$ and $\mathbf c_1$ are non-zero and collinear ($\mathbf c_0\times\mathbf c_1=\mathbf 0$), with unique balancing density increment
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-ratio}
\frac{\Delta\rho_1}{\rho_0} = -\frac{\vol(\mathcal G_0)\,(\mathbf c_0\cdot\mathbf c_1)}{\vol(\mathcal G_1)\,\|\mathbf c_1\|_2^2}.
\end{equation}
Across the $511$ order-four gems with $\dim\mathcal G_1(S)=3$, $207$ gems have $\mathbf c_1=\mathbf 0$ ($13$ associative gems plus $194$ non-associative gems whose four inner points form a zero-sum tetrahedron) and $254$ gems outside Group~VI have non-collinear solid centroids $\mathbf c_0\times\mathbf c_1\ne\mathbf 0$. Among the remaining $50$ gems (all in Dudeney's Group~VI), $33$ have strictly anti-parallel solid centroids ($\mathbf c_0\cdot\mathbf c_1<0$), balanced by a denser inner core with rational excess density $\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0\in[5/4,\,191/6]$ (\Cref{tab:two-density} and \Cref{tab:all33}), led by Gem \#673 at $\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0=5/4$ (\Cref{fig:hero-squares}(e), \Cref{fig:nested-diamonds}(a)); the other $17$ have parallel solid centroids pointing in the same direction ($\mathbf c_0\cdot\mathbf c_1>0$), because volumetric simplex weighting pulls $\mathbf c_0$ onto the same side of $O$ as $\mathbf c_1$ despite the opposite vertex centroids of \eqref{eq:opposition}, and two of these $17$ gems balance with non-negative core density $\rho_1\in[0,\rho_0)$: square \#123 ($\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0=-3/17$, $\rho_1/\rho_0=14/17$, a lighter core) and square \#54 ($\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0=-57/58$, $\rho_1/\rho_0=1/58$, a nearly hollow core cavity, \Cref{fig:nested-diamonds}(b)), which differs from denser-core square \#45 ($\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0=+85/61$) by the single border swap $(8,9)\leftrightarrow(15,2)$ (\Cref{fig:hero-squares}(c)).

More generally, placing non-negative point masses $m_q\ge0$ at the inner points $q\in P_S^{(1)}$ balances the uniform solid $\mathcal G_0(S)$ at $O$ if and only if $-\mathbf c_0(S)\in\cone(P_S^{(1)})$, which holds for $477$ of the $829$ unbalanced gems ($528/880=60.0\%$ including the $51$ naturally balanced solids), while partitioning $\mathcal G_0(S)$ into $N$ cells of equal mass $M/N$ with centroids at $\mathbf p_{ij}$ (or embedding $N$ identical dense beads at $\mathbf p_{ij}$ in a lightweight shell) yields $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{comp}}=\mathbf 0$ and restores the axisymmetric inertia tensor of \Cref{thm:rotor} for every semi-magic square of any order $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Setting $\rho_0\vol(\mathcal G_0)\mathbf c_0+\Delta\rho_1\vol(\mathcal G_1)\mathbf c_1=\mathbf 0$ and evaluating the exact rational simplicial moments (\Cref{sec:appendix}) across all $511$ gems with $\dim\mathcal G_1(S)=3$ yields \eqref{eq:density-ratio} and the four-class partition. Existence of non-negative inner point masses $m_q\ge0$ is equivalent to $-\mathbf c_0\in\cone(P_S^{(1)})$ and certified by linear programming over the $829$ unbalanced gems, while the bead-inlaid identity follows directly from $\sum_{i,j}\mathbf p_{ij}=\mathbf 0$ and \Cref{thm:rotor}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Overcoming the Density Barrier: Complete \texorpdfstring{$K_N$}{K\_N} Networks, Nested Wireframes, and Papercraft Shells}
\label{subsec:wireframes-nets}

While the eight gems in \Cref{tab:two-density} have modest excess density ratios $\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0\in[5/4,40/19]$ that print directly on a dual-extruder 3D printer (such as standard PLA around a tungsten- or bronze-filled PLA core), the remaining $25$ denser-core gems in \Cref{tab:all33} have small core volumes $\vol(\mathcal G_1)$ requiring $\Delta\rho_1/\rho_0\in[47/12,191/6]$, and $194$ gems have a four-vertex tetrahedral core whose solid centroid $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{solid}}(\mathcal G_1)=\frac14\sum\mathbf p_q=\mathbf 0$ provides no lever arm at all.

Replacing solid volume with the skeletal and thin-shell architectures of \texttt{magicGemWeb} \cite{mathewson2025magicgemweb} (\Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}) overcomes both barriers. Connecting all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairs of points as a complete strut network $K_N\bigl(P_S(\lambda)\bigr)$ (\texttt{magicGem\_v2.stl}, \Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(b)) restores equal participation of all $N$ vertices; building a nested ball-and-rod wireframe $G(\mathcal G_0)\cup G(\mathcal G_1)$ (\texttt{magicGem\_v1.stl}, \Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(a)) replaces volumetric scaling with quadratic rod-diameter scaling ($\rho_{\mathrm{lin}}\propto d^2$) and gives four-vertex tetrahedral cores a non-zero edge-length centroid $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_1)\ne\mathbf 0$; and unfolding $\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ along a dual spanning tree produces a flat printable papercraft net (\Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(c)).

\begin{theorem}[Complete $K_N$ strut networks and two-gauge nested wireframes]
\label{thm:complete-network}
Let $S$ be an arrangement of $\{1,\dots,N\}$, $N=n^2$ ($n\ge2$), with $\sum_{u=1}^N\mathbf p_u(\lambda)=\mathbf 0$ and empirical covariance matrix $\Sigma(\lambda)=\frac1N\sum_{u=1}^N\mathbf p_u(\lambda)\mathbf p_u(\lambda)^{\top}$ in the slender-rod limit $d/L_{uv}\to 0^+$. If each of the $\binom{N}{2}$ rods connecting $\mathbf p_u(\lambda)$ and $\mathbf p_v(\lambda)$ ($1\le u<v\le N$) carries equal mass $m_{\mathrm{edge}}=M_{\mathrm{rods}}/\binom{N}{2}$ (with optional equal node masses $m_{\mathrm{node}}=M_{\mathrm{nodes}}/N$), then $(N-1)$-regularity of $K_N$ places the centre of mass at $O$ for every arrangement $S$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kN-com}
\mathbf c\bigl(K_N(\lambda)\bigr) = \frac{1}{\binom{N}{2}}\sum_{1\le u<v\le N}\frac{\mathbf p_u(\lambda)+\mathbf p_v(\lambda)}{2} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^N\mathbf p_u(\lambda) = \mathbf 0,
\end{equation}
and the continuous second-moment covariance matrix of the $\binom{N}{2}$ rods is a universal scalar multiple of $\Sigma(\lambda)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kN-cov}
\Sigma_{\mathrm{rods}}^{(=\text{mass})}\bigl(K_N(\lambda)\bigr)
= \frac{2N-3}{3(N-1)}\,\Sigma(\lambda)
\qquad\Bigl(=\frac{5}{8}\,\Sigma(\lambda)\text{ at }n=3,\quad \frac{29}{45}\,\Sigma(\lambda)\text{ at }n=4\Bigr),
\end{equation}
so that for every semi-magic square $S$, the equal-mass complete network $K_N\bigl(P_S(\lambda)\bigr)$ shares the exact axisymmetric inertia ratio $I_\perp(\lambda)/I_{zz}=\frac12\bigl(1+\lambda^2(n^2+1)\bigr)$ and spherical-top scale $\lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}=(n^2+1)^{-1/2}$ of \Cref{thm:rotor}.

When a subgraph $G=(V_G,E_G)$ is instead printed with uniform rod diameter $d$ so that each rod has mass proportional to its length $L_{uv}(\lambda)=\|\mathbf p_u(\lambda)-\mathbf p_v(\lambda)\|_2$, both $K_{16}$ and the outer wireframe $G(\mathcal G_0)$ have $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{len}}=\mathbf 0$ if and only if $S$ is one of the $48$ associative squares, while passing from $G(\mathcal G_0)$ to the $120$-rod complete network $K_{16}$ reduces mean centroid drift $\|\mathbf c_{\mathrm{len}}\|_2$ across $\mathcal M_4$ by $2.7\times$ at $\lambda=1$ (from $0.194$ to $0.072$) and by $3.6\times$ at $\lambda=0.20$ (from $0.088$ to $0.025$; \Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(d)). Furthermore, for the $848$ order-four gems with $\dim\mathcal G_1(S)\ge1$, printing the outer edges $E(\mathcal G_0)$ with rod diameter $d_0$ and the disjoint inner core edges $E(\mathcal G_1)$ with rod diameter $d_1$ restores $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_0\cup\mathcal G_1)=\mathbf 0$ whenever $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_0)$ and $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_1)$ are strictly anti-parallel, at the unique rod diameter ratio
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rod-diam-ratio}
\frac{d_1}{d_0} = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{lin},1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{lin},0}}}
= \sqrt{-\frac{L\bigl(G(\mathcal G_0)\bigr)\,\bigl(\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_0)\cdot\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_1)\bigr)}{L\bigl(G(\mathcal G_1)\bigr)\,\|\mathbf c_{\mathrm{wire}}(\mathcal G_1)\|_2^2}},
\end{equation}
balancing $158$ Group~VI gems at $\lambda=1$ (such as Gem \#673 at $d_1/d_0=0.793$ and Gem \#45 at $d_1/d_0=0.795$) and $164$ Group~VI gems at $\lambda=0.20$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrating $\mathbf x(t)=(1-t)\mathbf p_u+t\mathbf p_v$ over $t\in[0,1]$ gives midpoint $\frac12(\mathbf p_u+\mathbf p_v)$ and second moment $\frac13(\mathbf p_u\mathbf p_u^{\top}+\mathbf p_v\mathbf p_v^{\top})+\frac16(\mathbf p_u\mathbf p_v^{\top}+\mathbf p_v\mathbf p_u^{\top})$. Since each vertex belongs to $N-1$ edges of $K_N$ and $\sum_{u<v}(\mathbf p_u\mathbf p_v^{\top}+\mathbf p_v\mathbf p_u^{\top})=-N\Sigma(\lambda)$, averaging over $\binom{N}{2}$ edges yields \eqref{eq:kN-com}--\eqref{eq:kN-cov}. Exact evaluation of the edge lengths and \eqref{eq:rod-diam-ratio} across all $880$ squares establishes the length-weighted and nested wireframe censuses (\Cref{sec:appendix}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Papercraft cutout nets, glue-tab invariant, and nested thin-shell balance]
\label{prop:papercraft-net}
Let $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ be any three-dimensional Magic Gem with $f$-vector $(f_0,f_1,f_2)$. Unfolding $\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ into a connected planar net along any spanning tree $T^*$ of the dual facet graph $G^*(\mathcal G)$ \cite{durer1525underweysung,shephard1975convex,demaine2007geometric,ghomi2014durer} partitions the $f_1$ edges into $f_2-1$ interior fold creases and $N_{\mathrm{tabs}}=f_1-(f_2-1)$ cut edges joined by trapezoidal glue tabs (paired by labels \texttt{a}--\texttt{k} in \Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(c)). Because $\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)\cong S^2$, the cut edges $E\setminus E(T^*)$ form a spanning tree $T$ of the primal $1$-skeleton $G(\mathcal G_\lambda)$ on the $f_0$ hull vertices, so the number of glue tabs depends solely on the hull vertex count $f_0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:glue-tabs}
N_{\mathrm{tabs}} = f_1 - (f_2 - 1) = |E(T)| = f_0 - 1,
\end{equation}
taking every integer value in $\{7,\dots,15\}$ across $\mathcal M_4$ ($11$ tabs for D\"urer's \#175). Under the deterministic breadth-first dual spanning tree $T^*$ rooted at the largest-area facet $F_0$ ($\bigstar F_0$ in \Cref{fig:wireframes-nets}(c)), all $880$ order-four Magic Gems ($100\%$) unfold into strictly non-overlapping 2D planar nets at both $\lambda=1$ and $\lambda=0.20$. Moreover, a uniform boundary shell $\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ of areal density $\sigma_0>0$ has $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{shell}}(\mathcal G_0)=\mathbf 0$ on the $48$ associative squares, and enclosing a nested inner shell $\partial\mathcal G_1(S)$ ($\dim\mathcal G_1=3$) of areal density $\sigma_1$ restores $\mathbf c_{\mathrm{shell}}(\partial\mathcal G_0\cup\partial\mathcal G_1)=\mathbf 0$ for $80$ Group~VI gems at $\lambda=1$ ($87$ at $\lambda=0.20$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By planar graph duality on $\partial\mathcal G_\lambda(S)\cong S^2$, the complement of a dual spanning tree $T^*\subset G^*(\mathcal G_\lambda)$ ($|E(T^*)|=f_2-1$) is a primal spanning tree $T\subset G(\mathcal G_\lambda)$ on the $f_0$ vertices, giving $N_{\mathrm{tabs}}=|E(T)|=f_0-1$ (invariant under facet triangulation). Exact 2D polygon intersection testing and triangular surface moments certify non-overlap and the shell censuses across all $880$ gems (\Cref{sec:appendix}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical realization of finite-diameter struts and core suspension]
\label{rem:finite-diameter}
In a physical 3D print of finite rod diameter $d>0$, each cylindrical strut of length $L_{uv}$ and direction $\hat{\mathbf e}_{uv}$ retains midpoint centroid $\frac12(\mathbf p_u+\mathbf p_v)$ and adds the radial inertia correction $m_{uv}\frac{d^2}{16}(I_3+\hat{\mathbf e}_{uv}\hat{\mathbf e}_{uv}^{\top})$ up to $O(d^3)$ hub-overlap volumes. Suspending the inner core wireframe $G(\mathcal G_1)$ inside $G(\mathcal G_0)$ on a Group~VI gem via $C_2$-symmetric pairs of equal-length struts places the suspension centroid at $O$, leaving the balancing diameter ratio $d_1/d_0$ in \eqref{eq:rod-diam-ratio} invariant.
\end{remark}
