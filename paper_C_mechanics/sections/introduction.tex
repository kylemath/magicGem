\section{Introduction: Balance in Inequality}
\label{sec:intro}

Over five centuries ago, Albrecht D\"urer contributed two enduring landmarks at the intersection of mathematics and the visual arts: the order-four magic square in his 1514 engraving \emph{Melencolia~I} (square \#175 in Fr\'enicle's 1693 census of the $880$ order-four magic squares \cite{frenicle1693,dudeney1917amusements,andrews1960magic}) and the first printed polyhedral net unfoldings in his 1525 treatise \emph{Underweysung der Messung} \cite{durer1525underweysung,shephard1975convex,demaine2007geometric,ghomi2014durer}. A magic square is a quintessential study in \emph{balance in inequality}: an arrangement $S=(s_{ij})_{0\le i,j<n}$ of the first $N=n^2$ positive integers $\{1,\dots,N\}$ in which no two cells are equal, yet every row, column, and main diagonal sums to the exact same magic constant $\mu_n=n(N+1)/2$ (a \emph{semi-magic square} if the row and column sums equal $\mu_n$). Measuring coordinates from the board centre and the mean entry $\bar s=(N+1)/2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coords}
x_j = j - \frac{n-1}{2},\qquad
y_i = \frac{n-1}{2} - i,\qquad
z_{ij} = s_{ij} - \frac{N+1}{2},
\end{equation}
lifts each cell $(i,j)$ into a three-dimensional point and radial vector from the origin $O=(0,0,0)$:
\[
\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda) = (x_j,\, y_i,\, \lambda z_{ij}) \in \R^3,\qquad
P_S(\lambda) = \bigl\{\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda) : 0\le i,j<n\bigr\},
\]
where $\lambda>0$ is the vertical lift scale (\Cref{fig:origin-vectors-3d}(a)). The convex hull
\[
\mathcal G_\lambda(S) := \conv P_S(\lambda) \subset \R^3
\qquad\bigl(\mathcal G(S):=\mathcal G_1(S)\bigr)
\]
is a three-dimensional convex polyhedron that we call the \emph{Magic Gem} of $S$, reuniting D\"urer's magic squares with his polyhedral geometry.

In the flat grid plane, Loly \cite{loly2004invariance} and Rogers and Loly \cite{rogers2004inertia,rogers2005multipole} showed that interpreting the unequal entries $s_{ij}$ as point masses at $\mathbf r_{ij}=(x_j,y_i)$ places the planar centre of mass at the origin $O$ ($\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\mathbf r_{ij}=\mathbf 0$) and makes the polar moment of inertia $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}(x_j^2+y_i^2)=n^2(n^4-1)/12$ invariant across all semi-magic squares of order $n$. When a lifted magic square is fabricated as a three-dimensional physical object—whether as a radial spoke star, a planar balance wheel, a spinning top on a pivot, a cast solid, a 3D-printed wireframe, a folded papercraft shell, or a freestanding tower of blocks \cite{mathewson2025magicgemweb}—its unequal entries are subjected to progressively nonlinear geometric transformations. This motivates our central hypothesis: \emph{combinatorial line-sum equality guarantees physical equilibrium whenever a mechanical observable is linear or symmetric-quadratic in the grid coordinates, neutralizing entry inequality universally across all $880$ order-four magic squares; when a nonlinear geometric constraint breaks that equilibrium, balance can be restored by pairing two opposing inequalities that cancel each other.}

We test this hypothesis across four progressive mechanical regimes, illustrated on six recurring flagship squares (\Cref{fig:hero-squares}). In \Cref{sec:vectors}, we examine one-dimensional radial spoke sculptures radiating from the origin $O$. At natural spoke lengths, every magic square balances at $O$ because unequal lengths compensate unequal directions. Trimming all sixteen spokes to equal unit length breaks equilibrium on all $832$ non-associative squares in 3D and on \emph{every} square in 2D, where the middle grid ring sits at an irrational radius ratio $r_2/r_1=\sqrt{5}$ relative to the inner and outer rings ($r_3/r_1=3$) and prevents exact cancellation beyond the Pell convergent $9/4\approx\sqrt{5}$ (square \#70, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(b), \Cref{thm:three-ring}). By length--weight duality (\Cref{prop:reciprocity}), equilibrium is restored either by playing aligned inner and outer ring dipoles against the middle ring via a power-law length profile $r^{\alpha^*}$ (such as $\alpha^*=-1$ on \#45, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(c), \Cref{cor:planar-power-roots}), or by assigning positive unequal tip weights $\mathbf w^*>\mathbf 0$ on the unit sphere $S^2$ and cubic spoke lengths ($\beta=3$, where the vertical resultant vanishes universally, \Cref{thm:3d-power}).

In \Cref{sec:dynamics}, we set balanced sculptures into three-dimensional rotation on a central pivot under gravity. When the sixteen unequal vertices $\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda)$ carry equal masses, line-sum equality eliminates all off-diagonal products of inertia, turning every semi-magic square into a smooth axisymmetric top that passes through an exact spherical-top transition at $\lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}=(n^2+1)^{-1/2}$ ($1/\sqrt{17}$ at $n=4$, \Cref{thm:rotor}). Replacing equal masses with the unequal magic entries $m_{ij}=s_{ij}$ (\Cref{thm:quadrupole-split}) breaks this rotational symmetry by exposing the planar quadrupole moment $Q_{xy}=\sum z_{ij}x_jy_i$ and the cubic vector resultant from \Cref{sec:vectors}, splitting the horizontal principal axes by $2|Q_{xy}|$ and triggering $180^\circ$ Dzhanibekov flips on $824$ of the $880$ squares (led by \#46 at $|Q_{xy}|=60$, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(d)).

In \Cref{sec:nested}, we pass from discrete point clouds to continuous polyhedral hulls $\mathcal G_0(S)=\conv P_S$. Casting $\mathcal G_0(S)$ as a uniform solid preserves near-axisymmetric inertia about its own centroid $\mathbf c_0(S)$, but shifts $\mathbf c_0(S)$ off the pivot $O$ on $829$ of the $880$ squares ($51$ balance naturally, \Cref{prop:51-balanced}) because interior grid points $P_S^{(1)}$ are omitted and boundary facets are weighted by unequal simplex volumes. Because $\sum\mathbf p_{ij}=\mathbf 0$, the outer hull vertices and the inner core points pull in strictly opposite directions across the origin ($V_0\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}}=-V_1\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{inner}}$, \Cref{prop:opposition}). Physically re-engaging the inner core $\mathcal G_1(S)=\conv P_S^{(1)}$ restores $\mathbf c=\mathbf 0$ across composite solids (such as denser-core gem \#673 and hollow-cavity gem \#54, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(c,e), \Cref{thm:diamond-balance}), single-material two-gauge wireframes and complete $K_{16}$ strut networks (\Cref{thm:complete-network}), and unfolded papercraft shells with $f_0-1$ glue tabs (\Cref{prop:papercraft-net}).

Finally, in \Cref{sec:stacking}, we remove the central pivot and place Magic Gems onto a flat horizontal table under gravity. While tall $\lambda=1$ gems topple on more than half of their facets, compressing the vertical lift to the cubic print scale $\lambda_{\mathrm{cube}}=1/5$ stabilizes $74.5\%$ of all $14{,}824$ facets (\Cref{prop:stability-census}). When stacking multiple blocks vertically, identical copies of a single gem wedge apart whenever the top and bottom facet normals fail to oppose ($\mathbf n_-\ne-\mathbf n_+$ on $756$ gems); alternating $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ with its inverted mirror twin $\mathcal G_\lambda(r^2\overline S)=-\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (\Cref{thm:mirror-tower}) pairs every slanted facet with an equal and opposite partner, locking chiral knight's-move blocks (\#763, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(f)) into a straight vertical column (\Cref{prop:knights-move}).

Across all four regimes, the order-four censuses stratify into three symmetry tiers (\Cref{sec:discussion}): universal properties governed by linear line sums hold across all $880$ squares; unweighted nonlinear cancellations hold on the $48$ associative squares of Dudeney's Group~III ($r^2S=\overline S$, led by D\"urer's \#175), where central inversion $\mathbf p_{-c}=-\mathbf p_c$ cancels opposite cells pairwise; and one-parameter counterbalancing between opposing rings or hull layers occurs in the $304$ self-complementary squares of Dudeney's Group~VI ($\overline S=hS$ or $vS$), where a $180^\circ$ half-turn symmetry ($C_2\subset SO(3)$) confines residual imbalance vectors to a single coordinate axis. Every theorem and census table is certified in exact rational arithmetic by \texttt{anc/verify\_paper\_c.py} (\Cref{sec:appendix}) and accompanied by real-time 3D simulations in \texttt{web/index.html}.

Throughout the paper, $A_n=\{(x_j,y_i):0\le i,j<n\}$ denotes the centred grid \eqref{eq:coords}, $a_n=(n^2-1)/12$ and $b_n=(n^4-1)/12=(n^2+1)a_n$ denote the marginal variances of $x_j$ and $z_{ij}$, and the dihedral group $D_4=\langle r,h\rangle$ (with $90^\circ$ rotation $r$ and reflections $h,v$) acts on arrangements together with the complement involution $\tau(S)=\overline S=(N+1)\mathbf 1-S$.
