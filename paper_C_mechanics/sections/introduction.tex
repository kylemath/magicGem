\section{Introduction: Balance in Inequality}
\label{sec:intro}

Over five centuries ago, Albrecht D\"urer contributed two enduring landmarks at the intersection of mathematics and the visual arts: the order-four magic square in his 1514 engraving \emph{Melencolia~I} (square \#175 in Fr\'enicle's 1693 census of the $880$ order-four magic squares \cite{frenicle1693,dudeney1917amusements,andrews1960magic}) and the first printed polyhedral net unfoldings in his 1525 treatise \emph{Underweysung der Messung} \cite{durer1525underweysung,shephard1975convex,demaine2007geometric,ghomi2014durer}. A magic square is a quintessential study in \emph{balance in inequality}: an arrangement $S=(s_{ij})_{0\le i,j<n}$ of the first $N=n^2$ positive integers $\{1,\dots,N\}$ in which no two cells are equal, yet every row, column, and main diagonal sums to the exact same magic constant $\mu_n=n(N+1)/2$ (a \emph{semi-magic square} if the row and column sums equal $\mu_n$). Measuring coordinates from the board centre and the mean entry $\bar s=(N+1)/2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coords}
x_j = j - \frac{n-1}{2},\qquad
y_i = \frac{n-1}{2} - i,\qquad
z_{ij} = s_{ij} - \frac{N+1}{2},
\end{equation}
lifts each cell $(i,j)$ into a three-dimensional point and radial vector from the origin $O=(0,0,0)$:
\[
\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda) = (x_j,\, y_i,\, \lambda z_{ij}) \in \R^3,\qquad
P_S(\lambda) = \bigl\{\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda) : 0\le i,j<n\bigr\},
\]
where $\lambda>0$ is the vertical lift scale (\Cref{fig:origin-vectors-3d}(a)). The convex hull
\[
\mathcal G_\lambda(S) := \conv P_S(\lambda) \subset \R^3
\qquad\bigl(\mathcal G(S):=\mathcal G_1(S)\bigr)
\]
is a three-dimensional convex polyhedron that we call the \emph{Magic Gem} of $S$, reuniting D\"urer's magic squares with his polyhedral geometry.

In the flat grid plane, Loly \cite{loly2004invariance} and Rogers and Loly \cite{rogers2004inertia,rogers2005multipole} showed that interpreting the unequal entries $s_{ij}$ as point masses at $\mathbf r_{ij}=(x_j,y_i)$ places the planar centre of mass at the origin $O$ ($\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\mathbf r_{ij}=\mathbf 0$) and makes the polar moment of inertia $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}(x_j^2+y_i^2)=n^2(n^4-1)/12$ invariant across all semi-magic squares of order $n$. When we fabricate a lifted magic square as a three-dimensional physical object—as a radial spoke star, a planar balance wheel, a spinning top on a pivot, a cast solid, a 3D-printed wireframe, a folded papercraft shell, or a tower of blocks \cite{mathewson2025magicgemweb}—we subject its unequal entries to nonlinear geometric transformations. This motivates one central hypothesis across all physical realizations:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
\textbf{Central Hypothesis (Balance in Inequality).} Combinatorial line-sum equality guarantees physical equilibrium whenever a mechanical observable is linear or symmetric-quadratic in the grid coordinates, neutralizing entry inequality universally across all $880$ order-four magic squares. As soon as a physical realization introduces a nonlinear geometric constraint—normalizing spoke lengths, weighting rotating vertices by their unequal heights, wrapping points in their 3D convex hull, or stacking slanted facets—hidden higher-order inequalities break equilibrium; yet in each regime, exact balance is restored by pairing two opposing inequalities that cancel each other.
\end{quote}

We test this hypothesis through four progressive mechanical experiments, illustrated on six recurring flagship squares (\Cref{fig:hero-squares}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Test~1: 1D Radial Rays from the Origin (\Cref{sec:vectors}).} Natural spoke vectors $\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda)$ balance at $O$ because unequal lengths compensate unequal directions. Trimming all $16$ spokes to equal unit length breaks balance on $832$ squares in 3D and on \emph{every} square in 2D, where the irrational middle-to-inner ring radius ratio $r_2/r_1=\sqrt{5}$ prevents the inner and outer rings from cancelling the middle ring beyond the Pell convergent $9/4\approx\sqrt{5}$ (square \#70, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(b)). By \emph{length--weight duality} (\Cref{prop:reciprocity}), equilibrium is restored by playing unequal positive tip weights $\mathbf w^*>\mathbf 0$ against unit 3D lengths (\Cref{thm:3d-power}) or by power-law spoke scaling $r^{\alpha^*}$ where aligned ring dipoles cancel (such as $\alpha^*=-1$ on \#45, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(c)).

\item \textbf{Test~2: 3D Rotational Precession vs.\ Dzhanibekov Tumbling (\Cref{sec:dynamics}).} When the $16$ unequal points $\mathbf p_{ij}(\lambda)$ carry equal masses, line-sum balance eliminates all off-diagonal inertia moments, making every semi-magic square a smooth axisymmetric rotor that passes through an exact spherical top at $\lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}=(n^2+1)^{-1/2}$ (\Cref{thm:rotor}). Replacing equal masses with the unequal magic entries $m_{ij}=s_{ij}$ (\Cref{thm:quadrupole-split}) exposes the quadrant quadrupole $Q_{xy}=\sum z_{ij}x_jy_i$, splitting the horizontal principal axes by $2|Q_{xy}|$ and triggering $180^\circ$ Dzhanibekov flips on $824$ squares (led by \#46, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(d)).

\item \textbf{Test~3: 3D Convex Hulls and ``Diamond-in-a-Diamond'' Counterweighting (\Cref{sec:nested}).} Casting the uniform solid convex hull $\mathcal G_0(S)=\conv P_S$ breaks centroid balance on $829$ squares by discarding the interior points $P_S^{(1)}$ and weighting facets by unequal simplex volumes. Because $\sum\mathbf p_{ij}=\mathbf 0$, the outer hull vertices and inner core points pull in strictly opposite directions ($V_0\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{outer}}=-V_1\overline{\mathbf p}_{\mathrm{inner}}$, \Cref{prop:opposition}). Re-engaging the inner core $\mathcal G_1(S)=\conv P_S^{(1)}$ restores $\mathbf c=\mathbf 0$ across solids (heavier core \#673 and hollow cavity \#54, \Cref{thm:diamond-balance}), two-gauge wireframes and complete $K_{16}$ strut networks (\Cref{thm:complete-network}), and unfolded papercraft shells with $f_0-1$ glue tabs (\Cref{prop:papercraft-net}).

\item \textbf{Test~4: Freestanding Gravity and Mirror-Twin Towers (\Cref{sec:stacking}).} Placing a gem on a table or stacking identical copies fails along steeply slanted facets because upper and lower normals rarely oppose each other ($\mathbf n_-\ne-\mathbf n_+$ on $756$ gems). Compressing the vertical lift to $\lambda_{\mathrm{cube}}=1/5$ stabilizes $74.5\%$ of all resting facets (\Cref{prop:stability-census}), while alternating $\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ with its inverted mirror twin $\mathcal G_\lambda(r^2\overline S)=-\mathcal G_\lambda(S)$ (\Cref{thm:mirror-tower}) pairs every slanted facet with an equal and opposite partner, locking chiral knight's-move blocks (\#763, \Cref{fig:hero-squares}(f)) into a vertical tower (\Cref{prop:knights-move}).
\end{enumerate}

Across all four tests, our censuses are governed by a single three-tier symmetry hierarchy (\Cref{sec:discussion}): properties linear in the magic constraints hold universally across all \textbf{$880$ squares}; pointwise nonlinear cancellations hold on the \textbf{$48$ associative squares} of Dudeney's Group~III ($r^2S=\overline S$, such as D\"urer's \#175, where central inversion $\mathbf p_{-c}=-\mathbf p_c$ cancels opposite cells pairwise); and one-parameter counterbalancing between opposing layers or rings occurs in the \textbf{$304$ self-complementary squares} of Dudeney's Group~VI ($\overline S=hS$ or $vS$, where a $180^\circ$ half-turn symmetry $C_2\subset SO(3)$ confines residual imbalance vectors to a single coordinate axis). Every result is certified in exact rational arithmetic by \texttt{anc/verify\_paper\_c.py} (\Cref{sec:appendix}) and paired with real-time 3D simulations in \texttt{web/index.html}.

\paragraph{Conventions and notation.}
Let $A_n=\{(x_j,y_i):0\le i,j<n\}$ denote the centred grid \eqref{eq:coords}, let $a_n=(n^2-1)/12$ and $b_n=(n^4-1)/12=(n^2+1)a_n$ denote the marginal variances of $x_j$ and $z_{ij}$, and let the dihedral group $D_4=\langle r,h\rangle$ (with $90^\circ$ rotation $r$ and reflections $h,v$) act on arrangements together with the complement involution $\tau(S)=\overline S=(N+1)\mathbf 1-S$. Companion preprints \cite{mathewson2026gems,mathewson2026moments} treat the static polyhedral combinatorics and bivariate Prouhet--Tarry--Escott moment hierarchy of semi-magic squares; the present paper is self-contained. Order-four magic squares are numbered $\#1,\dots,\#880$ by their 1-based position in Fr\'enicle's standard list (\texttt{anc/squares880.txt}).
