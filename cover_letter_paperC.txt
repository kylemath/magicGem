Dear Professor Ko,

Please find enclosed my manuscript titled "Mechanics and Balance of Magic Gems: Radial Vector Laws, Symmetric-Top Precession, Nested Core Inclusions, and Mirror-Twin Towers" (28 pages, 7 multi-panel figures, 5 tables), which I am submitting for consideration for publication as a Research Article in the Journal of Mathematics and the Arts.

Over five centuries ago, Albrecht Durer contributed two enduring landmarks at the intersection of mathematics and the visual arts: the order-four magic square in his engraving Melencolia I (1514) and the first printed polyhedral net unfoldings in Underweysung der Messung (1525). When the entries of an n x n magic square S are lifted vertically above the centered grid to form three-dimensional points p_{ij}(lambda) = (x_j, y_i, lambda z_{ij}), their convex hull defines a polyhedral sculpture—the "Magic Gem" G_lambda(S). Motivated by fabricating physical 3D-printed models, ball-and-rod wireframes, fully interconnected strut sculptures, and folded cardstock cutouts across all 880 order-four magic squares (openly shared with an interactive 3D visual gallery and physics web essay at https://github.com/kylemath/magicGemWeb and https://github.com/kylemath/magicGem), this paper investigates how the combinatorial symmetries of a magic square govern the physical balance, rotational dynamics, skeletal and papercraft architecture, and sculptural stacking of its 3D polyhedron:

1. Radial vector balance and the sqrt(5) Pell barrier (Section 2): By an exact length-weight duality, scaling 3D origin vectors by power-law lengths R_{ij}^beta is mechanically equivalent to weighting the natural vectors by R_{ij}^{beta - 1}. For cubic 3D lengths (beta = 3), the vertical resultant vanishes identically for every semi-magic square, while on the unit sphere S^2 (beta = 0) all 880 order-four magic squares admit positive minimum-variance balancing weights. In the plane, decomposing the 4 x 4 grid into three concentric rings proves that planar unit vectors never balance because r_2 / r_1 = sqrt(5) is irrational; the global minimum norm across all 880 squares (approx. 0.07049) is governed by the fundamental Pell convergent 9/4 on eight Dudeney Group VI squares.

2. Symmetric-top precession, spherical tops, and Dzhanibekov flips (Section 3): Extending the planar magic-square inertia results of Loly and Rogers (2004, 2005) into three dimensions, we show that equal point masses make every semi-magic gem an axisymmetric rotor with universal spherical-top transition scale lambda_{iso} = (n^2 + 1)^{-1/2} (= 1/sqrt(17) at n = 4) and closed 9:1 polhode orbits at lambda = 1. Under entry-weighted masses m_{ij} = s_{ij}, the off-diagonal quadrupole moment Q_{xy}(S) splits the horizontal moments and triggers the intermediate-axis (Dzhanibekov) flip on 824 of the 880 squares while leaving all 48 associative squares (including Durer's square #175) strictly axisymmetric.

3. Multi-material "diamond-in-a-diamond" cores, 3D-printed wireframes, complete K_{16} strut sculptures, and Durer papercraft nets (Section 4): While passing from discrete point masses to a uniform solid polyhedron shifts the center of mass off the origin for 829 of the 880 squares, we classify the 51 naturally balanced solids and show how two-density core inclusions G_1(S) subset G_0(S) restore exact balance in 35 Group VI gems (led by 8 printable denser-core gems with modest excess density Delta rho_1 / rho_0 <= 40/19 and 2 lighter-core gems). Extending this analysis to physical skeletal and thin-shell realizations, we prove that the complete 120-rod network K_{16} with equal-mass struts has centroid at the origin and continuous rod covariance equal to (29/45) Sigma(lambda) for every order-four square; that tuning the inner-to-outer rod diameter ratio d_1 / d_0 in a single material balances 158 Group VI nested wireframes G(G_0) U G(G_1); and that unfolding the boundary surface along a breadth-first dual spanning tree yields a strictly non-overlapping 2D papercraft cutout net with N_{tabs} = f_0 - 1 glue tabs across all 880 order-four Magic Gems (verifying Durer's / Shephard's unfolding conjecture across the entire order-four ensemble).

4. Freestanding mirror-twin sculptural towers (Section 5): Finally, we prove that central inversion G_lambda(r^2 \bar{S}) = -G_lambda(S) locks any gem with centrally symmetric top and bottom facets onto its complementary mirror twin to form zero-rotation vertical towers, led by 17 gems whose contact facets project to centered area-5 knight's-move squares (such as square #763, which stands without sliding at the cubic print scale lambda = 0.20 whenever the static friction coefficient exceeds mu_s = 0.20).

For complete transparency, two companion preprints by the author examine separate pure-combinatorial aspects of lifted magic squares: one (submitted to Experimental Mathematics, arXiv:2512.09170) catalogs the static polyhedral combinatorics (f-vectors, vertex survival, and all-odd-n Siamese volumes), and a second (submitted to Discrete Applied Mathematics) studies abstract Prouhet-Tarry-Escott polynomial matrix moments and random permutation energies. The present manuscript is self-contained and is the only paper in the series investigating vector balance laws, 3D rigid-body dynamics, nested multi-material and wireframe/papercraft fabrication, and mirror-twin sculptural stacking.

This manuscript is original, is my sole work, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. Thank you very much for your time and consideration.
