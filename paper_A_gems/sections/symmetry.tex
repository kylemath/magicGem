\section{Symmetry and congruence}
\label{sec:symmetry}

Let $\tau(x,y,z)=(x,y,-z)$ be the reflection in the plane $z=0$.
The \emph{complement} of an arrangement is $\overline S=N+1-S$, with height function $-z$; $S$ is \emph{self-complementary} if $\overline S=gS$ for some $g\in D_4$, and \emph{associative} if $\overline S=r^2S$, i.e.\ if entries in cells symmetric about the centre add up to $N+1$.
Complements of magic squares are magic.
Since the points of $\overline S$ are $\tau\mathbf p_c$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(\overline S)=\tau\,\mathcal G(S).
\label{eq:complement}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Elements of $D_4$ that can complement]
\label{lem:selfcomp}
Let $\overline S=gS$ for an arrangement $S$ of order $n\ge2$ and some $g\in D_4$. Then $g$ is unique, $g^2=\mathrm{id}$, and $g$ fixes no cell except possibly the centre cell of an odd grid, whose entry is then $\bar s$.
Consequently $g=r^2$ (so $S$ is associative) if $n$ is odd, and $g\in\{r^2,h,v\}$ if $n$ is even.
The map $\iota_g=\hat g\,\tau$ is an isometry of $\R^3$ that maps the point set of $S$ onto itself, so $\iota_g\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))$; explicitly $\iota_{r^2}=-\mathrm{id}$ (a point reflection), $\iota_h(x,y,z)=(-x,y,-z)$ and $\iota_v(x,y,z)=(x,-y,-z)$ (half-turns about the $y$- and the $x$-axis).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If also $\overline S=g'S$ then $g^{-1}g'$ fixes $S$; as the entries are distinct it fixes every cell, and $D_4$ acts faithfully on the cells, so $g=g'$.
Since complementation commutes with $g$, $g^2S=g\overline S=\overline{gS}=\overline{\overline S}=S$, so $g^2=\mathrm{id}$.
If $g$ fixes a cell $c$ then $s_c=N+1-s_c=2\bar s$.
The involutions of $D_4$ are $r^2$, $h$, $v$ and the two diagonal reflections; the diagonal reflections fix $n\ge2$ cells, $h$ and $v$ fix the $n$ cells of the middle column or row when $n$ is odd and none when $n$ is even, and $r^2$ fixes only the centre cell when $n$ is odd.
Finally, the points of $S$ are mapped by $\tau$ to those of $\overline S=gS$, and by $\hat g=\hat g^{-1}$ back to those of $g^2S=S$, by \eqref{eq:d4}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Point sets of semi-magic squares]
\label{thm:pointset}
Let $S$ and $T$ be arrangements of order $n\ge2$ whose dipole moments vanish, for instance semi-magic squares, and let $P_S$, $P_T$ be their point sets.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every isometry $A$ of $\R^3$ with $A(P_S)=P_T$ is of the form $\hat g$ or $\hat g\,\tau$ with $g\in D_4$; $\hat g(P_S)=P_T$ if and only if $T=gS$, and $\hat g\,\tau(P_S)=P_T$ if and only if $T=g\overline S$.
\item $P_S$ and $P_T$ are congruent if and only if $T\in D_4S\cup D_4\overline S$.
\item $\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)=\{\mathrm{id}\}$ unless $S$ is self-complementary, and $\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)=\{\mathrm{id},\iota_g\}$ if $\overline S=gS$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An isometry of $\R^3$ mapping one finite set onto another maps centroid to centroid, and both centroids are $\mathbf 0$ (\Cref{lem:lines}(1)); so $A$ is orthogonal, and $A\Sigma_SA^{\top}=\Sigma_T$ for the covariance matrices.
By \Cref{lem:cov}, $\Sigma_S=\Sigma_T=\operatorname{diag}(a_n,a_n,b_n)$ with $a_n\neq b_n$, so $A$ commutes with $\Sigma_S$ and preserves its eigenspaces, the plane $z=0$ and the $z$-axis: $A=\operatorname{diag}(B,\varepsilon)$ with $B\in O(2)$ and $\varepsilon=\pm1$.
The projection of both point sets to the plane is the grid, so $B$ is a symmetry of the grid, that is, an element $g\in D_4$; hence $A=\hat g$ or $A=\hat g\tau$.
By \eqref{eq:d4}, $\hat g(P_S)=P_{gS}$ and $\hat g\tau(P_S)=P_{g\overline S}$, and the point set determines the arrangement; this proves (1), and (2) follows.
For (3) take $T=S$: $\hat g(P_S)=P_S$ forces $gS=S$, hence $g=\mathrm{id}$, and $\hat g\tau(P_S)=P_S$ forces $g\overline S=S$, i.e.\ $\overline S=g^{-1}S=gS$ by \Cref{lem:selfcomp}.
\end{proof}

So for every order the symmetries of the point set of a semi-magic square are exactly those coming from self-complementarity, and congruences between point sets come only from $D_4$ and complementation.
For the gem, $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))=\operatorname{Iso}(\operatorname{vert}\mathcal G(S))$, since isometries preserve extreme points, and $\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))$; the inclusion is an equality when every point is a vertex, but in general the gem might have isometries that do not preserve the non-vertex points.
For order four this does not happen.

\begin{theorem}[Isometry groups of the order-four gems; exact computation]
\label{thm:symmetry}
For each of the $880$ essentially different magic squares $S$ of order $4$, $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))=\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)$. Hence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for the $48$ associative squares, $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))=\{\mathrm{id},-\mathrm{id}\}$ ($C_i$);
\item for the $304$ self-complementary squares that are not associative, $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))=\{\mathrm{id},\iota_g\}$ with $g\in\{h,v\}$, a half-turn about a horizontal coordinate axis ($C_2$);
\item for the remaining $528$ squares, $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))$ is trivial.
\end{enumerate}
In particular a gem has an improper isometry if and only if $S$ is associative, and the $832$ gems in cases (2) and (3) are chiral.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The squares were sorted into the three cases by comparing $\overline S$ with the eight images $gS$; by \Cref{lem:selfcomp} the complementing element is $r^2$, $h$ or $v$.
For each square we computed $\operatorname{Iso}(\operatorname{vert}\mathcal G(S))$ in exact integer arithmetic (\Cref{sec:appendix}); its order is $2$ in cases (1) and (2) and $1$ in case (3), equal to the order of $\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)$ by \Cref{thm:pointset}(3), and $\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))$.
The maps $\iota_h$, $\iota_v$ are proper.
\end{proof}

The partition (1)--(3) is classical: the associative squares form Dudeney's group III and the self-complementary non-associative ones his group VI \cite{dudeney1917amusements}.
What \Cref{thm:symmetry} adds is that the hulls have no further symmetry.
Likewise the congruence classes of the $880$ gems are the orbits of complementation (\Cref{prop:gem-injective}).

\begin{lemma}[Parity for associative squares]
\label{lem:parity}
Let $S$ be associative of order $n$ and not a $D_4$-image of the row-by-row arrangement (for instance, associative and semi-magic). Then $V$ and $V^\partial$ are even and $V^\circ\equiv n\pmod2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The point set is invariant under $-\mathrm{id}$ (\Cref{lem:selfcomp}), which therefore permutes the vertices, the interior points and the boundary non-vertex points of the $3$-polytope $\mathcal G(S)$ (\Cref{prop:fulldim}). Its only fixed point is $\mathbf 0$, which is a point $\mathbf p_c$ exactly when $n$ is odd (the centre cell), and then an interior point.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis is needed: the row-by-row arrangement of order $3$ is associative, but its gem is a parallelogram and has no interior points.
For $n=4$ the $48$ associative gems have $V^\circ\in\{0,2,4\}$ ($4$, $23$ and $21$ gems); among the other squares both parities occur.

\begin{remark}[Facet orbits]
\label{rem:facet-orbits}
For an associative square, $-\mathrm{id}$ fixes no facet, since no facet plane passes through the interior point $\mathbf 0$; so $F$ is even. For the half-turn $\iota_h$ (the case $\iota_v$ is the same after transposition), a facet plane is $\iota_h$-invariant if and only if its normal is parallel to the axis, i.e.\ if it is one of the vertical planes $y=\pm\frac{n-1}2$ over the top and bottom rows; by \Cref{prop:subdivisions}(2) these carry facets unless the row is an arithmetic progression. Among the $304$ order-four gems of case (2), $282$ have two fixed facets, $18$ one and $4$ none. Neither isometry fixes a vertex, so $V$ is even for all $352$ self-complementary squares.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Central-symmetry defect]
\label{rem:sigma}
For a convex body $K\subset\R^3$ with non-empty interior let $\sigma(K)=\operatorname{vol}\bigl(\tfrac12(K-K)\bigr)/\operatorname{vol}(K)$. By the Rogers--Shephard inequalities \cite{rogers1957difference}, $1\le\sigma(K)\le\binom63/8=\frac52$, with equality on the left if and only if $K$ is centrally symmetric and on the right if and only if $K$ is a simplex; see \cite{grunbaum1963measures,schneider2014convex} for this and other measures of symmetry.
By \Cref{thm:symmetry}, $\sigma=1$ exactly for the $48$ associative gems of order four; for the other $832$, an exact computation (\texttt{anc/central\_symmetry\_defect.py}) gives $\sigma\in[1057/1018,\,145/88]\approx[1.038,\,1.648]$.
\end{remark}
