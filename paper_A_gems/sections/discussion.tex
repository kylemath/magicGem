\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:discussion}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \emph{Hull versus point set.} Is $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal G(S))=\operatorname{Iso}(P_S)$ for every magic, or semi-magic, square $S$? By \Cref{thm:pointset} this would determine the isometry groups of all gems; it holds for order four (\Cref{thm:symmetry}) and for the Siamese squares (\Cref{thm:siamese}). For odd $n$ the self-complementary squares are associative (\Cref{lem:selfcomp}), so the point set of an order-five square has isometry group $\{\pm\mathrm{id}\}$ or trivial; whether the same holds for the gems is open.
\item \emph{Order five.} Schroeppel's enumeration gives $275{,}305{,}224$ essentially different squares of order five \cite{gardner1976}. At the rate of the order-four computation (about $6$\,ms per exact hull) a full census would take about three CPU-weeks. How many combinatorial types and congruence classes occur, and are the congruence classes again the complement orbits?
\item \emph{Growth of the vertex count.} For uniformly random arrangements, with $10^5$ samples for each $n$, the mean vertex count is $8.22$, $12.06$, $15.39$, $18.39$, $21.08$, $23.53$, $25.79$, $27.91$ for $n=3,\dots,10$ (standard errors at most $0.01$), and the ratio $\mathbb E[V]/\ln^2N$ decreases slowly from $1.70$ to $1.32$. For $N$ independent uniform points in a $3$-polytope the expected number of vertices of their hull is $\Theta(\ln^2N)$ \cite{barany1993random}. Is $\mathbb E[V]=\Theta(\ln^2 n)$ for random arrangements, and for uniformly random magic squares? Samples of magic squares obtained by simulated annealing (\texttt{anc/sa\_magic.c}; $59{,}993$, $24{,}000$, $8{,}000$ and $2{,}400$ squares for $n=5,\dots,8$) have mean vertex counts $15.38$, $18.41$, $21.08$, $23.56$, within $1.2$ standard errors of the random values; the sampling distribution of the annealer is not known (compare the Monte Carlo methods of \cite{pinn1998number}). At $n=4$ the exact magic mean is $269/22\approx12.23$.
\item \emph{Secondary cones.} Which pairs of secondary cones of the $n\times n$ grid contain $(z,-z)$ for the height function $z$ of a magic square (\Cref{prop:subdivisions})? For $n=4$ the $7040$ magic height functions induce $417$ regular subdivisions up to $D_4$.
\item \emph{Linear squares.} Which linear squares (\Cref{rem:linear}) have a vertex count bounded independently of $n$, and does the digit-exchanged Siamese square of \Cref{rem:linear} have $f$-vector $(12,24,14)$ for every odd $n\ge5$?
\item \emph{Other families.} The construction applies to semi-magic, pandiagonal and Franklin squares \cite{loly2007franklin}, and \Cref{prop:fulldim} and \Cref{thm:pointset} already cover the semi-magic case.
\end{enumerate}
