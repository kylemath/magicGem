\begin{abstract}
Place the centred entries of an $n\times n$ magic square as a height function $z$ over the centred cell grid.
The magic conditions are then the orthogonality of $z$ to a $2n$-dimensional space $W$ of centred line indicators.
This viewpoint gives several exact results.
(i)~A moment hierarchy: orthogonality to $x^m,y^m$ for $m\le k$ is equivalent to equal row and column sums exactly when $k\ge n-1$ for real heights, whereas for permutations of $1,\dots,n^2$ integrality lowers the threshold to at most $n-2$ for every $n\ge14$.
(ii)~Balance of the signed mass $z$: $z\perp W$ forces every separable moment, including Loly's moment-of-inertia invariant, to vanish; for an arbitrary arrangement the centre-of-mass offset is bounded by the line-sum energy with a sharp constant; balance of unit vectors is not implied (exact values for the eight $3\times3$ squares).
(iii)~Exact mean and variance of the energy of a uniformly random arrangement, with a limit law.
(iv)~An exact single-swap formula, giving strict local minimality of every magic square and gaps $d^2m/n^4$.
(v)~The exact number $14{,}639{,}440$ of $4\times4$ arrangements satisfying four low-order conditions, of which $7{,}040$ are magic.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} magic squares, orthogonality, permutation statistics, moment of inertia, local search

\noindent\textbf{MSC 2020:} 05B15, 11B75, 60C05
\end{abstract}
