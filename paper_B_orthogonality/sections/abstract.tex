\begin{abstract}
We regard the centred entries of an $n\times n$ arrangement of $1,\dots,n^2$ as a function $z$ on the centred grid; the semi-magic and magic conditions say that $z$ is orthogonal to the centred row, column and diagonal indicators.
Replacing the line indicators by the powers $x^m,y^m$ ($m\le k$) of the column and row coordinates gives the degree-$k$ moment conditions, which for real-valued $z$ are equivalent to equal row and column sums exactly when $k\ge n-1$.
The permutation threshold $k^*(n)$ is the least $k$ for which the degree-$k$ conditions force every arrangement to be semi-magic.
These conditions say that the column deviations are the coefficients of an integer polynomial divisible by $(t-1)^{k+1}$, which connects $k^*(n)$ with the Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem and with the Littlewood-type problems of Borwein, Erd\'elyi and K\'os.
We prove $(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt n\le k^*(n)\le\sqrt{\pi e\,(n-1)\log(n^4/4)}$, the lower bound by a box-principle construction inside semi-magic squares with many complementary pairs of rows, and we determine $k^*(n)$ for $n\le40$: $k^*(n)=n-1$ for $n\le13$, $k^*(n)=n-2$ for $14\le n\le17$, and $k^*(n)\le n-3$ for every $n\ge18$.
For a uniformly random arrangement we prove an exact formula for the variance of a quadratic form with zero row sums in the centred entries.
It gives closed forms for the mean and variance of the sum of squared line-sum deviations and of a four-term low-order energy; the latter, divided by its mean, converges in distribution to $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$.
We also give the null distribution of Loly's moment-of-inertia invariant, and the exact number $14{,}639{,}440$ of $4\times4$ arrangements satisfying the four low-order conditions, of which $7{,}040$ are magic.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} magic squares, Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem, polynomials with integer coefficients, permutation statistics, combinatorial central limit theorem, moment of inertia

\end{abstract}
