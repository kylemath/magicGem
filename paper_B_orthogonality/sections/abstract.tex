\begin{abstract}
Every semi-magic square of order $n$ satisfies the polynomial moment identities $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}j^m=M(n)\sum_jj^m$ and $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}i^m=M(n)\sum_ii^m$ for all $m\ge1$, where $M(n)=n(n^2+1)/2$ is the magic constant. For arbitrary real matrices, the converse requires these axial moment conditions up to degree $m=n-1$ (Vandermonde). What happens when the matrix is constrained to be an \emph{arrangement}—a permutation of $\{1,\dots,n^2\}$?

We study this question from two complementary directions. First, we define the \emph{permutation threshold} $k^*(n)$ as the smallest degree $k$ such that every arrangement satisfying the axial moment conditions for $m=1,\dots,k$ is semi-magic. In terms of the column-sum deviations $\gamma_j$, the degree-$k$ conditions state that $\sum_j\gamma_jt^j$ is divisible by $(t-1)^{k+1}$, linking $k^*(n)$ to the Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem and to integer polynomials with a high-order zero at $t=1$. While Schur's bound on real zeros yields $k^*(n)\le\sqrt{2(n-1)\log(n^4/4)}=O(\sqrt{n\log n})$, our main theorem establishes the converse realisability direction: by constructing semi-magic squares with many pairs of complementary rows and perturbing them via horizontal swaps and a pigeonhole collision, we prove that $k^*(n)\ge(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt n$ for all $n$, and $k^*(n)\ge(\sqrt6-o(1))\sqrt n$ when $4\mid n$. Explicit witness arrangements and short hand proofs show that $k^*(n)=n-1$ holds for $3\le n\le13$ before breaking down to $k^*(n)=n-2$ for $14\le n\le17$ and $k^*(n)\le n-3$ for all $n\ge18$; with computer assistance we determine $k^*(n)$ exactly for $18\le n\le40$.

Second, we examine the $k=1$ level of this moment hierarchy across the full permutation group $S_{n^2}$, comparing the full line-sum energy $\Efull$ against the four-term energy $\Efour$ formed from the two linear axial moments and the two main diagonals. Specialising the Mantel--Hubert variance formula for quadratic assignment statistics via M\"obius inversion on the partition lattice, we derive exact closed-form means and variances for both energies and for Loly's moment-of-inertia invariant under a uniform random arrangement, prove that $\Efour/\E[\Efour]\xrightarrow{d}\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, show that at order $n=3$ the four conditions $\Efour=0$ suffice to force a magic square, and determine the exact size $|L_4|=14{,}639{,}440$ of the zero set $\{\Efour=0\}$ at order $n=4$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} magic squares, Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem, polynomials with integer coefficients, permutation statistics, combinatorial central limit theorem, moment of inertia

\end{abstract}
