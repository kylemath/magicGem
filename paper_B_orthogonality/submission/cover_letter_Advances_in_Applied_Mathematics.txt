An n x n arrangement S = (s_{ij}) of {1, ..., n^2} is semi-magic if all its row and column sums equal the magic constant M(n) = n(n^2 + 1)/2. Every semi-magic square satisfies the axial moment identities sum_{i,j} s_{ij} j^m = sum_{i,j} s_{ij} i^m = M(n) sum_j j^m for all m >= 1. For real matrices, Vandermonde invertibility requires these identities for 1 <= m <= n - 1 to force equal row and column sums; however, for permutations of {1, ..., n^2}, integrality and bounded line sums cause the required degree to drop once n >= 14. Defining the permutation threshold k*(n) as the least k such that every arrangement of order n satisfying the axial moment identities of degrees 1 <= m <= k is semi-magic, this manuscript establishes a hierarchy of extremal, number-theoretic, and probabilistic results at the intersection of enumerative combinatorics, integer polynomials, and asymptotic permutation statistics:

1. Prouhet-Tarry-Escott equivalence and two-sided asymptotic bounds for k*(n): In terms of the column-sum deviations gamma_j, the degree-k moment conditions state that the generating polynomial P_gamma(t) = sum_j gamma_j t^j is divisible by (t - 1)^{k+1}, linking k*(n) to the Prouhet-Tarry-Escott problem and integer polynomials with a zero of high multiplicity at t = 1. Applying Schur's bound on real zeros with the majorization bound ||gamma||_1 <= n^4 / 4 yields the universal upper bound k*(n) <= floor(sqrt(2(n - 1) log(n^4 / 4))) <= 2 sqrt(2) sqrt(n log n) (Theorem 3.8(a)). Our main theorem establishes the converse realizability direction: by constructing semi-magic squares with many pairs of complementary rows and perturbing them via matched horizontal swaps combined with a pigeonhole box principle, we prove k*(n) >= (sqrt(2) - o(1)) sqrt(n) for all n, and k*(n) >= (sqrt(6) - o(1)) sqrt(n) when 4 | n (Theorem 3.8(b)).

2. Exact threshold values for small and intermediate orders: Using explicit witness arrangements and elementary lattice bounds, we prove unconditionally that k*(n) = n - 1 for 3 <= n <= 13, k*(n) = n - 2 for 14 <= n <= 17, and k*(n) <= n - 3 for all n >= 18 (Theorem 3.8(c)). Furthermore, via exact lattice enumeration backed by a standalone arithmetic certificate and computer-discovered witness arrangements, we determine the exact value of k*(n) for every order 18 <= n <= 40 (Theorem 3.8(d) and Table 2), showing that k*(n) coincides with the column-feasibility lattice threshold U(n) throughout this range (Conjecture 3.16).

3. Random permutation energies and exact zero-set enumeration at k = 1: At the lowest level k = 1 of the moment hierarchy, we analyze uniformly random arrangements across S_{n^2}. Specializing the Mantel-Hubert variance formula for quadratic assignment statistics to symmetric matrices with zero row sums via Mobius inversion on the partition lattice Pi_4 (Proposition 4.3), we derive exact closed-form rational variances for both the full line-sum energy E_full and the four-term relaxation E_4 (combining the two degree-1 axial moments with the two main diagonals; Theorem 4.4), as well as the exact null mean and variance of Loly's moment-of-inertia invariant (Corollary 4.2). Hoeffding's combinatorial central limit theorem shows that E_4 / E[E_4] converges in distribution to Exp(1) as n -> infinity (Corollary 4.5). Finally, whereas E_4 = 0 at n = 3 collapses strictly to the 8 magic squares (proved elementarily in Proposition 5.1), a meet-in-the-middle enumeration at n = 4 shows that exactly |L_4| = 14,639,440 of the 16! arrangements satisfy E_4 = 0, of which 7,040 are magic (Proposition 5.3), in close agreement with a Gaussian local-limit heuristic.

For complete transparency, this manuscript is algebraically and probabilistically self-contained. Companion preprints by the author examine separate geometric and physical questions on three-dimensional convex hulls ("Magic Gems", arXiv:2512.09170, submitted to Experimental Mathematics) and mechanical equilibria (submitted to the Journal of Mathematics and the Arts) of lifted magic squares; there is no overlap in theorems, proofs, or figures. All verification scripts, exact witness arrangements, C enumerators, and lattice certificates are included in the ancillary directory and publicly available at https://github.com/kylemath/magicGem.
