\begin{abstract}
An $n\times n$ arrangement of $1,\dots,n^2$ is semi-magic if all its row and column sums are equal.
If they are, then $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}j^m=M(n)\sum_jj^m$ and $\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}i^m=M(n)\sum_ii^m$ for every $m$; for real matrices the converse needs these moment identities up to $m=n-1$.
For arrangements fewer suffice.
Let the permutation threshold $k^*(n)$ be the least $k$ such that every arrangement satisfying them for $m=1,\dots,k$ is semi-magic.
In terms of the column-sum deviations $\gamma$ these conditions say that $\sum_j\gamma_jt^j$ is divisible by $(t-1)^{k+1}$, which links $k^*(n)$ to the Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem and to work of Borwein, Erd\'elyi and K\'os on integer polynomials with a zero of high order at $1$.
We prove $(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt n\le k^*(n)\le\sqrt{\pi e\,(n-1)\log(n^4/4)}$; the lower bound uses semi-magic squares with many pairs of complementary rows and the pigeonhole principle.
Explicit arrangements together with short proofs give $k^*(n)=n-1$ for $3\le n\le13$ and $k^*(n)=n-2$ for $14\le n\le17$, and we prove $k^*(n)\le n-3$ for all $n\ge18$; with computer assistance we determine $k^*(n)$ for all $n\le40$.
We also treat uniformly random arrangements: specialising the classical variance formula for quadratic assignment statistics, we give the exact mean and variance of the sum of squared line-sum deviations and of a four-term energy $E_4$ formed from the first moments and the two diagonals, show that $E_4$ divided by its mean converges in distribution to $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, give the mean and variance of Loly's moment-of-inertia invariant, and count the $14{,}639{,}440$ arrangements of order $4$ with $E_4=0$, of which $7{,}040$ are magic.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} magic squares, Prouhet--Tarry--Escott problem, polynomials with integer coefficients, permutation statistics, combinatorial central limit theorem, moment of inertia

\noindent\textbf{MSC 2020:} 05B20 (primary); 05A05, 11C08, 60C05, 60F05
\end{abstract}
