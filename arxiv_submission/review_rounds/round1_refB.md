# Referee B — Round 1 report on *Magic Gems: Magic Squares as Orthogonality Conditions on a Height Function* (v5, 26 pp.)

Referee profile: physicist / applied mathematician working on the physical properties of magic squares (moments of inertia, multipoles, spectra).

All numbers quoted below were checked against `Analysis/revision/*/results.json`, `SUMMARY.md` and, where stated, by my own scripts in `review_rounds/scratch_refB/`.

---

## 1. Summary, recommendation, score

The paper takes the entries of an n x n arrangement as a height function z over the centred grid, observes that the magic conditions are z ⊥ W for a 2n-dimensional space of centred line indicators, and uses this viewpoint to prove (i) a "moment hierarchy" (Cov(X^m,Z)=Cov(Y^m,Z)=0 for m ≤ k ⇔ equal row/column sums iff k ≥ n−1, with a permutation refinement k*(n)); (ii) exact mean/variance and a Hoeffding limit law for two sum-of-squares "energies" of a random arrangement; (iii) an exact single-swap formula; (iv) the exact count |L_4| = 14,639,440 of 4 x 4 arrangements with E_low = 0; (v) a classification of the 880 order-4 gems and a computer-assisted proof that Siamese gems have 12 vertices. Version 5 is a very substantial and candid repair of v4: the asymmetric energy, the trimodal bands, the entropy and the scaling fits are gone and every number I checked matches the data. What remains to fix is attribution and framing on the physics side, one internal inconsistency, and length.

**Recommendation: Major revision** (the fixes are not large, but two of them concern how prior work is represented and must be seen again). **Score: 6/10** now; 8/10 if the points below are addressed.

## 2. Numbered points

### (a) Attribution and framing of Loly / Rogers–Loly

**1. [MAJOR] "Loly's invariance is the degree-two case" conflates a one-dimensional invariant with a four-condition class.**
Location: abstract ("Loly's moment-of-inertia invariance is the degree-two case"), Contributions item 1, Corollary 4.13 title ("Loly's moment-of-inertia invariance is the degree-two statement"), Remark 4.9(i), Section 6.1 ("Loly's invariance ... is the degree-two member"), Conclusion.
Problem: Equation (12) itself shows that I(S) is constant iff the single scalar condition Cov(Z, X^2+Y^2) = 0 holds. The "degree-2 conditions" of Definition 4.10 are four separate conditions ⟨x,z⟩=⟨y,z⟩=⟨x^2,z⟩=⟨y^2,z⟩=0. The invariance of I is therefore a *consequence* of the degree-2 level, not the degree-2 level itself, and it is much weaker. I enumerated all 9! arrangements for n = 3 (`scratch_refB/check_n3.py`): **24,192 arrangements (6.7 % of 9!) have I = 60**, against 72 satisfying the degree-2 conditions (= the 72 semi-magic squares, in agreement with Remark 4.16). Describing Loly's invariant as "the degree-two member" of the hierarchy therefore oversells the correspondence by a factor of 336 at n = 3.
Resolution: say precisely what is true: "Loly's invariant is the linear combination Σ_j x_j^2 γ_j + Σ_i y_i^2 ρ_i + const of the degree-2 moments; it is forced by the degree-2 conditions and in particular by the semi-magic condition, but the invariance of I is a single condition and is satisfied by many more arrangements." Retitle Corollary 4.13 ("Loly's invariant from the hierarchy") and reword the abstract/contributions accordingly.

**2. [MAJOR] Semi-magic validity of Loly's invariant is not new and must not be listed as a contribution.**
Location: Contributions item 1: "Loly's invariance of the moment of inertia [13] is the degree-two statement and holds for all semi-magic squares (Corollary 4.13)"; Section 6.4 item 5: "the inertia invariance of Corollary 4.13 already covers the semi-magic case".
Problem: Loly's 2004 derivation computes I_z = I_x + I_y with I_x = Σ_j x_j^2 (column sum j) and I_y = Σ_i y_i^2 (row sum i), i.e. from the row and column sums alone; the diagonals never enter, and the fact that the formula holds for semi-magic squares is part of the standard account of that note (e.g. Peterson's *Science News* report of it, 2006, states explicitly that the same formula applies to semi-magic squares). Loly also records I_x = I_y = n^2(n^4−1)/24, i.e. I_z is twice the in-plane moment — the paper's eq. (12) reproduces this split but does not say so.
Resolution: attribute the semi-magic statement to Loly [13] in Corollary 4.13 and in the contributions list ("as Loly noted, no diagonal condition is needed"), state the I_x = I_y split with attribution, and remove "and holds for all semi-magic squares" as a claim of this paper. The author's own "What is and is not new" paragraph is otherwise exemplary and should be kept.

**3. [MAJOR] Rogers–Loly (2005), the multipole expansion of a magic cube, is the direct precedent for Theorem 4.11(a) and is not cited.**
Location: Section 4.3 ("The moment hierarchy"), Theorem 4.11(a), Remark 4.9(i), reference list.
Problem: Rogers and Loly, "The electric multipole expansion for a magic cube", *Eur. J. Phys.* 26 (2005) 809–813, DOI 10.1088/0143-0807/26/5/013, treat the entries as charges and show that the dipole and quadrupole moments vanish, attributing this to the semi-magic property (equal row/column/pillar sums). Theorem 4.11(a) — "if S is semi-magic then ⟨x^m, f⟩ = ⟨y^m, f⟩ = 0 for every m" — is exactly the statement that every axial multipole moment Σ s_ij x_j^m of the "charge" distribution vanishes, in two dimensions. The change log says the entry `rogers2005rotational` was "unverified and not used"; I have verified the bibliographic data above. (The *Am. J. Phys.* 72(6):786–789, 2004 entry, DOI 10.1119/1.1701845, is correct — I resolved the DOI.)
Resolution: cite Rogers–Loly 2005 at the start of Section 4.3 and in Theorem 4.11(a), and state in one sentence how the hierarchy relates to the Cartesian multipole expansion: the hierarchy uses only the *pure* powers x^m, y^m; the *mixed* moments Σ s_ij x_j^a y_i^b (a, b ≥ 1), beginning with the product of inertia / off-diagonal quadrupole Σ s_ij x_j y_i, are not determined by the line sums and vary across magic squares. That is the honest boundary between what Loly–Rogers already knew and what Theorem 4.11(b),(c) adds (the converse degree threshold n−1, and Proposition 4.14 for permutations), which is genuinely new and nice.

**4. [MINOR] Reference [5] is miscast.** Intro: "physical invariants such as the moment of inertia [13, 19, 5]" and Section 2.4 "Loly's physical invariants [13, 19, 14, 5]". Cameron–Rogers–Loly 2013 ("Signatura ... isentropic clans and indexing", *Discuss. Math. Probab. Stat.* 33) is about singular-value signatures and Shannon entropy, not inertia. Cite it under "spectral and matrix properties" with [15–17] or describe it correctly.

### (b) Inertia-tensor statements

**5. [MINOR] Proposition 4.22 is correct but should be reduced to a remark, and the physically meaningful object stated.**
Location: Section 4.5, Section 6.2 ("the three-dimensional inertia tensor of the unit-mass gem is uninformative").
Check: I_xx = N(Var Y + Var Z) = n^2[(n^2−1) + (n^4−1)]/12 = n^2(n^2−1)(n^2+2)/12 and I_zz = n^2(n^2−1)/6 are right; I_xy = 0 and I_xz = −N Cov(X,Z) are right; the unit-mass gem is a symmetric (oblate, I_zz < I_xx = I_yy) top for every arrangement with the degree-1 conditions. The paper's own verdict "uninformative" is correct and I appreciate the candour, but then a full Proposition with proof is more weight than the fact carries.
Resolution: compress to a remark. In its place, give the physicist what the viewpoint *does* buy about Loly's object: with masses s_ij in the plane, the arrangement-dependent part of the planar inertia tensor is the product of inertia I_xy = −Σ s_ij x_j y_i (not fixed by any line sum), while I_xx, I_yy are fixed by the row/column sums. One sentence plus, ideally, the distribution of I_xy over the 880 (it is a free by-product of the geometry run).

**6. [MINOR] The "Loly's planar moment of inertia [13] is a different object" sentence (Section 2.2) is good, but the paper should also say in which sense it is the *same* object:** with Σ the 3 x 3 covariance of the unit-mass gem, N Cov(Z, X^2+Y^2) is the only new ingredient; the planar value-weighted I is an affine function of the degree-2 cross-moment of the gem. This is already (12); just connect the two paragraphs.

### (c) Statistical language and the limit law

**7. [MAJOR] Internal inconsistency in the diagonal correlation.** Section 5.4, paragraph "Why random sampling never found a counterexample": "the two diagonals have correlation −1/(n^2−1) for even n". Corollary 5.3's proof says "corr(T_Dm, T_Da) = −1/(n−1)". The Wald–Wolfowitz identity gives Cov ∝ Σ_c (D_m − 1/n)(D_a − 1/n) = −1 and Var ∝ n−1, so the correlation is −1/(n−1) (= −1/3 for n = 4); `gaussian_estimates.json` indeed has the 4 x 4 covariance block [[68, −22.67],[−22.67, 68]], i.e. −1/3. The Gaussian estimates in the paper are therefore computed correctly; only the printed formula in 5.4 is wrong (and `lowmode_n4/SUMMARY.md` carries the same slip, "−1/(N−1)"). Fix the text.

**8. [MINOR] Terminology is used precisely, with two small exceptions.** "Cov" is defined once as the empirical covariance over the N cells under the uniform measure (Section 4.1) and used consistently; "random arrangement" = uniformly random permutation (5.2); "orthogonality" is w.r.t. (5). Good. **9. [MINOR] Corollary 5.3 is stated correctly and the Monte Carlo is reported honestly.** I checked: E_low/((n^4−1)/144) → χ^2_2 and E[E_low] ~ n^4/72 = 2·(n^4/144), so E_low/E[E_low] → Exp(1) is right; the Hoeffding ratio for a product array is N^{1−r/2} κ_r(a) κ_r(z), and for a Bernoulli(1/n) indicator κ_r = O(n^{r/2−1}) gives O(N^{1/2−r/4}) → 0, as stated. ### (h) Value for the physics-of-magic-squares community and the single most useful addition

**19. [MAJOR, constructive] What the paper delivers, and one cheap addition that would make it directly useful to the Loly school.**
Delivered: Theorem 4.11(b),(c) and Proposition 4.14 settle, for the first time as far as I know, *how many* axial moments one must impose before they are equivalent to the line sums (n−1 for real heights; the permutation refinement k*(n) with the n ≥ 14 phenomenon is a genuinely new and pretty fact); the exact random-arrangement moments; the swap formula.
The addition: **the null distribution of Loly's invariant.** Proposition 5.1 gives it for free. Since E[Cov(Z, X^2+Y^2)] = 0 over a uniformly random arrangement, eq. (12) says that **the expected moment of inertia of a random arrangement is exactly Loly's value n^2(n^4−1)/12 for every n**, and Var(I) = N^2 σ_n^2 Var_grid(x^2+y^2). I verified both at n = 3 by enumeration: mean I = 60.000 over all 9!, Var I = 30 (sd 5.48), 29 distinct values from 46 to 74. So magic and semi-magic squares are not extreme in I — they sit exactly at the population mean, and the "invariance" is the statement that the fluctuation vanishes, not that the value is special. This is a one-paragraph corollary (closed form for Var(I) in n is elementary), it answers the question any physicist asks first ("how unusual is 60?"), and it is the kind of statement the Loly–Rogers–Cameron programme can use immediately. I would put it next to Corollary 4.13 and mention it in the abstract in place of the "degree-two case" phrase.

## 4. Confidential comments to the editor

This is the first version of this paper in which every number I checked is right and every withdrawn claim is named; the author has done the work the previous reviewers asked for, and the moment hierarchy with its permutation threshold is a real, if modest, result. My remaining concerns are about how the physics prior work is represented: the "degree-two case" phrase overstates the correspondence with Loly's invariant (by a factor of 336 at n = 3), the semi-magic remark is Loly's, and the Rogers–Loly multipole paper — the closest precedent for Theorem 4.11(a) — is uncited despite being in the author's own change log as "unverified". None of this is malicious; it is a mathematician's reading of a physics literature. One round should suffice. The paper will be of interest to the recreational-mathematics and physics-of-magic-squares communities rather than to a general mathematics readership; its length should come down to about 20 pages.
