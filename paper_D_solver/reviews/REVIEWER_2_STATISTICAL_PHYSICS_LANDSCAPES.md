## 1. General Assessment & Summary of Contributions

This is an original, deeply physical, and mathematically lucid manuscript that bridges recreational combinatorics, discrete statistical mechanics, and large-scale combinatorial optimization. Whereas Papers I–III of the *Magic Gems* series investigated the static polyhedral geometry, moment-of-inertia tensors, and rigid-body rotational mechanics of already-completed magic squares, Paper D poses the natural inverse problem in statistical physics: *what is the global topography of the potential energy surface (PES) defined by the quadratic line-sum Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(S) = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ on the symmetric group $S_{n^2}$, why do naive continuous-particle and single-spin-exchange dynamics vitrify into frustrated states, and what collective multi-body operators restore ergodicity up to $n = 100$ ($10{,}000$ lattice sites)?*

From the perspective of energy-landscape theory (Wales 2003), conserved-order-parameter lattice dynamics (Kawasaki 1966), and frustrated plaquette systems (Pauling 1935; Bramwell & Gingras 2001; Moüssner & Chalker 1998), five contributions stand out:

1. **Zero-Mean Spectral Rationalization and Maxwell–Calladine Counting (Section 2):** By restricting the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals operator $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ to the zero-mean permutation hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$, Proposition 2.1 proves that the irrational ambient eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm(n)$ vanish, collapsing the spectrum into exact integers $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-2)^{[1]}, n^{[2n-1]}\}$ (even $n$) and $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-1)^{[2]}, n^{[2n-2]}\}$ (odd $n$) with universal mean eigenvalue $\bar{\mu}(\widetilde{L}_n) = 2$ and random-shuffle expectation $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(S)] = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$. Remark 2.2 ("the impostor square $S_{\mathrm{low}}$" at $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}=1{,}584$) and the Maxwell–Calladine nullspace-to-constraint ratio $\eta_{\mathrm{MC}}(n) = (n^2-2n-1)/(2n)$ provide an immediately intuitive physical explanation of why macroscopic rigid-body balance ($4$ modes) leaves $2n-4$ higher-degree polynomial modes unconstrained.
2. **Ground-State Degeneracy Splitting via Multiscale Window Entropies and Concentric Shell Resonance (Section 3):** Inside the degenerate ground-state manifold $\ker(\widetilde{L}_n)$, Lemma 3.1 proves the equivalence of interior and toroidal $2\times 2$ quartet balance on even-order squares (uniquely isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group I at $n=4$), while Proposition 3.2 establishes the Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$, explaining the $56 = 48 + 8$ octupole zeros across the $880$ Frénicle squares via a Diophantine $(-3k, 8k)$ shell cancellation in Groups IX and X.
3. **Exhaustive $S_9$ Disconnectivity Topology and the Kawasaki Recoil Barrier (Section 4):** Lemma 4.1 decomposes each elementary $2$-exchange transposition $\tau_{ab}$ into a linear stress-gradient drive $-2d(g_a - g_b)$ opposed by a quadratic Kawasaki self-energy recoil $c_{ab}d^2 \ge 8$. Theorem 4.2 maps all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$, proving that $74.53\%$ quench to the $8$ Lo Shu ground states while $25.47\%$ ($92{,}438$ states) vitrify into a single dihedral trap orbit $D_4 \cdot S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} = 8$ that blocks all $36$ pair swaps ($\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \ge +4$) and all $168$ directed $3$-cycles ($\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \ge +8$), but is unlocked across a $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}^\ddagger = 12$ intermediate saddle by a unique skew intra-row $(2,2)$ double exchange $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$.
4. **The "Border-Parking" Pathology of Soft Continuous Relaxations vs. Discrete Plaquette Relief (Section 5):** Using the interactive `bubble-trinity.html` simulator, the author demonstrates that continuous 2D Gaussian particles lower their kernel-induced energy $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}}(t)$ by $37.5\%$ ($1{,}681.89 \to 1{,}050.55$) yet yield $0.0\%$ magic squares under both greedy and exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian bipartite projection because positive and negative amplitudes co-localize on cell boundaries. By contrast, discrete Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search (ME-ILS) achieves $100\%$ convergence across $n=3\dots 10$, exposing a frustration maximum at $n=5$ ($8.03$ restarts) followed by topological relief as the diagonal-free $(2,2)$ plaquette fraction $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ approaches unity.
5. **The $n = 6\dots 50$ Nullspace-Relief Plateau and Exact $O(n^3)$ Bilinear Plaquette Acceleration to $n = 100$ (Section 6):** Extending the census across all $48$ consecutive orders $n=3\dots 50$, the manuscript shows that per-restart $2$-exchange descent steps grow strictly linearly ($d_{\mathrm{run}}(n) = 3.52\,n + 20.86$, $r = 0.9934$) while basin-hopping restarts remain bounded on an order-invariant plateau (median $4.32$). Theorem 6.1 (Active-Set Pruning, the Bilinear Plaquette Identity $\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_1, p_2) = \Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_1) + \Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_2) + 2\,\boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_2)$, and the Consecutive-Value Candidate Bound) reduces $O(n^5)$ plaquette loops to a rank-$(n+2)$ BLAS matrix multiply, achieving $O(n^{2.93})$ per-restart scaling and a $34.7\times\text{--}50.2\times$ speedup at $n=46, 48, 100$, while illuminating why defective-line CBLS freezes in endgame states ($\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \in [24, 56]$) due to missing zero-residual relay swaps on balanced lines.

### M2.1. Continuous-to-Discrete Relaxation Physics in the *Bubble Trinity*: Formalizing "Border-Parking" via Convex vs. Concave Relaxation on the Birkhoff Polytope $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$
In **Section 5.1** (`bubble_trinity.tex`, lines 8–53, Eq. 10) and **Section 7** (line 72), the manuscript gives a vivid physical explanation of why the continuous 2D bubble dynamics in `bubble-trinity.html` lowers the kernel-induced energy $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}}(t) = \|A_n W(t)^\top \mathbf{z}\|_2^2$ by $37.5\%$ in Preset IV (*Centrifugal Balance*, $1{,}681.89 \to 1{,}050.55$) while failing completely ($0.0\%$ recovery, Hungarian discrete energy stuck at $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} = 2{,}336$) when projected onto $S_{16}$. This "border-parking" or amplitude-averaging phenomenon is actually a foundational pathology in continuous relaxations of discrete quadratic assignment problems (QAP) and colloidal self-assembly, and the theoretical physics/optimization community will benefit enormously if Section 5.1 states the exact matrix-convexity mechanism up front (rather than saving a brief mention of the Birkhoff polytope for the last sentence of the Discussion):
1. **Column-Stochastic vs. Doubly Stochastic Relaxation:** In Eq. (10), the normalized Gaussian kernel matrix $W_{ab}(t) = \exp(-2\|\mathbf{r}_a(t) - \mathbf{r}_b^{(0)}\|_2^2) / \sum_{a'} \exp(-2\|\mathbf{r}_{a'}(t) - \mathbf{r}_b^{(0)}\|_2^2)$ is *column-stochastic* ($W^\top \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$, so each grid cell $b$ computes a convex combination of nearby bubble heights), but it is **not row-stochastic** ($W \mathbf{1} \ne \mathbf{1}$) when multiple bubbles cluster near the same cell border or leave a cell vacant. Even if one enforced double stochasticity so that $W(t) \in \mathcal{B}_{n^2} = \operatorname{conv}(S_{n^2})$ lies in the Birkhoff polytope of doubly stochastic matrices, the induced objective $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}}(W) = \mathbf{z}^\top W L_n W^\top \mathbf{z}$ is a **convex** quadratic form on $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ because $L_n = A_n^\top A_n \succeq 0$ is positive semidefinite.
2. **Interior Minima vs. Extreme-Point Permutations:** By Bauer's Maximum Principle, a *concave* function over a compact convex polytope $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ attains its global minimum at the extreme points (the permutation matrices $P \in S_{n^2}$, by the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem), whereas a *convex* function $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}}(W) = \|A_n W^\top \mathbf{z}\|_2^2$ attains its global minimum $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}} = 0$ at the **barycenter** $W_{\mathrm{bary}} = \frac{1}{n^2}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ in the deep interior of $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ (since $W_{\mathrm{bary}}^\top \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{0} \in \ker(A_n)$)!
3. **Actionable Revision:** Please add a brief remark or paragraph in Section 5.1 explicitly connecting Eq. (10) to the Birkhoff polytope $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ and Bauer's Maximum Principle: explain that because $L_n \succeq 0$, the continuous functional $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ind}}(W)$ is convex on $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$, so gradient flow naturally pulls the weight matrix $W(t)$ *inward* toward the zero-amplitude barycenter $\widetilde{\mathbf{z}} \to \mathbf{0}$ (manifesting physically in $\mathbb{R}^2$ as opposite-sign bubbles $+Z_a$ and $-Z_b$ parking side-by-side on cell boundaries to cancel their Gaussian tails, opposed only by finite core repulsion $k_r$ and lattice well springs $k_w$), whereas forcing convergence to true permutation vertices $P \in S_{n^2}$ requires a **concave** (negative-definite shift) continuation $\mathcal{H}_\gamma(P) = \mathbf{z}^\top P L_n P^\top \mathbf{z} - \gamma \|P\|_F^2$ with $\gamma > \lambda_{\max}(\widetilde{L}_n) = n$. Framing Section 5.1 in this exact mathematical language will make the *Bubble Trinity* section a heavily cited reference across both soft-matter self-assembly and combinatorial optimization.

### M2.2. Precision in Energy-Landscape Terminology: Distinguishing Canonical Metropolis Basin-Hopping (Wales & Doye 1997) from Deterministic Threshold-Leashed ILS
In **Section 5.2** (`bubble_trinity.tex`, lines 61, 79–80) and **Section 6**, the manuscript refers to Tier 5 as *"Thresholded Funnel Re-Anchoring ($\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \le \mathcal{H}_{\min} + 24 s_{\mathrm{fac}}(n)$)"* and Tier 6 as *"Multi-Restart Basin-Hopping (ME-ILS) [Wales & Doye 1997; Wales 2003]"*. As someone deeply invested in the energy-landscape literature, I appreciate the connection to funnel topography and basin-hopping, but the manuscript should explicitly clarify the dynamical distinction between three related global-optimization paradigms on rugged potential energy surfaces:
1. **Canonical Basin-Hopping (Wales & Doye, *J. Phys. Chem. A* 1997):** Transforms the potential energy surface $\mathcal{H}(S)$ into a step-function landscape $\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(S) = \min_{\text{quench}}\mathcal{H}(S)$ by pairing each structural perturbation ("kick") with local energy minimization, and accepts or rejects the new local minimum $S_{\mathrm{new}}^*$ relative to the current minimum $S_{\mathrm{curr}}^*$ via a **canonical Metropolis criterion** $\min\bigl(1, \exp[-(\mathcal{H}(S_{\mathrm{new}}^*) - \mathcal{H}(S_{\mathrm{curr}}^*)) / k_B T_{\mathrm{BH}}]\bigr)$ along a Markov chain.
2. **Threshold Accepting / Tsallis Generalized Walks (Dueck & Scheuer 1990):** Accepts any post-quench local minimum whose energy lies within a deterministic ceiling $\mathcal{H}(S_{\mathrm{new}}^*) \le \mathcal{H}(S_{\mathrm{curr}}^*) + \Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{thresh}}$, moving to $S_{\mathrm{new}}^*$ as the new anchor if accepted.
3. **The Present "Funnel Re-Anchoring Leash" (Tier 5) + Uniform Multi-Start (Tier 6):** As described in Section 5.2 (line 61), within a single restart ($18$ kicks), the solver tracks the deepest local minimum $S_{\min}$ encountered so far in that funnel and **snaps back** to $S_{\min}$ whenever a $2$-swap kick plus quench lands above $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(S_{\min}) + 24 s_{\mathrm{fac}}(n)$, while Tier 6 performs independent uniform random restarts ($\le 80$ restarts) whenever an $18$-kick funnel walk fails to hit $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}=0$.
4. **Actionable Revision:** Please clarify in Section 5.2 that Tier 5 is a *zero-temperature threshold-leashed basin-hopping walk* on the quenched landscape $\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{line}}(S)$ (with deterministic hard-wall cutoff $\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{leash}} = 24 s_{\mathrm{fac}}(n) \approx \frac{3}{2}n^2$, which scales naturally with the mean squared height difference $\mathbb{E}[d^2] = \frac{2}{3}(n^4-1)$ of a single kick), whereas Tier 6 is a *multi-start iterated local search (ILS)* over independent attraction basins rather than a single continuous Metropolis Markov chain across basins. You should also briefly explain *why* hard-wall re-anchoring to $S_{\min}$ outperforms a canonical Metropolis walk at $n \ge 5$: on $S_{n^2}$, the density of states $\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}})$ grows super-exponentially with energy (as shown in Fig. 4a), creating an overwhelming configurational entropy gradient $\partial S_{\mathrm{conf}}/\partial \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \gg 0$ that pulls finite-temperature Markov chains upward out of narrow ground-state funnels!

### M2.3. Algebraic Clarification of Rectangular Plaquettes $2\binom{n}{2}^2$ vs. General Line-Preserving $(2,2)$ Double Swaps $2\binom{n}{2}^3$ at $n=3$
In **Section 4.2** (`swap_landscape.tex`, line 63), **Table 5** (`tab:s9_s16_landscape`, line 101), and **`results_paper_d.json`** (`multi_order_upgraded.n3.num_lp22_moves`, line 10621), there is a subtle but important combinatorial discrepancy at $n = 3$ that must be resolved:
- In **Table 5** (line 101), the row labeled `"Line-preserving (2,2) double-swap neighborhood"` writes the formula $2\binom{n}{2}^3$ and lists the values **$18$** for $n=3$ and **$432$** for $n=4$. Similarly, line 63 of `swap_landscape.tex` refers to *"the $18$ line-preserving $(2,2)$ double swaps"* on $S_9$.
- However, evaluating the formula $2\binom{n}{2}^3$ at $n = 3$ gives $2 \times 3^3 = \mathbf{54}$ (as correctly recorded in `results_paper_d.json` line 10621: `"num_lp22_moves": 54`), whereas at $n = 4$ it gives $2 \times 6^3 = \mathbf{432}$!
- Why is there a factor of $3$ difference ($18$ vs. $54$) at $n=3$? Because on an $n\times n$ grid, there are two distinct subsets of line-preserving $(2,2)$ double transpositions:
  1. **General intra-row / intra-column $(2,2)$ double swaps:** Choose a pair of rows $\{r_1, r_2\}$ in $\binom{n}{2}$ ways, a pair of columns $\{c_1, c_2\}$ in row $r_1$ in $\binom{n}{2}$ ways, and an *independent* pair of columns $\{c_3, c_4\}$ in row $r_2$ in $\binom{n}{2}$ ways (plus the transpose for columns), giving $2\binom{n}{2}^3 = 54$ moves at $n=3$ and $432$ moves at $n=4$.
  2. **Rectangular $2\times 2$ plaquette swaps (spin-ice loops):** Require $\{c_1, c_2\} = \{c_3, c_4\}$ so that the four cells form an aligned $2\times 2$ rectangle $(r_1, r_2)\times(c_1, c_2)$, giving $2\binom{n}{2}^2 = 18$ moves at $n=3$ and $72$ moves at $n=4$.
- Crucially, look at the unique escaping move on $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ proved in **Theorem 4.2** (Eq. 43 & 57): $(6\leftrightarrow 7)$ swaps columns $(2,3)$ in Row 1, while $(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ swaps columns $(1,2)$ in Row 3! Thus $\{c_1, c_2\} = \{2,3\} \ne \{1,2\} = \{c_3, c_4\}$: the unique unlocking move on $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ is a **skew (three-column) intra-row $(2,2)$ move** belonging to the $2\binom{n}{2}^3 = 54$ general line-preserving neighborhood (or the $2\binom{3}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 36$ non-rectangular skew subset), **not** the $2\binom{3}{2}^2 = 18$ rectangular $2\times 2$ plaquette neighborhood! (Indeed, in a $3\times 3$ grid, an aligned rectangular swap in $\{c_1, c_3\}$ cannot transfer net sum between $c_1$ and $c_3$ while preserving row sums unless it uses column $c_2$ as a relay pivot!)
- **Actionable Revision:** Change $18$ to $54$ in Section 4.2 (`swap_landscape.tex` line 63) and Table 5 (`tab:s9_s16_landscape` line 101) so that it matches the formula $2\binom{n}{2}^3 = 54$ and `results_paper_d.json` (`num_lp22_moves = 54`), and explicitly point out in the text following Theorem 4.2 that the unique $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ escape is a *skew three-column intra-row $(2,2)$ exchange* using Column 2 as a zero-net-change pivot to transfer $\pm 1$ between Columns 1 and 3 while cancelling the anti-diagonal perturbation! This directly foreshadows the "zero-residual relay swap" mechanism discovered at $n=46, 100$ in Section 6.3.
