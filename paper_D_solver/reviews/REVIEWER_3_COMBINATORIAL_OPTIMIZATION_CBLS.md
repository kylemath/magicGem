## 1. General Assessment & Summary of Contributions

This manuscript investigates the combinatorial optimization landscape of the quadratic line-sum Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(S) = \|A_n \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ over the symmetric group $S_{n^2}$ from $n=3$ up to $n=100$ ($N = 10{,}000$ cells). While deterministic $O(n^2)$ constructions for magic squares (Siamese, doubly-even masks, LUX) have been known for centuries, finding magic squares from uniform random permutations via local search is a notorious benchmark in the Constraint-Based Local Search (CBLS) and metaheuristics literature (e.g., Codognet & Diaz, *ICTAI* 2001; Van Hentenryck & Michel, *Constraint-Based Local Search*, MIT Press 2005).

From the perspective of combinatorial optimization and operations research, the manuscript makes three genuinely impressive contributions:
1. **Complete Landscape & Nullspace-Relief Characterization ($n=3\dots 50$):** By exhaustively mapping $S_9$ ($362{,}880$ states) and conducting a continuous order-by-order scaling census across all $48$ orders $n=3\dots 50$, the author demonstrates that the $8$ antipodal traps $D_4 \cdot S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $n=3$ are unlocked by a unique row-preserving $(2,2)$ double swap (a $4$-cell Kempe chain), and proves that basin-hopping restarts peak at $n=5$ ($8.03$ mean restarts) before settling onto an order-invariant *Nullspace Relief Plateau* ($n=6\dots 50$, median $4.32$ restarts) as the diagonal-free $(2,2)$ plaquette subspace fraction $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ approaches $1$, while per-restart steepest-descent steps scale strictly linearly as $d_{\mathrm{run}}(n) = 3.52\,n + 20.86$ ($r = 0.9934$).
2. **Exact Algebraic Pruning & The Bilinear Plaquette Identity (Theorem 6.1):** To break the $O(n^{5.92})$ empirical runtime scaling of brute-force all-pairs $2$-exchange descent and $O(n^5)$ nested $(2,2)$ plaquette simulation, the author establishes three zero-approximation algebraic identities: (i) *Active-Set Steepest-Descent Pruning* restricting endgame pair evaluations to cells with $g_a(S) \ne 0$, (ii) the *Bilinear Plaquette Identity* $\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_1, p_2) = \Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_1) + \Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_2) + 2\,\boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_2)$, which reduces $K^2$ pairwise $(2,2)$ plaquette evaluations to a single rank-$(n+2)$ BLAS matrix multiplication $2 X X^\top$, and (iii) an *Exact $O(n^2)$ Consecutive-Value Candidate Bound*. This lowers empirical per-restart runtime scaling to $O(n^{2.93})$ and solves $n=100$ ($10{,}000$ cells) in $34.06\text{ s}$ minimum / $147.70\text{ s}$ mean.
3. **Structural Diagnosis of Endgame Stagnation ("Zero-Residual Relay Swaps"):** In Section 6.3, the author provides a crystal-clear mathematical explanation for why defective-line heuristics stall in the deep endgame ($\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \in [24, 56]$): eliminating the final $\pm 2$ line residuals frequently requires an intra-row transposition $p_1$ inside an *already balanced row* ($R_{r_1}=0$, $|Z_{a_1}-Z_{b_1}|=2$, $\Delta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}}(p_1)=+8$) to create a temporary column dipole that a conjugate swap $p_2$ in a defective row can annihilate.

While I find the Bilinear Plaquette Identity and the zero-residual relay swap insight mathematically sound and algorithmically compelling, several important methodological, reproducibility, and presentation issues must be addressed before publication.

### M3.2. Fair Characterization of "Defective-Line CBLS" vs. Classical Adaptive Search (Codognet & Diaz 2001; Van Hentenryck & Michel 2005)
In `sections/large_scale_competition.tex` (lines 102, 124, 129, 134, 144), the author names the second competing baseline *"Defective-Line Constraint-Based Local Search (CBLS) \cite{codognet2001yet,vanhentenryck2005constraint}"* and describes it as restricting $2$-exchange descent to cells on the most defective lines ($R_L \ne 0$) and truncating intra-line $(2,2)$ checks to the top-$25$ lowest-barrier candidates across $80$ restarts $\times 18$ kicks.
As someone who has worked extensively on CBLS and Adaptive Search for magic squares, I must push back on conflating a *strictly defective-line-restricted multi-exchange variant* with the full CBLS / Adaptive Search framework of Codognet & Diaz (2001) and Van Hentenryck & Michel (2005):
1. **Variable Selection vs. Partner Selection in Adaptive Search:** In Codognet & Diaz's Adaptive Search for magic squares, variable error is computed by aggregating line errors $|R_{r_i}| + |R_{c_j}| + |R_{d}|$, and the solver selects the *most defective variable* $a \in \mathcal{A}(S)$, but evaluates swaps $(a, b)$ across **all** cells $b \in \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ on the board (Notice that by Theorem 6.1 Claim 1, evaluating $\mathcal{A}(S) \times \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ is actually *exact* for $2$-exchange steepest descent!). What traps the author's "Defective-Line CBLS" baseline at $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \in [24, 56]$ is specifically that at $2$-exchange local minima, it restricts $(2,2)$ plaquette candidates to *defective lines* and *top-25 truncation*, thereby missing the **zero-residual relay swaps** where $p_1$ lies entirely inside a non-defective row ($R_{r_1}=0$, so neither endpoint of $p_1$ is in a defective row!).
2. **Tabu Tenure & Partial Reset Diversification:** Classical Adaptive Search does not rely on $(2,2)$ plaquette moves; instead, when trapped in a $2$-exchange local minimum, it uses short-term variable Tabu lists / mark-based penalties and random multi-swap resets to escape. Of course, in the deep endgame at $n=100$ ($\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{line}} \le 56$), random resets destroy the balanced lines and wander out of the narrow funnel (just as unanchored kicks do in Tier 4 of Table 2), which is why classical Adaptive Search also struggles at $n=100$ without tuned variable-neighborhood moves.
**Actionable Fix:** Please clarify in Section 6.3 that the tested baseline is a *Defective-Line-Restricted CBLS heuristic* designed to test whether restricting multi-exchange candidate generation to defective lines ($R_L \ne 0$) suffices at large $n$, and explicitly contrast this with both (a) Theorem 6.1(1)'s exact active-set condition $(a,b) \in \mathcal{A}(S)\times \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ (where only *one* endpoint of a $2$-swap need be active) and (b) why $p_1$ in a $(2,2)$ relay plaquette often has *neither* endpoint on a defective row ($R_{r_1}=0$).

### M3.3. Disentangling Algorithmic Complexity ($O(n^4)$ vs. $O(n^3)$ Operations) from Python Interpreter vs. C/BLAS Vectorization Speedup
In `sections/large_scale_competition.tex` (lines 57, 98, 126, 131, 136, 146) and Table 4 (`tab:head_to_head_competition`), the author compares `Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS` against `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS`. Let us examine the numbers at $n=46$ and $n=48$ carefully:
- At $n=46$: Operator evaluations drop by **$18.1\times$** ($18.48\times 10^9 \to 1.02\times 10^9$), even though mean restarts slightly increased ($15.67 \to 19.33$) and mean descents increased ($3{,}071.0 \to 3{,}737.7$). Thus, **evaluations per descent step** dropped by $(18.48/1.02) \times (3737.7/3071.0) = \mathbf{22.0\times}$. Meanwhile, wall-clock time dropped by **$34.7\times$** ($386.13\text{ s} \to 11.12\text{ s}$), which corresponds to a **$42.8\times$ speedup per restart** ($23.16\text{ s/run} \to 0.547\text{ s/run}$).
- At $n=48$: Operator evaluations drop by **$18.8\times$** ($13.79\times 10^9 \to 0.733\times 10^9$), while wall-clock time drops by **$35.0\times$** ($280.95\text{ s} \to 8.02\text{ s}$), or **$41.5\times$ per restart** ($26.33\text{ s/run} \to 0.633\text{ s/run}$).
- Across $n=15\dots 50$, `Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS` scales empirically as $t_{\mathrm{all}}(n) \propto n^{5.92}$, whereas `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS` scales per restart as $t_{\mathrm{run}}(n) \propto n^{2.93}$.
As an algorithmic experimentalist, I appreciate that the manuscript reports **both** machine-independent *Mean Operator Evaluations* (Panel b of Figure 9 and Column 7 of Table 4) and *Wall-Clock Time* (Panel a of Figure 9 and Column 8 of Table 4). However, the text should explicitly decompose the $35\times\text{--}50\times$ wall-clock speedup into its two distinct sources:
1. **Exact Algorithmic Pruning ($\sim 18\text{--}22\times$ reduction in candidate evaluations at $n=46, 48, 100$):** reducing per-step steepest-descent work from $\binom{n^2}{2} = \Theta(n^4)$ to $|\mathcal{A}(S)|n^2 = O(k n^3)$ and pruning the $O(n^5)$ intra-line $(2,2)$ search down to $K^2$ pairs ($K \le 800$).
2. **Hardware / Level-3 BLAS Vectorization ($\sim 2\times$ additional throughput per evaluated candidate):** replacing sequential nested swap-and-undo simulation loops with a contiguous rank-$(n+2)$ matrix multiplication $2 X X^\top$ (Equation 81), which exploits SIMD cache locality and Level-3 BLAS (`SGEMM`/`DGEMM`). Explaining both factors will preempt any referee criticism about scalar vs. vectorized loops.

### M3.4. Why Do `Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS` and `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS` Take Different Trajectory Paths on the Same Seed?
Look closely at Table 4 (`tab:head_to_head_competition` in `sections/large_scale_competition.tex`, lines 125–126 and 130–131):
- Both solvers are initialized with the exact same three uniform random permutations (`seeds = 20000, 20001, 20002`).
- Yet at $n=46$, `Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS` takes `[7, 24, 16]` restarts (mean $15.67$), whereas `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS` takes `[32, 9, 17]` restarts (mean $19.33$).
- At $n=48$, `Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS` takes `[1, 27, 1]` restarts (mean $9.67$), whereas `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS` takes `[11, 10, 14]` restarts (mean $11.67$).
If Theorem 6.1 provides *"zero mathematical approximation to the local-minimum and $(2,2)$ plaquette definitions"* (`large_scale_competition.tex`, line 59), a careful reader will immediately ask: **Why do the two solvers not follow the exact same deterministic sequence of descent steps and restarts on `seed = 20000`?**
The answer is clear once one reads line 98 of `large_scale_competition.tex`:
1. During the initial high-energy waterfall, `Bilinear + Active-Set ME-ILS` uses a *two-stage active-set waterfall* ("scanning the top $2n$ highest-$|g_a|$ cells during the initial high-energy waterfall before verifying the full active set $\mathcal{A}(S)$ to certify an exact $2$-exchange local minimum"). Taking the steepest swap among the top-$2n$ highest-stress cells during early descent steps follows a different steepest-descent trajectory into the funnel than scanning all $\binom{n^2}{2}$ pairs at every early step, even though **at the bottom of the waterfall** it verifies the full active set $\mathcal{A}(S)$ so that every terminal state is a certified $100\%$ exact $2$-exchange local minimum!
2. Furthermore, at $2$-exchange local minima, capping the intra-row/intra-column shift matrix at $K \le 800$ candidates (or breaking ties differently in matrix order vs. row-by-row order) alters which $(2,2)$ kick is taken.
**Actionable Fix:** State this explicitly in Section 6.2/6.3! Point out that "zero mathematical approximation" applies to the *certification of $2$-exchange local minima* (Claim 1), the *exact algebraic evaluation of any $(2,2)$ plaquette energy* (Claim 2), and the *membership of the top-$40$ general transpositions at endgame local minima* (Claim 3), while the two-stage top-$2n$ waterfall pivot rule and tie-breaking order naturally guide the solver into different (statistically equivalent) local minima along the Nullspace Relief Plateau, explaining why the per-seed restart counts `[32, 9, 17]` vs. `[7, 24, 16]` differ while sharing the same geometric distribution.
