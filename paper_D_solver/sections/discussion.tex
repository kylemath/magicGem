\section{Discussion and Invitation to Explore}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across the four papers of the \emph{Magic Gems} series \cite{mathewson2026gems,mathewson2026moments,mathewson2026mechanics}, a single guiding question has united recreational mathematics, three-dimensional polyhedral geometry, classical rigid-body mechanics, and combinatorial optimization: what happens when we treat the numbers of a magic square as physical matter in space? Whereas Papers~I--III established how the magic line-sum constraints $\Hline(S) = 0$ sculpt the polyhedral hull $K(S)$ (Paper~I), diagonalize the inertia tensor so that every magic square spins smoothly as a dynamically symmetric top (Paper~II), and govern tabletop resting stability (Paper~III), the present study stepped into the interactive laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver}) and scaled its operator dynamics up to $n = 100$ ($10{,}000$ cells) to understand how physical forces and cooperative exchanges behave \emph{before} a board becomes magic.

What makes this landscape illuminating for both researchers and students is that the most natural physical and heuristic intuitions fail in ways that directly expose the underlying algebra of $\Hline(S) = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$. Balancing the macroscopic center-of-mass tilt and main diagonals of a square plate is necessary for magicness, but as our impostor square $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ (\Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency}) demonstrates, it constrains only $4$ modes out of $2n$ and leaves $2n-4$ higher-degree polynomial row and column waves completely unchecked ($\Hline(S_{\mathrm{low}}) = 1{,}584$). Restricting the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals line-incidence Gram operator $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ \cite{godsil2001algebraic,brouwer2012spectra,loly2009magic} to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ (\Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization}) collapses all non-zero eigenvalues onto exact integers $\{n-2, n\}$ (even $n$) or $\{n-1, n\}$ (odd $n$) with universal average $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$. Once inside the magic nullspace $\ker(\Ltilde_n)$, the \emph{Energy Decomposition Explorer} (\texttt{energy-decomposition.html}) reveals a second-tier spatial hierarchy (\Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} and \Cref{prop:octupole_census}): vanishing interior $2\times 2$ quartet energy automatically forces toroidal boundary-wrapping quartet balance on any even-order magic square (isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I at $n=4$), while the Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ explains why $8$ non-associative squares in Groups~IX and~X join the $48$ associative squares of Group~III to produce $56$ octupole-zero Gems.

Equally instructive is the contrast between continuous particle drift, defective-line heuristics, and exact bilinear multi-exchange search across \texttt{physics-lab.html}, \texttt{vector-physics.html}, \texttt{bubble-trinity.html}, and our large-scale benchmarks (\Cref{sec:landscape}--\Cref{sec:large_scale}). Allowing bubbles to float continuously in $\R^2$ under a soft Gaussian kernel (\Cref{tab:bubble_regimes}) lowers the continuous energy gauge $\Hind(t)$ by $37.5\%$, yet recovers $0.0\%$ magic squares under both greedy snapping and exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian matching \cite{kuhn1955hungarian,munkres1957algorithms} because the bubbles ``cheat'' by parking on cell borders to blend positive and negative heights. On the discrete grid, single-pair swaps freeze behind the quadratic recoil barrier $c_{ab}d^2$ (\Cref{lem:swap_law}), capturing $25.47\%$ of all $362{,}880$ states of $S_9$ in the $8$ antipodal traps $D_4 \cdot S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps}), where every pair swap and every triplet rotation raises the energy and only a single synchronized $(2,2)$ double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ unlocks the Lo Shu ground state. Combining $2$-exchange descent, whole-row/column glides, line-preserving and general $(2,2)$ plaquette moves, and tethered basin-hopping (\emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search}, \Cref{tab:multi_order_settler} and \Cref{tab:scan_milestone_orders}) achieves $100.0\%$ convergence across all $48$ consecutive orders from $n = 3$ to $n = 50$, showing that combinatorial frustration peaks at $n = 5$ ($8.03$ restarts) before stabilizing across the $n = 6\dots 50$ Nullspace Relief Plateau at a median of $4.32$ restarts ($\text{IQR} = 2.67\text{--}8.67$) as $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2 \to 1$. Finally, our \emph{Active-Set Steepest-Descent Theorem} and \emph{Bilinear Plaquette Identity} $\Delta\Hline(p_1, p_2) = \Delta\Hline(p_1) + \Delta\Hline(p_2) + 2\,\boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_2)$ (\Cref{thm:bilinear_plaquette}) reduce $(2,2)$ plaquette evaluation to a single $(K \times (n+2))$ BLAS matrix multiplication, achieving $O(n^{2.93})$ per-restart scaling and accelerating ground-state synthesis by $34.7\times\text{--}50.2\times$ at $n = 46, 48,$ and $100$ (\Cref{fig:competition_100}), while demonstrating why defective-line CBLS stalls in endgame traps when it discards zero-residual relay swaps on balanced lines.

We invite readers, educators, and students to open the interactive browser laboratory and explore four open mathematical and algorithmic challenges that emerge from our findings. First, on the $4\times 4$ board ($S_{16}$), what is the exact number of strict local minima under $2$-exchange and line-preserving $(2,2)$-exchange moves across all $16! \approx 2.09\times 10^{13}$ permutations, and what is the smallest multi-tile cycle family that guarantees a $100\%$ trap-free single-quench basin on $S_{16}$ just as $(2,2)$ double swaps do on $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps})? Second, how does the zero-mean integer spectrum of \Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization} and the Bilinear Plaquette Identity of \Cref{thm:bilinear_plaquette} extend to three-dimensional magic cubes $S_{n^3}$ \cite{rogers2004inertia,rogers2005multipole} and pandiagonal toroidal Laplacians? Third, can one push random-start magic-square synthesis from $n = 100$ ($10{,}000$ cells) to $n = 1{,}000$ ($10^6$ cells) by replacing the dense $(K \times (n+2))$ BLAS multiplication in \Cref{eq:blas_plaquette_matrix} with an exact $O(1)$ hash table keyed on the sparse column-pair shift $(\delta R_{c_1}, \delta R_{c_2}) = (-d_1, +d_1)$? Fourth, can one redesign the continuous \emph{Bubble Trinity} simulator so that floating particles \emph{cannot} cheat---for instance, by replacing $2\mathrm{D}$ Gaussian kernel blending with a concave quadratic-assignment flow $\min_{P \in \mathcal{B}_{n^2}} \tr(P^\top L_n P \mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^\top) - \gamma \|P\|_F^2$ over the Birkhoff polytope $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ of doubly stochastic matrices that forces the trajectory into true permutation vertices? All interactive simulators, verification scripts, and datasets are openly available for hands-on experimentation.
