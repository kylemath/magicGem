\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across the four papers of the \emph{Magic Gems} series \cite{mathewson2026gems,mathewson2026moments,mathewson2026mechanics}, a unified picture emerges connecting discrete combinatorics, convex geometry, rigid-body mechanics, and statistical-mechanical energy landscapes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{From Forward Invariants to Inverse Landscapes:} Whereas Papers~I--III showed how the magic line-sum constraints $\Hline(S) = 0$ sculpt the convex hull $K(S)$ (Paper~I), diagonalize the inertia tensor via bivariate moment orthogonality (Paper~II), and stabilize rigid-body rotation and tabletop resting equilibria (Paper~III), the present paper demonstrates that the operator $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ governing $\Hline(S) = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ possesses an equally rich spectral and topological structure away from the ground state.
  \item \textbf{Zero-Mean Spectral Rationalization:} The collapse of the irrational eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm(n)$ of $L_n$ into exact integers $\{0, n-2, n\}$ (even $n$) or $\{0, n-1, n\}$ (odd $n$) on $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ (\Cref{thm:spectral_rationalization}) and the universal mean eigenvalue $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$ (\Cref{thm:ensemble_mean}) show that the magic-square constraint operator is intrinsically rational on the hyperplane of centred permutations. At the same time, the Low-Mode Insufficiency Proposition (\Cref{prop:low_mode_insufficiency}) proves that physical moment balance ($\langle X,Z\rangle = \langle Y,Z\rangle = 0$) and diagonal balance ($R_{D_\pm}=0$) leave a $(2n-3)$-dimensional high-frequency subspace unconstrained.
  \item \textbf{Multiscale Sub-Window Hierarchy:} Within the ground-state manifold $\ker(\Ltilde_n)$, our census of sliding-window Shannon entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ and $2\times 2$ quartet energies (\Cref{thm:quartet_equivalence} and \Cref{prop:octupole_census}) establishes a second-tier energy hierarchy that separates Dudeney Group~I panmagic squares ($W_{2\times 2}^{\mathrm{int}} = W_{2\times 2}^{\mathrm{tor}} = 0$) and Group~III associative squares ($B_{\mathrm{comp}} = 0, M_{xyz} = 0$) from generic magic squares, while uncovering the $8$ anomalous non-associative octupole-zero squares in Groups~IX and~X.
  \item \textbf{Topological Frustration, Unique $(2,2)$ Escape, and $(2,2)$-Polished Relaxation:} Exhaustive enumeration of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps}) reveals that even at $n=3$, the discrete permutation constraint creates $8$ strict local minima $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$ that are immune to both $2$-cycles and $3$-cycles and capture $25.47\%$ of all steepest-descent trajectories, yet escape via a \emph{unique} row-preserving $(2,2)$ double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ ($1/378$) that restores the deterministic ground-state basin of $S_9$ to $100.00\%$. At $n=4$ ($S_{16}$), where $81.75\%$ of greedy $2$-cycle descents become trapped and pure 2D spatial snapping collides at cell boundaries, incorporating line-preserving $(2,2)$ double swaps and periodic greedy polish into stress-guided multi-cycle settling raises ground-state synthesis from $24.5\%$ to $98.5\%$ at $B=1{,}500$ steps and $100.0\%$ at $B=2{,}500$ steps (\Cref{fig:bubble_trinity}).
\end{itemize}

Several natural open problems arise from these findings. First, what is the exact number of spurious $2$-cycle, $(2+3)$-cycle, and line-preserving $(2,2)$-cycle local minima across the full $16!$-state space of $S_{16}$, and what minimal cycle structure guarantees a $100\%$ zero-temperature basin on $S_{16}$ as $(2,2)$ double swaps do on $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps})? Second, can the Spectral Rationalization Theorem (\Cref{thm:spectral_rationalization}) be extended to three-dimensional magic cubes $S_{n^3}$ \cite{rogers2004inertia,rogers2005multipole} and toroidal panmagic Laplacians? Third, can a continuous Birkhoff-polytope relaxation of $\mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ over doubly stochastic permutation matrices replace greedy 2D spatial snapping so that continuous trajectories project barrier-free onto $\ker(\Ltilde_n)$ at high orders $n \ge 6$? All interactive simulators, verification scripts, and datasets supporting this work are openly available for further exploration.
