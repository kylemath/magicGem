\section{Discussion and Invitation to Explore}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across the four papers of the \emph{Magic Gems} series \cite{mathewson2026gems,mathewson2026moments,mathewson2026mechanics}, a single guiding question has connected recreational mathematics, three-dimensional geometry, classical mechanics, and interactive computation: \emph{what happens when we treat the numbers of a magic square as physical matter in space?} Whereas Papers~I--III showed how the magic line-sum constraints $\Hline(S) = 0$ sculpt the polyhedral hull $K(S)$ (Paper~I), diagonalize the inertia tensor so that every magic square spins smoothly as a dynamically symmetric top (Paper~II), and govern tabletop resting stability (Paper~III), the present study stepped into the interactive laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver}) to ask how physical forces and local swaps behave \emph{before} a board becomes magic.

What makes this laboratory rich for both researchers and students is that the most natural physical intuitions fail in surprising, mathematically illuminating ways. Balancing the macroscopic center-of-mass tilt and main diagonals of a squareplate is necessary for magicness, but as our impostor square $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ (\Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency}) demonstrates, it leaves $2n-4$ higher-degree polynomial row and column waves completely unchecked ($\Hline(S_{\mathrm{low}}) = 1{,}584$). Restricting the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals Laplacian $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ \cite{godsil2001algebraic,brouwer2012spectra,loly2009magic} to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ (\Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization}) collapses all non-zero eigenvalues onto exact integers $\{n-2, n\}$ (even $n$) or $\{n-1, n\}$ (odd $n$) with universal average $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$. Once inside the magic nullspace $\ker(\Ltilde_n)$, the \emph{Energy Decomposition Explorer} (\texttt{energy-decomposition.html}) reveals a second-tier spatial hierarchy (\Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} and \Cref{prop:octupole_census}): vanishing interior $2\times 2$ quartet energy automatically forces toroidal boundary-wrapping quartet balance on any even-order magic square (isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I at $n=4$), while the Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ explains why $8$ non-associative squares in Groups~IX and~X join the $48$ associative squares of Group~III to produce $56$ octupole-zero Gems.

Even more instructive is the contrast between continuous bubbles and discrete combination moves in \texttt{physics-lab.html}, \texttt{vector-physics.html}, and \texttt{bubble-trinity.html}. Allowing bubbles to float continuously in $\R^2$ under a soft Gaussian kernel (\Cref{tab:bubble_regimes}) lowers the continuous energy gauge $\Hind(t)$ by $37.5\%$, yet recovers $0.0\%$ magic squares under both greedy snapping and exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian matching because the bubbles ``cheat'' by blending positive and negative heights across cell boundaries. Meanwhile, on the discrete grid, single-pair swaps get trapped behind the quadratic recoil barrier $c_{ab}d^2$ (\Cref{lem:swap_law}), and on a $3\times 3$ board (\Cref{thm:s9_traps}) $25.47\%$ of all $362{,}880$ starting states land in the $8$ antipodal traps $D_4 \cdot S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$, where every pair swap and every triplet rotation raises the energy, and only a single synchronized $(2,2)$ double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ unlocks the Lo Shu square. Combining $2$-exchange descent, row/column-preserving $(2,2)$ plaquette moves, candidate-list $(2,2)$ and row/column swaps, and tethered basin-hopping (\emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search}, \Cref{tab:multi_order_settler}) achieves $100.0\%$ convergence across all orders $n = 3\dots 10$, while revealing that $5\times 5$ requires more restarts ($8.03$) than $10\times 10$ ($3.34$) as the diagonal-free fraction of the $(2,2)$ subspace climbs to $f_{\mathrm{null}}(10) = 97.5\%$.

We invite readers, educators, and students to open the interactive browser laboratory and explore several questions that remain wide open. First, on the $4\times 4$ board ($S_{16}$), what is the exact number of strict local minima under $2$-exchange and line-preserving $(2,2)$-exchange moves across all $16! \approx 2.09\times 10^{13}$ permutations, and what is the smallest multi-tile cycle family that guarantees a $100\%$ trap-free basin on $S_{16}$ just as $(2,2)$ double swaps do on $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps})? Second, how does the zero-mean integer spectrum of \Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization} extend to three-dimensional magic cubes $S_{n^3}$ \cite{rogers2004inertia,rogers2005multipole} and toroidal panmagic Laplacians? Third, can one redesign the continuous \emph{Bubble Trinity} simulator so that the bubbles \emph{cannot} cheat---for instance, by replacing 2D Gaussian kernel blending with a concave quadratic-assignment flow $\min_{P \in \mathcal{B}_{n^2}} \tr(P^\top L_n P \mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^\top) - \gamma \|P\|_F^2$ over the Birkhoff polytope $\mathcal{B}_{n^2}$ of doubly stochastic matrices that forces the system into true permutation vertices? All interactive simulators, verification scripts, and datasets are openly available for hands-on experimentation.
