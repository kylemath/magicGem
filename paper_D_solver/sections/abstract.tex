\begin{abstract}
If we attach virtual springs to the rows, columns, and diagonals of a scrambled $n\times n$ grid of numbers $1\dots n^2$---or let the numbers float as buoyant ``bubbles'' in a two-dimensional dish driven by line-sum imbalances---can physical relaxation discover a magic square on its own? Whereas the first three papers of the \emph{Magic Gems} series studied the static geometry (Paper~I), polynomial moment identities (Paper~II), and rigid-body rotational mechanics (Paper~III) of completed magic squares, this paper and its open-source interactive browser laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver}) investigate the inverse dynamical landscape governed by the quadratic line-sum Hamiltonian $\Hline(S) = \sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_n} R_L(S)^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ on the symmetric group $S_{n^2}$. Combining elementary linear algebra with exhaustive computer censuses and interactive simulations, we uncover four surprises across scales. First, we show why balancing a square as a flat 3D plate with zero gravitational torque ($\langle X,Z\rangle = \langle Y,Z\rangle = 0$) and balanced main diagonals ($R_{D_\pm} = 0$) fails to produce a magic square: restricting the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals operator $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ collapses its irrational eigenvalues into exact integers with universal average $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$ and random-shuffle expectation $\mathbb{E}[\Hline(S)] = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$ (\Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization}), but tilt and diagonal balance fix only $4$ modes out of $2n$, leaving $2n-4$ alternating row and column modes unconstrained---vividly illustrated by an explicit $4\times 4$ ``impostor square'' $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ at $\Hline = 1{,}584$ (\Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency}). Second, inside the ground-state manifold $\Hline(S) = 0$ where all $1\mathrm{D}$ line sums are equal, we use sliding $k\times k$ window entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ and a Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ (\Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} and \Cref{prop:octupole_census}) to map all $880$ order-$4$ magic squares, explaining both the $48$ symmetric associative squares and $8$ unexpected non-symmetric squares in Dudeney Groups~IX and~X whose corner and edge shells cancel in an exact $3:8$ coprime resonance. Third, by mapping all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps}), we prove that greedy $2$-swap descent converges to the Lo Shu square from $74.53\%$ of all starting shuffles and gets stuck from the remaining $25.47\%$ in a \emph{single} dihedral trap orbit $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$; no $2$-swap and no $3$-cycle can improve $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$, but \emph{one and only one} of the $378$ disjoint $(2,2)$ double swaps---the row-preserving move $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$, acting like a $4$-cell Kempe chain---unlocks the trap in a single step. Fourth, in our continuous \emph{Bubble Trinity} simulator, we show why soft 2D Gaussian particles lower their field energy by $37.5\%$ yet fail ($0\%$) under both greedy snapping and exact Hungarian bipartite matching by huddling between grid cells to average out heights, whereas a discrete \emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search (ME-ILS)} engine achieves $100.0\%$ synthesis from $n=3$ to $n=10$ (up to $100! \approx 9.33\times 10^{157}$ states) and reveals that $n=5$ is the hardest frustration bottleneck ($8.03$ restarts) before higher orders ($n=6\dots 10$, dropping to $3.34$ restarts at $n=10$) gain ``dimensional relief'' as $1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ of the $(2,2)$ plaquette moves become diagonal-free.
\end{abstract}
