\begin{abstract}
If we attach virtual springs to the rows, columns, and diagonals of a scrambled $n\times n$ grid of numbers $1\dots n^2$---or let the numbers float as buoyant ``bubbles'' in a two-dimensional dish driven by line-sum imbalances---can physical relaxation discover a magic square on its own? Whereas the first three papers of the \emph{Magic Gems} series studied the static polyhedral geometry (Paper~I), polynomial moment identities (Paper~II), and rigid-body rotational mechanics (Paper~III) of completed magic squares, this fourth study and its open-source interactive browser laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver}) investigate the inverse dynamical landscape governed by the quadratic line-sum Hamiltonian $\Hline(S) = \sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_n} R_L(S)^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}$ across the symmetric group $S_{n^2}$ from $n = 3$ up to $n = 100$ ($10{,}000$ cells). Combining exact spectral linear algebra with exhaustive computer censuses and large-scale combinatorial optimization, we establish five principal findings: (1) restricting the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals operator $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ collapses its irrational eigenvalues into exact integers with universal mean $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$ and random-shuffle expectation $\mathbb{E}[\Hline(S)] = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$ (\Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization}), yet balancing $3\mathrm{D}$ plate tilt and main diagonals fixes only $4$ modes out of $2n$, leaving $2n-4$ alternating row and column modes unchecked as demonstrated by an explicit $4\times 4$ ``impostor square'' $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ at $\Hline = 1{,}584$ (\Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency}); (2) inside the ground-state manifold $\Hline(S) = 0$, sliding $k\times k$ window entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ and a Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ (\Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} and \Cref{prop:octupole_census}) classify all $880$ order-$4$ magic squares, explaining both the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I and $8$ anomalous non-associative octupole-zero squares in Groups~IX and~X whose corner and edge shells cancel in a coprime $(-3k, 8k)$ resonance; (3) across all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps}), greedy $2$-exchange descent converges to the Lo Shu square from $74.53\%$ of starting shuffles and freezes from the remaining $25.47\%$ in a single dihedral trap orbit $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$ that resists all $2$-swaps and all $3$-cycles, but is unlocked in one step by a unique row-preserving $(2,2)$ double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$ acting as a $4$-cell Kempe chain; (4) in our continuous \emph{Bubble Trinity} simulator, soft $2\mathrm{D}$ Gaussian particles lower their kernel energy $\Hind(t)$ by $37.5\%$ yet recover $0.0\%$ magic squares under both greedy snapping and exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian bipartite matching by parking on cell borders to blend amplitudes, whereas our discrete \emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search (ME-ILS)} engine achieves $100.0\%$ convergence across all $N = 48$ consecutive orders from $n = 3$ to $n = 50$, revealing a sharp frustration peak at $n = 5$ ($8.03$ restarts) followed by an order-invariant nullspace-relief plateau across $n = 6\dots 50$ (median $4.32$ restarts, $\text{IQR} = 2.67\text{--}8.67$, while per-restart descents scale linearly as $3.52\,n + 20.86$, $r = 0.9934$, $p = 6.39\times 10^{-45}$) as $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ of the $(2,2)$ plaquette subspace becomes diagonal-free; and (5) we prove the \emph{Bilinear Plaquette Identity} $\Delta\Hline(p_1, p_2) = \Delta\Hline(p_1) + \Delta\Hline(p_2) + 2\,\boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_2)$ and \emph{Active-Set Steepest-Descent Theorem} (\Cref{thm:bilinear_plaquette}), which replace $O(n^5)$ plaquette simulation loops with a single $(K \times (n+2))$ BLAS matrix multiplication, achieving $O(n^{2.93})$ per-restart scaling ($r = 0.9924$, $p = 1.08\times 10^{-6}$) and outperforming Baseline All-Pairs ME-ILS by $34.7\times\text{--}50.2\times$ in a head-to-head competition at $n = 46$, $n = 48$, and $n = 100$ ($11.12~\text{s}$, $8.02~\text{s}$, and $147.70~\text{s}$ mean solve time, minimum $34.06~\text{s}$ at $n=100$) while Simulated Annealing ($0\%$) and defective-line Constraint-Based Local Search ($0\%$ at $n=46, 100$) stall in endgame traps.
\end{abstract}
