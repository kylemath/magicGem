\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A magic square of order $n$ is a bijection $S : \{1,\dots,n\}^2 \to \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ whose $n$ rows, $n$ columns, and two main diagonals each sum to the magic constant $M_n = \frac{1}{2}n(n^2+1)$ \cite{frenicle1693,loubere1693,dudeney1917amusements,andrews1960magic}. When lifted into three-dimensional Euclidean space via centred integer coordinates
\begin{equation}
  X_j = 2j - (n+1), \qquad Y_i = (n+1) - 2i, \qquad Z_{ij} = 2s_{ij} - (n^2+1),
  \label{eq:centred_coords}
\end{equation}
each permutation $S \in S_{n^2}$ defines an $n^2$-point configuration $\mathcal{P}(S) = \{(X_j, Y_i, Z_{ij})\} \subset \Z^3$ whose convex hull $K(S) = \conv\mathcal{P}(S)$ is a \emph{Magic Gem}. Across the first three papers of this series, we investigated the forward map from magic squares to geometric and mechanical invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paper~I (\emph{Polytopal Geometry})} \cite{mathewson2026gems} classified the facial structure, vertex counts $V$, surface areas, volumes, and discrete symmetry groups of Magic Gems across the $880$ Fr\'enicle order-$4$ squares and higher-order families.
  \item \textbf{Paper~II (\emph{Bivariate Moments \& Orthogonality})} \cite{mathewson2026moments} proved that semi-magic balance forces $\langle X^j, Z\rangle = \langle Y^j, Z\rangle = 0$ for all $j \ge 0$, diagonalizing the $3\times 3$ inertia and covariance tensors of every magic square and generating universal Prouhet--Tarry--Escott power-sum identities.
  \item \textbf{Paper~III (\emph{Rigid-Body Mechanics \& Structural Stability})} \cite{mathewson2026mechanics} tested these algebraic invariants in physical rigid-body mechanics, proving that every equal-vertex-mass Magic Gem is an oblate symmetric top ($I_{xx}=I_{yy} < I_{zz}$) free of intermediate-axis Dzhanibekov tumbling, while continuous bulk polyhedra break covariance isotropy yet preserve exact static stability across all $50{,}334$ facets of the $880$ order-$4$ gems.
\end{enumerate}

Despite this complete characterization of the \emph{ground states}, a central statistical-mechanical and algorithmic question remained open: \emph{how are the magic ground states embedded within the ambient $n^2!$-state permutation space $S_{n^2}$, and what physical or combinatorial dynamics can relax an arbitrary disordered permutation into a magic square?} Treating magic square synthesis as energy minimization on a frustrated discrete lattice connects classical combinatorics to spin-glass theory \cite{mezard1987spin}, rugged potential energy landscapes \cite{wales2003energy}, and stochastic annealing \cite{kirkpatrick1983optimization}. In an interactive companion laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver} \cite{mathewson2026physicalsolver}), we prototyped four complementary physical representations of this inverse problem: a spectral operator decomposition of line residuals, a multiscale sliding-window entropy analyzer, a discrete permutation-cycle descent engine, and a continuous ``Bubble Trinity'' Langevin particle simulator.

Following Whitesides's blueprint-driven methodology for connecting mathematical structure to quantitative experiments \cite{whitesides2004writing}, this fourth paper formalizes the mathematical operators underlying \texttt{physicalSolver} and establishes exact theorems and exhaustive computational censuses for each stage of the relaxation hierarchy. Let $\mathcal{L}_n$ denote the set of $2n+2$ magic lines ($n$ rows, $n$ columns, and $2$ main diagonals), and let $R_L(S) = \sum_{(i,j)\in L} Z_{ij}$ be the centred residual of line $L \in \mathcal{L}_n$. Collecting the $2n+2$ line indicator vectors as rows of the incidence matrix $A_n \in \{0,1\}^{(2n+2)\times n^2}$, the \emph{line-indicator Hamiltonian} takes the quadratic form
\begin{equation}
  \Hline(S) = \sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_n} R_L(S)^2 = \|A_n \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}, \qquad L_n = A_n^\top A_n \in \Z^{n^2 \times n^2}.
  \label{eq:hamiltonian_def}
\end{equation}
By construction, $\Hline(S) \ge 0$ for all $S \in S_{n^2}$, and $\Hline(S) = 0$ if and only if $S$ is a magic square.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Spectral Rationalization \& Low-Mode Insufficiency (\Cref{sec:spectral}):} In \Cref{thm:spectral_rationalization}, we prove that while the unprojected Laplacian $L_n$ on $\R^{n^2}$ has irrational eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm(n)$ caused by the coupling between the constant vector $\mathbf{1}$ and the two main diagonals, restricting $L_n$ to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ via $\Ltilde_n = P_{\mathbf{1}^\perp} L_n P_{\mathbf{1}^\perp}$ collapses all eigenvalues into exact integers: $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-2)^{[1]}, n^{[2n-1]}\}$ for even $n \ge 4$ and $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-1)^{[2]}, n^{[2n-2]}\}$ for odd $n \ge 3$. In \Cref{thm:ensemble_mean}, we show that $\tr(\Ltilde_n) = 2(n^2-1)$ for all $n \ge 3$, so the mean eigenvalue of $\Ltilde_n$ on $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ is \emph{identically $2$}, yielding the exact random-permutation expectation $\mathbb{E}[\Hline(S)] = 2\|\mathbf{z}\|^2 = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}a). In \Cref{prop:low_mode_insufficiency}, we prove that enforcing Paper~II's dipole orthogonality $\langle X,Z\rangle = \langle Y,Z\rangle = 0$ together with diagonal balance $R_{D_+} = R_{D_-} = 0$ constrains only a $4$-dimensional subspace of the $(2n+1)$-dimensional active image of $\Ltilde_n$, leaving a $(2n-3)$-dimensional high-frequency residual subspace exemplified at $n=4$ by an explicit permutation $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ with $\Hline(S_{\mathrm{low}}) = 1{,}584$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}b).

  \item \textbf{Multiscale Window Entropies, Quartet Equivalence \& Octupole Census (\Cref{sec:windows}):} Moving beyond $1\mathrm{D}$ line sums to $2\mathrm{D}$ local patches, we analyze sliding $k\times k$ sub-window energies and normalized Shannon entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ across constructive families ($n=3,4,5,6,8$) and all $880$ order-$4$ squares (\Cref{fig:window_entropy}). In \Cref{thm:quartet_equivalence}, we prove that for any $4\times 4$ magic square, vanishing of the $9$ interior $2\times 2$ quartet sums ($W_{2\times 2}^{\mathrm{int}}(S) = 0$) automatically forces all $7$ boundary-wrapping toroidal $2\times 2$ quartets to vanish ($W_{2\times 2}^{\mathrm{tor}}(S) = 0$), isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I. In \Cref{prop:octupole_census}, we show that the centrosymmetric complement-break count $B_{\mathrm{comp}}(S) \in \{0,8\}$ is binary across all $880$ squares and that the mixed octupole moment $M_{xyz}(S) = \sum_{i,j} X_j Y_i Z_{ij}$ vanishes on all $48$ Group~III associative squares plus exactly $8$ non-associative squares in Groups~IX and~X (\Cref{fig:octupole}).

  \item \textbf{Combinatorial Swap Landscape, Antipodal Traps \& Unique $(2,2)$ Escape (\Cref{sec:landscape}):} In \Cref{prop:swap_law}, we derive the exact closed-form energy change $\Delta\Hline(\tau_{ab}) = -2d(g_a-g_b) + c_{ab}d^2$ under any $2$-cycle transposition $\tau_{ab}$. Exhaustive evaluation across all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps} and \Cref{fig:s9_landscape}) reveals a striking topological obstruction: besides the $8$ Lo Shu ground states ($\Hline = 0$), $S_9$ contains \emph{exactly $8$ spurious strict local minima} forming a single $D_4$ orbit $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$ ($E_{\mathrm{std}}=2$). Every single $2$-cycle AND every single $3$-cycle on $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ strictly increases $\Hline$. However, testing all $\frac{1}{2}\binom{9}{2}\binom{7}{2} = 378$ disjoint $(2,2)$ double transpositions proves that \emph{one and only one}---the row-preserving double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$---escapes $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ directly to the Lo Shu ground state, expanding the deterministic ground-state basin of $S_9$ from $270{,}442 / 362{,}880$ ($74.53\%$) to $362{,}880 / 362{,}880$ ($100.00\%$). At order $n=4$ ($S_{16}$), line-preserving $(2,2)$ double swaps escape $16.57\%$ of trapped $2$-cycle local minima ($4.83\times$ more than $3$-cycles at $3.43\%$), raising deterministic steepest-descent convergence from $18.25\%$ to $25.45\%$ (\Cref{fig:s16_descent}).

  \item \textbf{Continuous ``Bubble Trinity'' Relaxation \& $(2,2)$-Polished Multi-Cycle Settling (\Cref{sec:bubble}):} Finally, we embed the discrete grid into $\R^2$ by treating the $n^2$ integers as mobile viscoelastic ``bubbles'' coupled to home lattice wells, pairwise core repulsion, and a continuous line-residual force field (\Cref{fig:bubble_trinity}). We prove why greedy nearest-cell snapping of continuous 2D drift fails to reach magic ground states on its own (displacement boundary collisions raise discrete snapped energy from $\sim 1{,}200$ to $\sim 3{,}700$), whereas combining stress-guided $2$-cycles, $3$-cycles, and line-preserving $(2,2)$ double swaps with periodic greedy polish achieves $100.0\%$ convergence on $n=3$ ($B=500$), $98.5\%$ at $B=1{,}500$ and $100.0\%$ at $B=2{,}500$ on $n=4$ (up from $24.5\%$ and $38.5\%$ for the baseline $2+3$-cycle solver), and $27.0\%$ on $n=5$ ($B=2{,}500$).
\end{enumerate}
