\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A magic square of order $n$ is a bijection $S : \{1,\dots,n\}^2 \to \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ whose $n$ rows, $n$ columns, and two main diagonals each sum to the magic constant $M_n = \frac{1}{2}n(n^2+1)$ \cite{frenicle1693,loubere1693,dudeney1917amusements,andrews1960magic}. When lifted into three-dimensional Euclidean space via centred integer coordinates
\begin{equation}
  X_j = 2j - (n+1), \qquad Y_i = (n+1) - 2i, \qquad Z_{ij} = 2s_{ij} - (n^2+1),
  \label{eq:centred_coords}
\end{equation}
each permutation $S \in S_{n^2}$ defines an $n^2$-point configuration $\mathcal{P}(S) = \{(X_j, Y_i, Z_{ij})\} \subset \Z^3$ whose convex hull $K(S) = \conv\mathcal{P}(S)$ is a \emph{Magic Gem}. Across the first three papers of this series, we investigated the forward map from magic squares to geometric and mechanical invariants. In Paper~I \cite{mathewson2026gems}, we classified the polyhedral facial structure, vertex counts $V$, surface areas, volumes, and discrete symmetry groups of Magic Gems across the $880$ Fr\'enicle order-$4$ squares and higher-order families. Building on that geometric census, Paper~II \cite{mathewson2026moments} proved that semi-magic row and column balance forces the bivariate polynomial moments $\langle X^j, Z\rangle = \langle Y^j, Z\rangle = 0$ to vanish for all $j \ge 0$, thereby diagonalizing the $3\times 3$ inertia and covariance tensors of every magic square and generating universal Prouhet--Tarry--Escott power-sum identities. In Paper~III \cite{mathewson2026mechanics}, we tested whether those algebraic symmetries survive in physical rigid-body mechanics, demonstrating that every equal-vertex-mass Magic Gem is an oblate symmetric top ($I_{xx}=I_{yy} < I_{zz}$) immune to intermediate-axis Dzhanibekov tumbling, whereas continuous bulk polyhedra break covariance isotropy yet preserve exact static stability across all $50{,}334$ facets of the $880$ order-$4$ gems.

Despite this characterization of the \emph{ground states}, an inverse landscape question remained open: \emph{how are the magic ground states organized within the ambient $n^2!$-state permutation space $S_{n^2}$, and what discrete or continuous relaxation mechanisms can navigate from an arbitrary disordered permutation to a magic square?} For the purely constructive task of producing a single magic square of order $n$, classical closed-form algorithms (such as de~La~Loub\`ere's Siamese rule for odd $n$, diagonal masks for doubly-even $n=4k$, and Conway's LUX method for singly-even $n=4k+2$) require only $O(n^2)$ operations \cite{loubere1693,andrews1960magic,berlekamp2004winning}, and specialized constraint-based local search (CBLS) heuristics \cite{codognet2001yet,vanhentenryck2005constraint} scale to $n \sim 100$ by targeting defective lines. Treating magic-square relaxation instead as an energy-landscape problem on $S_{n^2}$ connects classical recreational combinatorics to the theory of frustrated lattice systems \cite{mezard1987spin,pauling1935structure,bramwell2001spin}, potential energy landscapes \cite{wales1997global,wales2003energy}, and stochastic local search \cite{kirkpatrick1983optimization}. In an interactive companion laboratory (\texttt{physicalSolver} \cite{mathewson2026physicalsolver}), we prototyped four representations of this landscape: a spectral decomposition of line residuals, a multiscale sliding-window entropy analyzer, a discrete multi-exchange local search engine, and a continuous 2D particle visualization.

Following Whitesides's outline-driven methodology for linking mathematical structure to quantitative computational experiments \cite{whitesides2004writing}, this fourth paper formalizes the operators underlying \texttt{physicalSolver} and establishes exact algebraic identities and exhaustive computational censuses for each stage of the hierarchy. Let $\mathcal{L}_n$ denote the set of $2n+2$ magic lines ($n$ rows, $n$ columns, and $2$ main diagonals), and let $R_L(S) = \sum_{(i,j)\in L} Z_{ij}$ be the centred residual of line $L \in \mathcal{L}_n$. Collecting the $2n+2$ line indicator vectors as rows of the incidence matrix $A_n \in \{0,1\}^{(2n+2)\times n^2}$, the \emph{line-indicator Hamiltonian} takes the quadratic form
\begin{equation}
  \Hline(S) = \sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_n} R_L(S)^2 = \|A_n \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}, \qquad L_n = A_n^\top A_n \in \Z^{n^2 \times n^2}.
  \label{eq:hamiltonian_def}
\end{equation}
By construction, $\Hline(S) \ge 0$ for all $S \in S_{n^2}$, and $\Hline(S) = 0$ if and only if $S$ is a magic square.

We begin in \Cref{sec:spectral} by reviewing the spectral decomposition of $L_n = L_{\mathrm{rc}} + D$ via the Rook's graph $K_n \square K_n$ \cite{godsil2001algebraic,brouwer2012spectra,loly2009magic} and characterizing the insufficiency of low-order spatial moments. In \Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization}, we show that while the unprojected operator $L_n$ on $\R^{n^2}$ exhibits two irrational eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm(n)$ caused by the overlap between $\mathbf{1}$ and the two main diagonals, restricting $L_n$ to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ via $\Ltilde_n = P_{\mathbf{1}^\perp} L_n P_{\mathbf{1}^\perp}$ confines the rank-$2$ diagonal perturbation inside $\ker(L_{\mathrm{rc}})$ and yields purely integer eigenvalues: $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-2)^{[1]}, n^{[2n-1]}\}$ for even $n \ge 4$ and $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-1)^{[2]}, n^{[2n-2]}\}$ for odd $n \ge 3$. Both the trace identity $\tr(\Ltilde_n) = 2(n^2-1)$ and finite-population sampling without replacement immediately give a mean zero-mean eigenvalue $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$ and random-permutation expectation $\mathbb{E}[\Hline(S)] = 2\|\mathbf{z}\|^2 = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}a). Furthermore, \Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency} notes that enforcing Paper~II's linear dipole orthogonality $\langle X,Z\rangle = \langle Y,Z\rangle = 0$ together with diagonal balance $R_{D_+} = R_{D_-} = 0$ constrains only a $4$-dimensional subspace of the $2n$-dimensional active image $\operatorname{im}(\Ltilde_n) \subset \mathbf{1}^\perp$, leaving a $(2n-4)$-dimensional subspace of higher-degree polynomial row and column moments unconstrained, exemplified at $n=4$ by an explicit permutation $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ with $\Hline(S_{\mathrm{low}}) = 1{,}584$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}b).

To resolve spatial structure inside the magic ground-state manifold $\ker(\Ltilde_n)$ where all $1\mathrm{D}$ line residuals vanish, \Cref{sec:windows} evaluates sliding $k\times k$ sub-window energies and normalized Shannon entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ across constructive families ($n=3,4,5,6,8$) and all $880$ order-$4$ squares (\Cref{fig:window_entropy}). After recording in \Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} that vanishing of the $(2m-1)^2$ interior $2\times 2$ quartet sums on any even-order magic square ($n=2m$) forces all boundary-wrapping toroidal $2\times 2$ quartets to vanish (isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I at $n=4$), we establish the Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ in \Cref{prop:octupole_census}. This identity explains why the antipodal complement-break count $B_{\mathrm{comp}}(S) \in \{0,8\}$ is strictly binary across all $880$ squares and proves that the mixed octupole moment $M_{xyz}(S)$ vanishes on all $48$ Group~III associative squares together with exactly $8$ coprime-resonance squares in Groups~IX and~X (\Cref{fig:octupole}).

Turning from static invariants to discrete relaxation dynamics on $S_{n^2}$, \Cref{sec:landscape} applies the rank-$1$ transposition difference formula $\Delta\Hline(\tau_{ab}) = -2d(g_a-g_b) + c_{ab}d^2$ ($c_{ab}\in\{2,\dots,7\}$, \Cref{lem:swap_law}) across all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps} and \Cref{fig:s9_landscape}). We prove that besides the $8$ Lo Shu ground states ($\Hline = 0$), $S_9$ contains \emph{exactly $8$ spurious strict local minima} forming a single $D_4$ orbit $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$ ($E_{\mathrm{std}}=2$). On $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$, every single $2$-cycle ($\min\Delta\Hline = +4$) and every single $3$-cycle ($\min\Delta\Hline = +8$) strictly increases $\Hline$. However, testing all $\frac{1}{2}\binom{9}{2}\binom{7}{2} = 378$ disjoint $(2,2)$ double transpositions proves that \emph{one and only one}---the row-preserving double swap $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$, which traverses an intermediate single-swap saddle at $\Hline^\ddagger = 12$ ($\Delta\Hline^\ddagger = +4$)---escapes $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ directly to the Lo Shu ground state, expanding the deterministic ground-state basin of $S_9$ from $270{,}442 / 362{,}880$ ($74.53\%$) to $362{,}880 / 362{,}880$ ($100.00\%$). At order $n=4$ ($S_{16}$), line-preserving $(2,2)$ double swaps similarly escape $16.57\%$ of trapped $2$-cycle local minima ($4.83\times$ more than $3$-cycles at $3.43\%$), raising deterministic steepest-descent convergence from $18.25\%$ to $25.45\%$ (\Cref{fig:s16_descent}).

Finally, \Cref{sec:bubble} compares continuous 2D spatial kernel drift against discrete multi-exchange local search (\Cref{fig:bubble_trinity} and \Cref{tab:physical_analogs_ablation}). We first demonstrate that naive 2D Gaussian-kernel particle relaxation fails ($0.0\%$ magic squares under both greedy cell snapping and exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian bipartite matching) because the soft kernel surrogate $\Hind(t)$ lowers energy via convex amplitude averaging in the interior of the assignment polytope rather than crossing discrete permutation saddles. By contrast, our discrete \emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search (ME-ILS)} algorithm---combining $O(1)$ incremental $2$-exchange steepest descent, whole-row/column permutations, row/column-preserving $(2,2)$ plaquette moves, candidate-list $(2,2)$ swaps, and threshold-bounded basin-hopping restarts---achieves $100.0\%$ ground-state synthesis across all orders $n = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10$ (\Cref{tab:multi_order_settler}, up to $100! \approx 9.33\times 10^{157}$ states) and shows that mean restarts peak at $n=5$ ($8.03$) before declining across $n=6\dots 10$ ($3.34$ at $n=10$) as the diagonal-free $(2,2)$ subspace fraction $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ approaches unity (\Cref{fig:dimensional_relief}).
