\section{Introduction: From Static Magic Gems to Interactive Physical Relaxation}
\label{sec:intro}

For more than two millennia---from the $3\times 3$ Lo Shu turtle diagram of ancient China to Albrecht D\"urer's 1514 engraving \emph{Melencolia~I} and Bernard Fr\'enicle de~Bessy's 1693 enumeration of all $880$ order-$4$ squares---magic squares have fascinated mathematicians, artists, and physicists alike \cite{frenicle1693,loubere1693,dudeney1917amusements,andrews1960magic}. A magic square of order $n$ is a bijection $S : \{1,\dots,n\}^2 \to \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ arranging the consecutive integers $1\dots n^2$ in an $n\times n$ grid such that every row, every column, and both main diagonals sum to the magic constant $M_n = \frac{1}{2}n(n^2+1)$. When we lift each grid cell into three-dimensional space using centred integer coordinates,
\begin{equation}
  X_j = 2j - (n+1), \qquad Y_i = (n+1) - 2i, \qquad Z_{ij} = 2s_{ij} - (n^2+1),
  \label{eq:centred_coords}
\end{equation}
where $s_{ij} \in \{1,\dots,n^2\}$ denotes the integer in row $i$ and column $j$, each arrangement $S \in S_{n^2}$ becomes a three-dimensional cloud of $n^2$ points $\mathcal{P}(S) = \{(X_j, Y_i, Z_{ij})\} \subset \Z^3$ whose convex hull $K(S) = \conv\mathcal{P}(S)$ forms a polyhedral sculpture that we call a \emph{Magic Gem}. Across the first three papers of this series, we investigated the geometry and mechanics of these sculptures once they are already constructed. In Paper~I \cite{mathewson2026gems}, we classified the polyhedral faces, vertex counts, surface areas, volumes, and dihedral symmetry groups of Magic Gems across all $880$ order-$4$ squares and higher-order families. In Paper~II \cite{mathewson2026moments}, we proved that equal row and column sums force the bivariate polynomial moments $\langle X^j, Z\rangle = \langle Y^j, Z\rangle = 0$ to vanish for all $j \ge 0$, which automatically diagonalizes the $3\times 3$ moment-of-inertia tensor of every magic square \cite{loly2004invariance,rogers2004inertia}. In Paper~III \cite{mathewson2026mechanics}, we subjected those algebraic identities to physical testing, demonstrating that every equal-vertex-mass Magic Gem spins in three dimensions as an oblate symmetric top ($I_{xx}=I_{yy} < I_{zz}$) immune to intermediate-axis Dzhanibekov tumbling, while solid three-dimensionally printed polyhedra rest in static equilibrium on every single one of their $50{,}334$ triangular facets.

Those three studies, however, only examined completed magic squares. When demonstrating the interactive Magic Gem viewer to students and colleagues, a natural dynamical question arose repeatedly: instead of entering a known magic square from a textbook, what happens if we initialize a randomly scrambled grid of numbers $1\dots n^2$, attach virtual physical forces that pull every row, column, and diagonal toward the magic sum $M_n$, and watch the system attempt to settle into a magic square on its own? To separate deterministic template construction from landscape relaxation, we note that if our sole objective were to write down a single magic square of order $n$, no physical simulation would be needed: classical closed-form algorithms---such as de~La~Loub\`ere's diagonal staircase (Siamese) rule for odd $n$, complementary X-masks for doubly-even $n=4k$, and Conway's LUX method for singly-even $n=4k+2$---assemble a rigid template in $O(n^2)$ operations \cite{loubere1693,andrews1960magic,berlekamp2004winning}. However, such deterministic rules populate only an exponentially tiny, structured submanifold of the full magic-square ensemble. Conversely, while general-purpose metaheuristics such as Simulated Annealing \cite{kirkpatrick1983optimization}, Tabu Search \cite{glover1989tabu}, Variable Neighborhood Search \cite{mladenovic1997variable}, Iterated Local Search \cite{lourenco2003iterated}, and defective-line Constraint-Based Local Search (CBLS) \cite{codognet2001yet,vanhentenryck2005constraint} attempt to synthesize magic squares from random permutations, we show below that standard defective-line CBLS and thermal Markov chains systematically stall in endgame local minima as $n$ grows. Our central question is therefore simultaneously geometric, physical, and algorithmic \cite{mezard1987spin,wales2003energy,diaconis1988group}: what is the exact structure of the magic-square energy landscape across the $n^2!$ permutations of $S_{n^2}$, why do intuitive continuous and single-swap relaxation schemes get trapped in deceptive non-magic states, and how can exact spectral and bilinear operator identities unlock $100\%$ ground-state convergence from $n = 3$ all the way to $n = 100$ ($10{,}000$ cells)?

To investigate this landscape both visually and quantitatively, we built an open-source browser laboratory, \texttt{physicalSolver} \cite{mathewson2026physicalsolver}, comprising four interactive web applications: (1) an \emph{Energy Decomposition Explorer} (\texttt{energy-decomposition.html}) that decomposes any board's error into three-dimensional tilt, line residuals, sliding $k\times k$ sub-window entropies, and complement-pair symmetry breaks; (2) a \emph{Discrete Physics Lab} (\texttt{physics-lab.html}) and (3) a \emph{Vector-Physics Explorer} (\texttt{vector-physics.html}) that render cell-by-cell stress vectors and test $2$-exchange and $3$-cycle descent; and (4) a continuous particle sandbox called the \emph{Bubble Trinity} (\texttt{bubble-trinity.html}), in which the $n^2$ numbers float in a two-dimensional dish as buoyant, size-modulated bubbles driven by line-sum imbalances, core repulsion, and home-cell wells. Experimenting with these interactive tools and scaling their underlying algorithms up to $n = 100$ exposed five striking phenomena: horizontally balanced plates with magic diagonals that remain far from magic; a stubborn $3\times 3$ combination lock that captures a quarter of all random shuffles; continuous floating bubbles that lower their field energy by $37.5\%$ on screen yet fail completely when snapped to the grid; a frustration peak at $n = 5$ followed by an order-invariant nullspace-relief plateau across $n = 6\dots 50$; and an endgame trap barrier at large $n$ where defective-line CBLS fails because it ignores zero-residual relay swaps on already balanced lines.

Following Whitesides's blueprint-driven methodology for uniting interactive experiments with exact mathematical theory \cite{whitesides2004writing}, this paper establishes the complete algebraic and computational framework governing these five phenomena. Let $\mathcal{L}_n$ denote the set of $2n+2$ magic lines ($n$ rows, $n$ columns, and $2$ main diagonals), and let $R_L(S) = \sum_{(i,j)\in L} Z_{ij} = 2\bigl(\sum_{(i,j)\in L} s_{ij} - M_n\bigr)$ denote the centred residual of line $L \in \mathcal{L}_n$. Stacking the $2n+2$ line indicators as rows of the incidence matrix $A_n \in \{0,1\}^{(2n+2)\times n^2}$, we define the \emph{line-indicator Hamiltonian} as the sum of squared line residuals:
\begin{equation}
  \Hline(S) = \sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_n} R_L(S)^2 = \|A_n \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 = \mathbf{z}^\top L_n \mathbf{z}, \qquad L_n = A_n^\top A_n \in \Z^{n^2 \times n^2},
  \label{eq:hamiltonian_def}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z} \in \Z^{n^2}$ is the flattened vector of centred heights $Z_{ij}$. Because every centred residual $R_L(S)$ equals twice the standard line error $\sum_{(i,j)\in L} s_{ij} - M_n$, $\Hline(S)$ is exactly four times the standard sum of squared line errors ($E_{\mathrm{std}} = \frac{1}{4}\Hline$), and $\Hline(S) = 0$ if and only if $S$ is a magic square.

We begin in \Cref{sec:spectral} with the puzzle that opens the \emph{Energy Decomposition Explorer} (\texttt{energy-decomposition.html}): if we rearrange a grid until its three-dimensional center-of-mass tilt vanishes ($\langle X,Z\rangle = \langle Y,Z\rangle = 0$) and both main diagonals sum to $M_n$ ($R_{D_+} = R_{D_-} = 0$), have we produced a magic square? We exhibit an explicit $4\times 4$ counterexample $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}b) that balances horizontally with zero diagonal error yet carries wildly unequal row and column sums ($\Hline = 1{,}584$). Using the Rook's-graph-plus-diagonals decomposition of $L_n = A_n^\top A_n$ \cite{godsil2001algebraic,brouwer2012spectra,loly2009magic}, we prove in \Cref{prop:spectral_rationalization} that restricting $L_n$ to the zero-mean hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ inhabited by all centred permutations collapses its irrational eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm(n)$ into exact integers---$\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-2)^{[1]}, n^{[2n-1]}\}$ for even $n$ and $\{0^{[n^2-2n-1]}, (n-1)^{[2]}, n^{[2n-2]}\}$ for odd $n$---with a universal average eigenvalue $\bar{\mu}(\Ltilde_n) = 2$ and random-shuffle expectation $\mathbb{E}[\Hline(S)] = 2\|\mathbf{z}\|^2 = \frac{2}{3}n^2(n^4-1)$ (\Cref{fig:spectral}a). This spectrum immediately reveals why $S_{\mathrm{low}}$ exists (\Cref{rem:low_mode_insufficiency}): linear tilt and diagonal sums constrain only $4$ directions out of the $2n$-dimensional active subspace on $\mathbf{1}^\perp$, leaving $2n-4$ alternating row and column modes invisible to rigid-body balance.

Next, in \Cref{sec:windows}, we use the second view of \texttt{energy-decomposition.html} to look inside the magic ground-state manifold $\Hline(S) = 0$, where one-dimensional line sums can no longer distinguish one magic square from another. Evaluating sliding $k\times k$ sub-window energies and Shannon entropies $\widetilde{H}_p(k)$ across classical constructive families ($n=3,4,5,6,8$) and all $880$ order-$4$ squares (\Cref{fig:window_entropy}), we prove in \Cref{lem:quartet_equivalence} that on any even-order magic square ($n=2m$), balancing the $(2m-1)^2$ interior $2\times 2$ quartets automatically balances all boundary-wrapping toroidal quartets, isolating the $48$ panmagic squares of Dudeney Group~I at $n=4$. We then establish the Concentric Shell Quadrupole Identity $M_{xyz}(S) = -8\,Q_{\mathrm{corner}}(S) - 3\,Q_{\mathrm{edge}}(S)$ (\Cref{prop:octupole_census}), which explains both why the complement-break count $B_{\mathrm{comp}}(S) \in \{0,8\}$ is all-or-nothing at order $4$ and why the three-dimensional twist moment $M_{xyz}(S)$ vanishes on the $48$ associative squares of Group~III plus exactly $8$ non-associative squares in Groups~IX and~X where the corner and edge shells cancel in a coprime $(-3k, 8k)$ resonance (\Cref{fig:octupole}).

In \Cref{sec:landscape}, we turn to the \emph{Discrete Physics Lab} (\texttt{physics-lab.html}) and \emph{Vector-Physics Explorer} (\texttt{vector-physics.html}), where a scrambled board relaxes by swapping pairs of cells that lower $\Hline(S)$ according to the exact quadratic difference formula $\Delta\Hline(\tau_{ab}) = -2d(g_a-g_b) + c_{ab}d^2$ (\Cref{lem:swap_law}). By exhaustively mapping all $9! = 362{,}880$ permutations of $S_9$ (\Cref{thm:s9_traps} and \Cref{fig:s9_landscape}), we prove that $74.53\%$ of all $3\times 3$ starting grids descend directly to the Lo Shu magic square, whereas the remaining $25.47\%$ ($92{,}438$ states) freeze in a single dihedral trap orbit $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ at $\Hline = 8$. Acting like a mechanical combination lock, $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ resists every single $2$-exchange transposition ($\min\Delta\Hline = +4$) and every directed $3$-cycle rotation ($\min\Delta\Hline = +8$). Yet among all $378$ disjoint $(2,2)$ double transpositions on the $3\times 3$ grid, one and only one---the simultaneous intra-row exchange $(6\leftrightarrow 7)(3\leftrightarrow 4)$, analogous to a $4$-vertex Kempe chain in graph coloring \cite{kempe1879geographical}---unlocks $S_{\mathrm{trap}}^{(3)}$ directly to $\Hline=0$, restoring the deterministic basin of $S_9$ to $100.00\%$.

In \Cref{sec:bubble}, we analyze the \emph{Bubble Trinity} laboratory (\texttt{bubble-trinity.html}), contrasting continuous two-dimensional floating-bubble dynamics against our discrete \emph{Multi-Exchange Iterated Local Search (ME-ILS)} hierarchy (\Cref{fig:bubble_trinity} and \Cref{tab:physical_analogs_ablation}). Watching the continuous bubbles drift across the four interactive presets reveals why soft Gaussian relaxation fails to solve discrete permutation problems: the kernel-induced field energy $\Hind(t)$ drops by $37.5\%$ on screen, yet projecting the bubbles onto the grid---whether via greedy nearest-cell assignment or exact $O(N^3)$ Hungarian minimum-weight bipartite matching \cite{kuhn1955hungarian,munkres1957algorithms}---recovers $0.0\%$ magic squares, because soft particles park on cell boundaries to average out positive and negative heights rather than crossing discrete transposition saddles. Switching to the discrete ME-ILS operator hierarchy---which unites $2$-exchanges, whole-row/column glides, Kempe-like $(2,2)$ plaquette loops, and threshold-leashed basin restarts \cite{wales1997global,mladenovic1997variable,lourenco2003iterated}---achieves $100.0\%$ synthesis across $n=3\dots 10$ (\Cref{tab:multi_order_settler}) and shows that $5\times 5$ represents the sharpest frustration bottleneck ($8.03$ restarts) before higher orders ($n=6\dots 10$, dropping to $3.34$ restarts at $n=10$) gain dimensional relief as $f_{\mathrm{null}}(n) = 1 - 2/(n-1)^2$ of the $(2,2)$ plaquette subspace becomes orthogonal to both main diagonals (\Cref{fig:dimensional_relief}).

Finally, in \Cref{sec:large_scale}, we push this discrete multi-exchange architecture across two orders of magnitude in grid size to answer two fundamental scaling questions: does nullspace dimensional relief persist beyond $n = 10$, and can we accelerate exact multi-exchange search to synthesize $100\times 100$ magic squares ($N = 10{,}000$ cells) from uniform random starts? First, by scanning every consecutive order from $n = 3$ to $n = 50$ ($N = 48$ orders, $100.0\%$ ground-state convergence at every order, \Cref{fig:scan_3_to_50}a), we show that basin-hopping restarts remain bounded on an order-invariant plateau across $n = 6\dots 50$ (median $4.32$ restarts, $\text{IQR} = 2.67\text{--}8.67$, mean $6.15 \pm 4.49$; Mann--Whitney $U = 191.5$, $p = 0.639$ between even and odd orders), while the per-restart descent trajectory length grows strictly linearly with order size as $3.52\,n + 20.86$ ($r = 0.9934$, $p = 6.39\times 10^{-45}$). Second, to overcome the $O(n^{5.92})$ wall-clock growth of brute-force all-pairs evaluation, we prove three zero-approximation algebraic accelerations (\Cref{thm:bilinear_plaquette}): an \emph{Active-Set Steepest-Descent Theorem}, the \emph{Bilinear Plaquette Identity} $\Delta\Hline(p_1, p_2) = \Delta\Hline(p_1) + \Delta\Hline(p_2) + 2\,\boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_1)^\top \boldsymbol{\delta R}(p_2)$, and an \emph{Exact $O(n^2)$ Consecutive-Value Candidate Bound}. By reducing $(2,2)$ plaquette evaluation to a single $(K \times (n+2))$ BLAS matrix multiplication, this bilinear solver achieves empirical $O(n^{2.93})$ per-restart time complexity ($r = 0.9924$, $p = 1.08\times 10^{-6}$, \Cref{fig:scan_3_to_50}b) and wins a fair head-to-head competition at $n = 46$, $n = 48$, and $n = 100$ by $34.7\times\text{--}50.2\times$ (\Cref{fig:competition_100} and \Cref{tab:head_to_head_competition}), synthesizing certified $100\times 100$ magic squares in a mean of $147.70~\text{s}$ (minimum $34.06~\text{s}$) on identical random seeds where Simulated Annealing ($0/9$ solved) and defective-line CBLS ($0/3$ at $n=46$ and $0/3$ at $n=100$) fail in endgame local minima.
