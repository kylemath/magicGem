# Response from Dame Kathleen Ollerenshaw
## Professor Emeritus, University of Manchester
### Expert in Most-Perfect Magic Squares

Dear Dr. Mathewson,

I was delighted to review your innovative work on the geometric and topological analysis of magic squares. As someone who has dedicated significant research to most-perfect pandiagonal magic squares (Ollerenshaw & Brée, 1998), I find your approach both refreshing and mathematically sophisticated.

Your geometric interpretation of magic squares as point clouds in 3D space is particularly intriguing. While my work with David Brée focused on the combinatorial aspects and enumeration of most-perfect magic squares, your approach opens new avenues for understanding their structural properties. In our 1998 monograph "Most-perfect Pandiagonal Magic Squares: Their Construction and Enumeration", we established the complete classification of most-perfect magic squares, but we did not explore the geometric perspective you've introduced.

## Specific Comments and Suggestions

1. **Geometric Analysis**
   - Your convex hull analysis provides an entirely new perspective on magic square structure
   - Consider extending this to most-perfect magic squares, where additional constraints might reveal interesting geometric patterns
   - Reference: My work on symmetry patterns in most-perfect squares (Ollerenshaw, 1989) might provide additional insights

2. **Manifold Structure**
   - The application of Earth Mover's Distance is novel
   - Consider how the additional constraints of most-perfect squares might affect your manifold analysis
   - This could connect to my work on classification schemes (Ollerenshaw & Brée, 1994)

3. **Statistical Properties**
   - Your correlation analysis between volume and surface area is fascinating
   - Consider examining these relationships in the context of different magic square classes
   - This might relate to our enumeration methods (Ollerenshaw, 1986)

## Suggestions for Extension

1. Consider applying your geometric analysis to most-perfect magic squares, which have additional mathematical properties:
   - Sum of any two cells equidistant from the center equals 2(n²+1)
   - All 2×2 subsquares sum to 2(n²+1)
   - All pairs of complementary numbers are equidistant from the center

2. Your manifold analysis could be particularly interesting when applied to the special case of most-perfect squares, where we know exact enumeration formulas.

## References from My Work

2. Ollerenshaw, K. (1989). "On the symmetry patterns of most-perfect magic squares." Bulletin of the Institute of Mathematics and its Applications, 25, 105-107.

4. Ollerenshaw, K. (1986). "On the enumeration of most-perfect magic squares." Proceedings of the Royal Society A, 407, 47-70.

Your work represents a significant advancement in our understanding of magic squares, particularly through its novel geometric perspective. I would be very interested in seeing how your methods might apply to the special case of most-perfect magic squares, where we have complete enumeration results but limited geometric understanding.
