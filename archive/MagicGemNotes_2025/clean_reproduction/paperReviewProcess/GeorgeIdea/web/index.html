What is a Spin Gem?

A Spin Gem is a geometric representation of a molecular spin configuration. Given a collection of atoms at positions (x, y, z) with spin projections Sz, we construct a four-dimensional point cloud and analyze its covariance structure.

Point cloud: 𝒮 = { (x̃ₖ, ỹₖ, z̃ₖ, s̃ₖ) } ⊂ ℝ⁴

The key insight is that the covariance between spatial coordinates and spin values captures a fundamental notion of balance. When these covariances vanish:

Cov(X, S) = Cov(Y, S) = Cov(Z, S) = 0

we say the configuration is magic—the spatial position of an atom provides no linear information about its spin state.

The Spin Balance Energy

We define the energy functional E = ||c||² = Cov(X,S)² + Cov(Y,S)² + Cov(Z,S)². Magic spin configurations are precisely the ground states where E = 0.

The Connection Theorem

Interpreting magic square values as spin projections on a planar lattice, we prove that the zero-covariance condition is equivalent to the magic property.

Theorem (Magic Squares ↔ Spin Gems)

An n×n arrangement S of {1, 2, ..., n²} is a magic square if and only if the corresponding Spin Gem has E = 0.

Exploring the Energy Landscape

For N atoms with spin assignments {s₁, ..., sₙ}, the spin balance energy measures how far the configuration is from the "magic" subspace where position and spin are uncorrelated.

Key Finding

For symmetric geometries with N ≥ 4 atoms, magic spin configurations exist and form a linear subspace of dimension N - 4. Random configurations almost never land in this subspace.

The Energy Landscape

Visualizing the covariance energy surface—magic configurations sit at the bottom of a deep well, isolated from all other arrangements.

Ground States and Isolation

The 3D surface shows the spin balance energy E as a function of two spin parameters for a tetrahedron. The other two spins are constrained to maintain zero mean: s₂ = -s₀, s₃ = -s₁.

Observation: Isolated Minima

Magic spin configurations are isolated local minima of the energy functional. Any perturbation immediately increases E, with the energy growing approximately quadratically with distance from the magic point.

✓ Verified computationally

The magenta marker at the origin indicates the magic configuration where E = 0. The surrounding bowl-shaped surface shows how energy increases as we deviate from perfect spin-position balance.

Physical Interpretation

If we interpret E as a physical energy, magic configurations are "ground states" of an imbalance Hamiltonian. Just as molecules minimize their potential energy, spin configurations "settle" into magic arrangements when geometric balance is the driving principle.

Scaling Behavior

As the number of atoms N increases:

  • The magic subspace dimension grows (N - 4 degrees of freedom)
  • Mean energy of random configurations increases
  • Probability of randomly finding a magic configuration decreases

Classical Precession

In an external magnetic field B pointing along the central axis (z), all spins precess around this same axis according to:

ds/dt = γ s × B

Each spin traces a cone around the B-field direction with angular frequency ω = γ|B|. The z-component (parallel to B) remains constant, while the perpendicular components rotate in the x-y plane. The gold arrow shows the central B-field; the magenta arrow shows the total magnetization vector.

Key Question

How does the collective covariance energy E(t) evolve as individual spins precess? Do magic configurations maintain their special properties under dynamics?

The chart below tracks E(t) in real time as the simulation runs. Observe how the energy oscillates for non-magic configurations but remains near zero for balanced initial conditions.
