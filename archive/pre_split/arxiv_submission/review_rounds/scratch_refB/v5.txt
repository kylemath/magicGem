                                                   Abstract

           We study Magic Gems : the convex hull of the points obtained by placing the entries of an n × n
      magic square as heights over a centred grid. The representation is D4 -invariant, so each essentially
      dierent square has one gem. Reading the centred entries as a height function z , the magic conditions
      are exactly the orthogonality of z to a 2n-dimensional space of centred line indicators; we treat this
      as a change of viewpoint, not a theorem, and use it to obtain: (i) a moment hierarchy: for real height
      functions, Cov(X m , Z) = Cov(Y m , Z) = 0 for m ≤ k is equivalent to equal row and column sums
      exactly when k ≥ n − 1; Loly's moment-of-inertia invariance is the degree-two case; (ii) exact mean
      and variance of two energies on a uniformly random arrangement, with a Hoeding-type limit law;
      (iii) an exact single-swap formula, giving strict local minimality and gaps d2 m/n4 ; (iv) the exact
      count 14,639,440 of 4 × 4 arrangements satisfying the four low-mode conditions; (v) a classication of
      the 880 order-four gems (39 f -vectors, 352 combinatorial types, 616 congruence classes) and a proof
      that Siamese gems have twelve vertices.
      Keywords:    magic squares, convex polyhedra, permutation statistics, moment of inertia, combina-
      torial geometry


1     Introduction
Magic squares are among the oldest objects of recreational mathematics:             the 3 × 3 Lo Shu appears
in Chinese sources more than two millennia old, a 4 × 4 square is engraved in Dürer's Melencolia I
(1514) [8], and systematic constructions for every order have been known since the seventeenth century
[2, 18]. The modern literature treats them from several directions: enumeration and the geometry of the
associated polytopes [20, 9, 1, 4, 3], spectral and matrix properties [15, 16, 17], and, in work of Loly and
collaborators, physical invariants such as the moment of inertia [13, 19, 5].

What is and is not new.                 The polyhedral geometry of magic squares is classical: semi-magic squares
with line sum t are the lattice points of the t-th dilate of the Birkho polytope, their counting function
is an Ehrhart quasi-polynomial [20, 9], the cones of magic squares and cubes have been described by
Hilbert bases [1], and the 4 × 4 magic squares were enumerated by inside-out polytopes [4, 3].                              The
Magic Gem is a dierent object: not the polytope whose lattice points are the squares, but a polyhedron
attached to one square. The orthogonality reading of the constraints is elementary and implicit in Loly's
work; what is new here is the hierarchy theorem, which identies the degree at which polynomial test
functions become equivalent to line sums, the exact random-arrangement moments, the swap formula,
the exact low-mode count, and the classication of the 880 gems. We make no claim that the energy
reformulation by itself is a result.

2      Background
2.1 Enumeration and symmetry
There are 8 magic squares of order 3, all images of the Lo Shu under the dihedral group D4 of the square
(four rotations, four reections); there are 7,040 of order 4, forming 880 D4 -orbits rst listed by Frénicle
de Bessy in 1693 and veried many times since [3]; and 275,305,224 of order 5 (Schroeppel, 1973), i.e.
34,413,153 orbits. For n ≥ 6 only statistical estimates are known; see Trump's compilation [21]. Since
D4 maps lines to lines and preserves the entry set, it maps magic squares to magic squares, and two
squares in the same orbit are called essentially dierent from squares in other orbits. The 880 order-four
squares were further sorted by Dudeney into twelve groups according to the pattern of complementary
pairs (a, 17 − a) [7]; we use these groups only as a classical reference partition in Section 5.5. Classical
constructionsthe Siamese (de la Loubère) method for odd n, the diagonal-complement method for
n ≡ 0 (mod 4), and the LUX or Strachey methods for n ≡ 2 (mod 4)show that magic squares exist
for all n ≥ 3 [2]; we use the Siamese squares as a family on which hull computations can be pushed to
large n.

2.4 Polytopes of magic squares
The polytope literature on magic squares concerns a dierent polytope from the one studied here. An
n × n nonnegative integer matrix with all row and column sums equal to t is a lattice point of the t-th
dilate of the Birkho polytope of doubly stochastic matrices; their number is a polynomial in t of degree
(n−1)2 (Stanley [20], Ehrhart [9]; see Ziegler [23] for the polytope background). Adding the two diagonal
conditions and the requirement of distinct entries leads to the inside-out polytopes of Beck and Zaslavsky
[4], which gave the rst enumeration of 4 × 4 magic squares of arbitrary magic sum [3]; Ahmed, De Loera
and Hemmecke described the cones of magic squares and cubes by Hilbert bases [1]. Contingency tables



                                                            3
with xed margins, i.e. the same lattice points studied as a sample space, are surveyed by Diaconis and
Gangolli [6]. In all of these the magic squares are the points of a polytope. The Magic Gem attaches a
polyhedron to a single square; the two constructions share nothing beyond the word. Spectral properties
of the matrix of a magic square [15, 16, 17] and Loly's physical invariants [13, 19, 14, 5] are closer to our
viewpoint: they, too, attach a quantity to one square and ask what it reveals.

Remark 4.9 (What the projection lemma does and does not say). Lemma 4.8(1) is the denition of a
magic square rewritten in the language of orthogonality: all line sums equal M (n) is literally  z ⊥ 1̃L
for every line L. It is not a theorem about magic squares, and we do not present it as one. Its use is
as a change of viewpoint. Once the constraints are read as orthogonality of the height function to a 2n-
dimensional test space W , three things become available that are not visible in the line-sum formulation:
(i) W can be truncated or enlarged in natural ways (polynomial test functions, Section 4.3), which
produces a hierarchy of relaxations with Loly's inertia invariance as the degree-two member; (ii) for a
uniformly random arrangement the quantities ⟨φ, z⟩ are permutation statistics in the sense of Hoeding,
whose exact moments and limiting law are computable (Section 5.2); (iii) a single swap changes z by
a multiple of a dierence of two cell indicators, and (8) turns the change of Efull into a two-line count
(Section 4.4).

the facet-adjacency graph of a 3-polytope is connected these are all the facets, so P      = Q. With (a),
P ⊆ Φ(G(S)) ⊆ Q = P , so Φ(G(S)) = P , whose vertex set is {v1 , . . . , v12 } by (d). Mapping back by Φ−1
gives the statement, and the facet counts are read o from (b)(c).


Remark 5.9. For m = 1 (n = 3) the triangles T , U , D , B degenerate to single points, v1 = v2 = v3 and
v10 = v11 = v12 , and the same argument gives the 8-vertex Lo Shu gem. The proof shows more than the
count: up to an ane map, every Siamese gem is the convex hull of ve parallel lattice polygons whose
shapes do not depend on n except through scale, which is why the combinatorial type is constant for all

have bounded hull-vertex counts is open.

6.1 What the orthogonality viewpoint buys
Reading the line-sum conditions as z ⊥ W changes nothing about which arrangements are magic, but

magic for every n ≥ 4, while Loly's invariance of the moment of inertia [13] is the degree-two member

6.2 What it does not buy
The projection lemma is a restatement of the denition (Remark 4.9); it proves nothing about magic
squares that the line sums do not already say, and we have not used it to count anything. The count-
ing in this paper is done by the meet-in-the-middle enumeration of Proposition 5.5 and by exact hull

computations, neither of which needs the orthogonality language. The low-mode energy is a poor proxy
for the magic property: for n = 4 its zero set is 2080 times larger than the set of magic squares, and
                                                                                                 9                   10
even its two-condition sub-classes (rst moments only, diagonals only) have 6.7 × 10                 and 5.0 × 10

inertia statement is Loly's planar one.

6.4 Open questions
  1. Size of the low-mode class.       |L3 | = 8, |L4 | = 14,639,440, and the Gaussian local-limit estimate
        N !(2π)−2 det(Σ)−1/2 is within 20% at n = 4 with ratio apparently increasing in n (Section 5.4). Is
        |Ln |/N ! ∼ c n−13 with an explicit constant, i.e. does a local limit theorem hold for the four lattice
        statistics (T1 , . . . , T4 )?

  2. The permutation threshold.        k ∗ (n) = n − 1 for 3 ≤ n ≤ 13 and k ∗ (n) ≤ n − 2 for n ≥ 14

        (Proposition 4.14). What is k (n) for large n? The proof compares the entries of the integer

                 ∗

  3. Combinatorial types for n = 5. The 880 order-four gems have 352 types; the 34,413,153 order-ve
        classes are within reach of the exact hull algorithm, and the number of types, the modal f -vector

    4. Bounded hull-vertex counts.   Siamese gems have twelve vertices for every odd      n ≥ 5 because,
      anely, they are hulls of ve lattice polygons whose shapes do not depend on n (Proposition 5.8).
      Do the LUX or Strachey constructions for n ≡ 2 (mod 4), or the diagonal-complement construction

      families have not been examined.



7     Conclusion
The Magic Gem is the convex hull of a magic square's height function over the centred grid, and it is the
same polyhedron for all eight images of the square under D4 . Reading the entries as a height function
makes the magic conditions the orthogonality of that function to a 2n-dimensional space of test functions.
We have treated this reading as a viewpoint, not a result, and have used it to obtain a moment hierarchy
that contains Loly's inertia invariance as its degree-two case and identies k = n−1 as the degree at which
polynomial test functions become equivalent to line sums; exact means, variances and a limit law for the
                                                                                         2      4
energies of a random arrangement; an exact single-swap formula with the gap spectrum d m/n ; the exact
count 14,639,440 of 4×4 arrangements satisfying the four low-mode conditions; and a classication of the
880 order-four gems into 39 f -vectors, 352 combinatorial types and 616 congruence classes, together with
a proof that Siamese gems have twelve vertices for every odd order. Several statements of the previous
versionthe trimodal gap structure, the landscape entropy, the sampled landscape statistics and the
local-minimum countwere artifacts of an asymmetric energy and are withdrawn. What remains is a
small set of exact statements about a classical object, and a polyhedron that is pleasant to look at.

incidence graphs, grouped by a WeisfeilerLehman hash and conrmed by explicit isomorphism tests.

distances (grouping by the sorted distance multiset, then an edge-labelled graph isomorphism), with a
