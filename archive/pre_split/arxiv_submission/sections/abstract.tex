\begin{abstract}
We study \emph{Magic Gems}: the convex hull of the points obtained by placing the entries of an $n\times n$ magic square as heights over a centred grid.
The representation is $D_4$-invariant, so each essentially different square has one gem.
Reading the centred entries as a height function $z$, the magic conditions are exactly the orthogonality of $z$ to a $2n$-dimensional space of centred line indicators; we treat this as a change of viewpoint, not a theorem, and use it to obtain:
(i)~a moment hierarchy. For real height functions, $\Cov(X^m,Z)=\Cov(Y^m,Z)=0$ for $m\le k$ is equivalent to equal row and column sums exactly when $k\ge n-1$ (a Vandermonde fact). For permutations of $1,\dots,n^2$, integrality lowers the threshold to at most $n-2$ for every $n\ge14$. Loly's moment-of-inertia invariance is a consequence of the degree-two level of the hierarchy, and the moment of inertia of a uniformly random arrangement has mean equal to Loly's invariant value, with an explicit variance;
(ii)~exact mean and variance of two energies on a uniformly random arrangement, with a limit law by Hoeffding's combinatorial central limit theorem;
(iii)~an exact single-swap formula, giving strict local minimality and gaps $d^2m/n^4$;
(iv)~the exact count $14{,}639{,}440$ of $4\times4$ arrangements satisfying the four low-mode conditions;
(v)~an exact computation of the geometry of the $880$ order-four gems ($39$ $f$-vectors, $352$ combinatorial types, $616$ congruence classes, which are exactly the orbits of the complement map $S\mapsto17-S$) and a computer-assisted proof that Siamese gems have twelve vertices.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} magic squares, convex polyhedra, permutation statistics, moment of inertia, combinatorial geometry
\end{abstract}
